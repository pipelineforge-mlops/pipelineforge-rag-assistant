Background

Patients are increasingly turning to online resources and artificial intelligence (AI)-based tools to obtain information about orthopedic conditions and surgical options. Large language models, such as ChatGPT, are becoming prominent in patient education; however, their reliability and readability remain uncertain. This study evaluated the quality and readability of responses generated by ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 to frequently asked patient questions regarding hallux rigidus fusion surgery.

Methods

Twenty commonly asked patient questions were compiled and presented to ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5. Readability was assessed using the Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level, Gunning Fog, Coleman–Liau, and Simple Measure of Gobbledygook indices. Quality was evaluated with the DISCERN tool, response accuracy scores, and Journal of the American Medical Association (JAMA) criteria. Interrater agreement was measured using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC).

Results

ChatGPT-4o generated longer responses (802 vs. 242 words; p<0.001) with slightly higher readability grade levels (10.81 vs. 10.37; p=0.031). Accuracy (2.00 vs. 1.85; p=0.323) and DISCERN scores (49.35 vs. 48.93; p=0.747) showed no significant differences. All responses received a JAMA score of 0 due to the absence of citations, authorship, or transparency indicators. Interrater reliability indicated moderate to good agreement (ICC: 0.68–0.80).

Conclusion

ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 provide generally satisfactory yet non-comprehensive, limited-quality information at a level above tenth-grade regarding hallux rigidus fusion surgery. Although linguistically coherent, responses lack evidence-based detail and individualized guidance. These models may supplement, but cannot replace, expert orthopedic counseling. Ensuring physician oversight and integrating validated, updated clinical content remain essential for safe implementation of AI-generated patient information.

1. Introduction

Hallux rigidus is a degenerative form of osteoarthritis affecting the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint. It impacts about 2.5% of the population, with a higher occurrence in those over 50 years old, and is more common in women.1,2 As this joint bears up to 119% of body weight during walking, limitations in its movement can significantly impair an individual’s overall quality of life. 3 In advanced disease, characterized by severely narrowed joint space and uncontrolled pain, surgical intervention becomes essential and arthrodesis of the first MTP joint is regarded as the “gold standard” for long-term pain relief and stability.4,5 Yet, the permanent loss of motion associated with this procedure raises concerns, particularly for active individuals, prompting ongoing exploration of alternative treatments. Consequently, over the past twenty years, there has been a significant trend in research and development of both joint-preserving surgical options (such as interposition arthroplasty and metatarsal osteotomies) and implant-based solutions (like synthetic cartilage implant and total-hemiarthroplasty).4,6,7

The growing number of treatment alternatives adds complexity to individualized decision-making, complicating patients’ chances of reaching optimal treatment. For advanced-stage hallux rigidus, treatment options depend on radiological findings and also on the severity of symptoms, the patient’s functionality, lifestyle, and expectations for postoperative recovery. 8 Considering all that, having access to unambiguous and reliable information is crucial for patients to make informed choices. Regrettably, the scarcity of basic conventional written materials and the widespread use of complicated terms frequently obstruct patient literacy from attaining the required standard.

Today, patients often seek online health information to explore treatment options after a diagnosis, formulate questions for their physician, or feel more confident in their decision-making. 9 This pattern is reshaping the relationship between patients and physicians, converting from a traditional model to a more collaborative one. Nevertheless, the accuracy and neutrality of online information can vary widely, and misinformation or incomplete data can negatively impact patient choices and even clinical outcomes. 10

In recent years, large language models (LLMs) powered by artificial intelligence (AI), particularly generative AI systems like ChatGPT, have become significant innovations in health literacy. ChatGPT is widely utilized in areas including patient education, health communication, and treatment guidance, as it delivers linguistically consistent and comprehensible responses to open-ended user inquiries. 11 Recent research indicates that ChatGPT performs at least satisfactorily, particularly in answering patient questions, explaining surgical options, and summarizing procedural details.12,13 However, the accuracy, readability, and dependence on scientific references in its responses are still subjects of debate, emphasizing an ongoing need for additional research.14,15

LLM-generated patient information has been evaluated in several foot and ankle settings.11,16–19 However, patient counseling for first metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis poses distinct challenges because patients must weigh expected pain relief against permanent loss of joint motion, alternative procedures, potential complications, and postoperative functional expectations.5,8,20–22 The scientific contribution of the present study lies in comparing the two models available through the interface at the time of data collection specifically for fusion-related questions and in evaluating their readability, accuracy, information quality, and performance across question types.

2. Material and methods

Reporting of this study followed the Chatbot Assessment Reporting Tool (CHART) guideline for studies evaluating generative artificial intelligence–driven chatbots providing health advice. 23 Common patient questions about hallux rigidus fusion surgery were collected from the patient information sections of the top ten orthopedic hospitals in the world, as ranked by Newsweek [https://rankings.newsweek.com/worlds-best-specialized-hospitals-2025/orthopedics], and drawing upon the clinical expertise of two board-certified orthopedic surgeons specialized in foot and ankle surgery (M.K. and B.E.), a list of the 20 most prevalent inquiries was curated. The questions were submitted to GPT-4o and GPT-5 through the freely accessible public ChatGPT interface (OpenAI, San Francisco, CA, USA) on September 12, 2025, in Ankara, Türkiye. The interface was accessed without signing into a user account; therefore, stored conversation history, user memory, and personalized settings were unavailable. A new chat session was initiated for each question and each model to prevent previous prompts or responses from influencing subsequent outputs. Each question was submitted once, using identical wording and order for both models, to reflect a single real-world patient interaction rather than repeated testing across sessions. The web-search function was not activated, and none of the collected responses displayed web-search indicators or linked sources. No custom instructions, uploaded files, follow-up prompts, or response regeneration were used. Temperature and other generation parameters were not accessible through the public interface and therefore remained at the platform defaults. The models were identified according to the version labels displayed in the publicly available user interface at the time of data collection, which indicated “ChatGPT-4o” and “ChatGPT-5” as selectable model options. These labels were recorded as presented in the platform and are reported accordingly to ensure reproducibility of the study conditions. The current study did not require ethical approval since no protected health information or patient identification was recorded, analyzed, or published.

2.1. Classification and evaluation process

Questions were grouped into the themes of Fact, Policy, and Value based on the Rothwell classification system. 24 In this system, Fact questions assess a situation’s validity, Policy questions analyze an action’s appropriateness and consequences, and Value questions evaluate the worth of an idea, event, or object.

The obtained answers were independently evaluated by two board-certified orthopedic specialists (M.K., B.E.), who were blinded to model identity and to each other’s ratings, in light of the evidence-based literature and American College of Foot and Ankle Surgeons (ACFAS) Clinical Consensus Statements on hallux rigidus. 5

2.2. Readability analysis

The understandability of ChatGPT’s generated texts was evaluated through four commonly used metrics in online medical literature: Flesch–Kincaid Grade Level (FKGL), Gunning Fog Index (GFI), Coleman–Liau Index (CLI), and Simple Measure of Gobbledygook Index (SMOG).25–27 These formulas give the number of years (grade level) of education required to understand a text, with lower scores reflecting higher levels of readability.

2.3. Quality assessment

The answers’ quality was assessed with the DISCERN tool, a widely recognized and validated instrument for evaluating health education materials.16,28–30 DISCERN comprises 16 questions, each rated from 1 (low) to 5 (high). The assessment focused on three primary categories: DISCERN-1: Reliability, DISCERN-2: Quality of Treatment Options, and DISCERN-3: Overall Quality. Scientific accuracy was assessed using the response scoring system developed by Mika et al. 31 This system was initially designed to evaluate ChatGPT responses related to hip arthroplasty and has since been applied to many orthopedic topics including foot and ankle surgery.17,18 Each rater independently compared every response with current evidence and the ACFAS Clinical Consensus Statements on hallux rigidus, considering factual correctness, completeness, clinically important omissions, overgeneralizations, and statements requiring clarification. 5 A score of 1 indicated an excellent response requiring no clarification; a score of 2 indicated a satisfactory response requiring minimal clarification; a score of 3 indicated a satisfactory response requiring moderate clarification; and a score of 4 indicated an unsatisfactory response requiring substantial clarification. 2.4. Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics software (version 28.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics (mean, median, standard deviation, minimum, maximum, and interquartile range) were calculated for continuous variables, while categorical data were presented as counts and percentages (%). The agreement between two independent raters was assessed using the Intraclass Correlation Coefficient (ICC), calculated with a two-way random-effects model assuming absolute agreement, and reported with 95% confidence intervals. ICC values below 0.50 indicate poor agreement; 0.50 to 0.75, moderate; 0.75 to 0.90, good; and above 0.90, excellent agreement. Normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. For pairwise comparisons between ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5, normally distributed data were analyzed with paired t-tests, while non-normally distributed data were tested using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Effect sizes were calculated for the readability comparisons. Cohen’s d_z was reported for paired t-tests, whereas r, calculated as Z/√N, was reported for Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. Absolute d_z values of 0.20, 0.50, and 0.80 and r values of 0.10, 0.30, and 0.50 were interpreted as small, moderate, and large effects, respectively. Comparisons across Rothwell categories (Fact, Policy, Value) were performed with one-way ANOVA for normal data and the Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normal data. In all analyses, the significance level was set at p < 0.05.

While in ChatGPT-4o, 45% (9) of them include recommendations to consult a doctor or healthcare professional and to follow the surgeon’s instructions, this ratio only reached 20% (4) in ChatGPT-5.

4. Discussion

This research is among the first to compare the responses provided by ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 to commonly asked patient questions about hallux rigidus fusion surgery. Our findings show that ChatGPT-4o produced significantly longer responses with a higher grade level of readability, while there were no notable differences between the two models in terms of accuracy and response quality.

The mean readability grade levels were 10.81 for ChatGPT-4o and 10.37 for ChatGPT-5. Although this difference was statistically significant (p = 0.031) and had a moderate standardized effect size (d_z = 0.52), the absolute difference was less than half a grade level and is unlikely to represent a substantial difference in patient comprehension. Differences in FKGL and SMOG scores also showed moderate-to-large standardized effects, but their absolute magnitudes remained below one grade level. Therefore, the more clinically relevant finding is that both models generated responses at approximately a tenth-grade level, exceeding the commonly recommended sixth- to eighth-grade readability range for patient education materials. 34 These findings are consistent with previous studies evaluating the readability of ChatGPT-generated responses in similar clinical contexts.12,19,35 However, some responses exceeded the twelfth-grade level, which may further limit comprehension, particularly among individuals with limited health literacy. Additionally, all ChatGPT responses had a consistent structure and included subheadings. This organized format showcases the capability of LLMs to deliver linguistically coherent and instructive guidance. In contrast to the 100% rate reported in previous literature, this study found that only half of the responses in one version and one-fifth in the other recommended consulting a healthcare professional. 19

The lack of a significant difference between ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 in terms of DISCERN and response accuracy scores suggests that both models provide a “satisfactory but not excellent” performance on patient information questions related to hallux rigidus fusion surgery. These satisfactory and fair quality responses align with previous studies in the literature on health literacy in the field of foot and ankle surgery and other orthopedic surgeries.17,36–40 More recent comparative studies have also indicated that the relative strengths of LLMs may vary across clinical tasks and evaluation domains, including content depth, source support, quality, reliability, and readability.41,42 All responses received a JAMA benchmark score of 0 because they lacked authorship, attribution, disclosure, and currency information. This result was largely expected under the non-browsing conditions of the study, as the chatbot interface did not routinely display these conventional publication metadata. Consequently, the JAMA benchmark had limited discriminatory value for comparing individual responses or the two models, and the uniform score should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalent clinical accuracy or overall information quality. Rather, it identifies a shared limitation in source transparency; the benchmark was retained primarily to facilitate comparison with previous LLM studies.12,43

The two models showed no statistically significant differences in DISCERN or response accuracy scores at a single assessment point; however, the present study cannot determine whether LLM performance has plateaued or predict the performance of future model versions. These findings should therefore be interpreted as a time-specific evaluation of the models available at the time of data collection. Continued reassessment will be necessary as model architectures, training data, and interface features evolve. Future research should focus on improving the reliability and clinical utility of AI-generated patient information through structured referencing systems, links to updated clinical guidelines, and clearer disclosure of information sources. Regardless of future developments, patient-specific interpretation and shared decision-making should remain grounded in clinician–patient interaction.

Although the evaluated patient responses provided largely accurate basic information, they had several deficiencies in terms of content. Firstly, the majority of responses lacked evidence-based data support; for example, the previous literature reported the non-union rate in hallux rigidus fusion surgery as 5–10% and the need for hardware removal as 5–15%. 20 This incomplete presentation of rates hinders patients from forming realistic expectations about treatment outcomes. Secondly, many responses did not effectively guide the clinical decision-making process. For instance, although surgical preferences should be tailored for patient subgroups using treatment algorithms based on the Coughlin and Shurnas classification, such guidance was rarely included.8,21 Other shortcomings included unclear timelines and decision criteria (e.g., radiological union, painlessness, balance tests) under functional headings like return to sports or work. 22 In many responses, exceptional clinical scenarios (such as elderly patients, bilateral surgeries, or accompanying deformities) were omitted, and explanations tended to be generalized with the term “most patients.” In some responses, the term “eliminating pain” was used as one of the reasons for fusion surgery. While this might not be entirely inaccurate, it raises patients’ expectations too high. In case of an adverse event, or even if everything seems perfect, some patients may still experience varying levels of pain, even a year after surgery. 44 Furthermore, ChatGPT’s responses rely on generic recommendations while often overlooking practical lifestyle and conservative management advice, including the role of footwear. Recent evidence suggests that although footwear may not be a primary causative factor in hallux rigidus, specific shoe types—such as those with rigid soles and low heels—can significantly improve symptomatic comfort, whereas flat, flexible shoes like sandals may exacerbate pain. 45 In line with these previously identified content gaps, the omission of practical footwear advice further underscores the broader limitations of LLMs in comprehensive patient counseling and suggests that, despite their demonstrated potential in health communication, AI systems like ChatGPT remain limited in clinical guidance, individualization, and evidence-based structuring.

In clinical practice, LLM-generated information could be incorporated into hallux rigidus counseling as a surgeon-reviewed previsit information sheet or postvisit summary covering the indication for first metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis, alternative procedures, expected loss of motion, potential risks, and postoperative recovery.5,20 Before patient use, the treating surgeon should verify the accuracy of the content and tailor it to disease severity, activity level, patient expectations, and an appropriate reading level. Evidence from musculoskeletal rehabilitation similarly supports clinician review of LLM-generated recommendations; therefore, such material should supplement rather than replace surgeon-led counseling, informed consent, and shared decision-making. 46

Exploratory subgroup analyses based on the Rothwell classification did not detect statistically significant differences between ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 in response length, readability, or quality scores across Fact, Policy, and Value questions. However, the predominance of Fact questions (n = 14), compared with only three Policy and three Value questions, substantially limited the statistical power of these comparisons, particularly for the latter two categories. Therefore, the nonsignificant findings should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalent model performance across question types, and the subgroup results should be considered descriptive and hypothesis-generating. Although the Rothwell classification provides a useful framework for categorizing patient questions, larger and more evenly distributed question sets are required for adequately powered subgroup comparisons. 12

This study has several limitations. First, the analysis was limited to 20 questions. Although these questions were selected to represent common concerns of patients considering first metatarsophalangeal joint arthrodesis, they may not capture the full range of patient information needs, thereby limiting the breadth and generalizability of the findings. This limitation is particularly relevant to the Rothwell subgroup analyses, in which the Policy and Value categories contained only three questions each. These subgroup comparisons should therefore be considered exploratory and interpreted cautiously. Second, data collection was conducted at a single time point, and each question was submitted only once per model. Because LLM responses may vary across independent sessions and following model or platform updates, the findings represent a snapshot of performance under the specific conditions of data collection and may not be fully reproducible at later time points. Future studies should incorporate repeated queries across independent sessions and multiple time points to quantify within-model variability and temporal changes. Third, only the models available through the unsigned public ChatGPT interface were evaluated; paid-account configurations and other LLM platforms were not assessed. Fourth, only English-language responses were analyzed, and the models were not evaluated using patient profiles representing different levels of health literacy. Finally, there is currently no widely accepted gold standard for assessing the accuracy of LLM-generated medical responses. To provide a structured assessment, two board-certified orthopedic surgeons independently evaluated response accuracy and quality using prespecified scoring instruments, and interrater reliability was calculated.

5. Conclusion

This study presents a comparative analysis of responses provided by ChatGPT-4o and ChatGPT-5 to frequently asked patient questions regarding hallux rigidus fusion surgery. The findings reveal that both models perform similarly in terms of content accuracy and quality; however, ChatGPT-5 generated shorter responses written at a somewhat lower grade level than ChatGPT-4o. Most responses were satisfactory in terms of patient education, but they fell short in depth, personalization, and the provision of adequate evidence-based information to aid in decision-making. The lack of support from the literature, neglect of clinical exceptions, and insufficient comparison of alternative treatment options might suggest that these systems provide limited reliability for clinical decision-making. These systems should not be considered a reliable source of information on their own; they must be supported by expert physician supervision and additional resources before being used in clinical practice. However, the potential for ChatGPT and other LLMs to increase patient literacy is undeniable, and combining this potential with reliable and up-to-date content moderation could significantly transform patient information processes in the future.

Ethical considerations

This study did not involve human participants, identifiable patient data, or biological materials. It was carried out using publicly available, anonymized, and non-interactive AI systems, along with expert assessments from the study authors. As per Turkish national research regulations, studies that do not involve human subjects or identifiable clinical data are exempt from ethics committee review. Consequently, IRB approval and informed consent were not necessary. The two orthopedic surgeons who assessed the AI-generated responses are co-authors and performed their evaluations as part of their academic responsibilities; therefore, consent to participate was not required. The study adhered to the ethical standards outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.
