Introduction

Aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage is a severe condition with reported in-hospital mortality ranging from 7.1% to 24.1%, but the term in-hospital mortality is not consistently defined, and methodological limitations persist in studies investigating mortality and potential risk factors for mortality. In Denmark, nearly all patients with aSAH are admitted to a neurosurgical department and recorded in a nationwide registry.

Research question

Using nationwide clinical data we aimed to estimate mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department and identify risk factors associated with mortality in an unselected aSAH cohort.

Material and methods

All patients with aSAH recorded in the Danish Stroke Registry between January 1st 2017 and August 31st, 2022 were included. Associations between potential risk factors and mortality were examined using univariate and multivariate logistic regression models, adjusting for age, sex and neurosurgical admission. Subgroup analyses were conducted among patients who underwent closure of the aneurysm.

Results

The cohort comprised 1374 patients. Overall mortality was 22.0%. Mortality among patients who underwent aneurysm treatment was 11.9%. Lower Glasgow Coma Scale score, higher World Federation of Neurological Surgeons score, higher Fisher scale score, aneurysm size >12 mm, posterior circulation location, rebleeding and cerebral infarction were significantly associated with mortality.

Discussion and conclusion

In conclusion, this nationwide clinical register-based study showed that mortality after aSAH remained substantial, with 22.0% among all patients admitted to a neurosurgical department in Denmark and 11.9% among those who underwent closure of the aneurysm. Mortality was mainly related to initial clinical severity and complications rather than pre-existing comorbidity.

Highlights

• First nationwide study including all aSAH patients from a detailed database.

• Estimated mortality and risk factors in an unselected nationwide cohort of aSAH patients.

• Overall in-hospital mortality was 22.0%, 11.9% among patients undergoing aneurysm closure.

• Mortality is mainly driven by initial clinical severity and acute neurological complications.

• Key predictors include GCS, WFNS, Fisher scores, large aneurysms, posterior location, rebleed.

1 Introduction

Aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage (aSAH) is a severe disease with high mortality and morbidity (Ikawa et al., 2020; Waweru and Gatimu, 2019; Dharia et al., 2020; Said et al., 2025; Ohta et al., 2023). Most patients die within the primary hospitalisation, often reported as in-hospital mortality, which over the past decade range from 7.1% to 24.1% (Ikawa et al., 2020; Waweru and Gatimu, 2019; Dharia et al., 2020; Said et al., 2025; Ohta et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Göttsche et al., 2022; Stienen et al., 2018; Schatlo et al., 2020; Øie et al., 2020; Stauning et al., 2022; Udy, 2020; Lantigua et al., 2015; Qureshi et al., 2005). However, the term in-hospital mortality is not consistently defined. In particular, the time frame covered by in-hospital mortality is frequently unclear and depends on the duration of hospitalisation, which may vary considerably, dependent on whether the term merely covers admittance to a neurosurgical department only or it also covers subsequent admittances to department of neurology and/or rehabilitation units (Ikawa et al., 2020; Waweru and Gatimu, 2019; Dharia et al., 2020; Said et al., 2025; Ohta et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Göttsche et al., 2022; Stienen et al., 2018; Schatlo et al., 2020; Øie et al., 2020; Stauning et al., 2022; Udy, 2020; Lantigua et al., 2015; Qureshi et al., 2005).

Several studies have investigated potential risk factors for poor outcome following aSAH, identifying a significant association between age, hypertension, smoking and severity of the haemorrhage and mortality (Dharia et al., 2020; Göttsche et al., 2022; Stienen et al., 2018; Øie et al., 2020; Stauning et al., 2022; Lantigua et al., 2015; Luong et al., 2021; Mahajan et al., 2023). However, significant methodological limitations persist in studies investigating mortality following aSAH, as well as in those evaluating potential risk factors for mortality. The studies are often based on single-centre study populations (Göttsche et al., 2022; Stauning et al., 2022; Lantigua et al., 2015), focus on selected patient populations, such as subgroups who underwent closure of their aneurism, leaving out patients not found eligible for treatment (Ikawa et al., 2020; Dharia et al., 2020; Zhu et al., 2023; Stienen et al., 2018; Udy, 2020; Stauning et al., 2022) or they are limited to smaller geographical areas (Waweru and Gatimu, 2019; Said et al., 2025; Ohta et al., 2023; Luong et al., 2021). These factors compromise the generalisability of the results and makes it difficult to generalise the findings to broader and unselected groups.

Consequently, overall mortality and the independent contribution of pre-existing comorbidities in a contemporary, clinically verified, and truly unselected national cohort remain incompletely investigated.

Since 2017, the Danish Stroke Register (DanStroke) has systematically collected comprehensive clinical data on all patients diagnosed with aSAH in Denmark. All aSAH patients are transferred to one of the four neurosurgical departments, where it is mandatory to register them in DanStroke. All four departments follow common national treatment guidelines which ensures standardized patient management (Obbekjær et al., 2024). These national guidelines are consistent with the guidelines set by American Stroke Association and European Stroke Organization (Hoh et al., 2023; Steiner et al., 2013). The nationwide registry combined with standardized national treatment guidelines, provides a unique opportunity to minimise selection bias, improve case ascertainment, and enable more granular adjustment for both baseline comorbidity and acute disease severity.

Hence, our objectives were to investigate the mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department following aSAH in a nationwide, unselected patient population, and to identify potential risk factors associated with mortality.

2 Methods

2.1 Study design and population

This study was a nationwide register-based cohort study. Patients were identified from the DanStroke register during the period January 1st, 2017, to August 31st, 2022. The DanStroke is a clinical register upheld by The Danish Healthcare Quality Institute. The clinical data are collected by physicians at the treating hospitals by filling a detailed scheme for every patient. Virtually all patients with aSAH are managed in one of the four departments of neurosurgery in Denmark, which ensures data collection on all patients. Completeness of data collection is continuously evaluated and adjusted by the steering committee of DanStroke.

2.2 Management of aSAH in Denmark

Denmark has a tax-funded healthcare system providing universal access to emergency and hospital care. Patients with suspected aSAH undergo initial evaluation with non-contrast computed tomography of the cerebrum (CTc) and CT angiography (CTA) at the nearest emergency department. Patients with SAH are subsequently transferred to one of the four neurosurgical centres. If a CTA cannot be performed at the primary hospital, the angiogram will be made after admission at the neurosurgical department.

All patients are admitted to a neurointensive care unit. The treatment strategy is determined collaboratively by vascular neurosurgeons and interventional neuroradiologists. When active treatment is deemed appropriate, the ruptured aneurysm is secured either by microsurgical clipping or endovascular occlusion. Depending on the clinical condition, it is aimed to treat the aneurysm during daytime within the first 24 h. All patients with aSAH receive prophylactic nimodipine for 21 days. Patients are typically managed at the neurosurgical department throughout this period, unless their clinical condition allows earlier transfer to a local neurological department. From there, the patients are referred for rehabilitation at either a municipal rehabilitation centre or a specialised neurorehabilitation centre.

2.3 Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patient aged ≥ 18 years admitted to one of the four neurosurgical centres in Denmark with a diagnosis of aSAH, identified by International Classification of Disease (ICD) 10 codes: 160.0-160.7. Patients with aneurysms from a dissection or patients with spontaneous SAH without a saccular aneurysm are not registered in DanStroke.

Patients were excluded if their vital status at the time of discharge from the neurosurgical department was not documented.

2.4 Endpoint and covariates

It is mandatory for the neurosurgical centres in Denmark to report data on all admitted patients with aSAH to DanStroke. The endpoint of this study was mortality within the primary admission to one of the four neurosurgical departments.

DanStroke provides data on the following variables: sex, age at aSAH diagnosis, neurosurgical department, hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, previous history for acute myocardial infraction (AMI), periphery atherosclerosis, smoking status, previous transient ischemic attack (TIA), and treatment with anticoagulation medication. Furthermore, the register contributes information on location of the intracranial aneurysm, number of intracranial aneurysms, the size of intracranial aneurysms, as well as Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS), Fisher scale score and World Federation of Neurological surgeons (WFNS) score at the time of admission.

Neurological complications to aSAH are recorded and includes rebleeding, hydrocephalus, ventriculitis, ischemic infarctions and vasospasm. Rebleeding is defined as the presence of rebleeding observed on a CTc after SAH was diagnosed but prior to aneurysm occlusion. Hydrocephalus is defined as a condition that required an external ventricular drain (EVD) due to clinical symptoms and radiological finding. Ventriculitis is defined as an EVD-associated infection confirmed by positive bacterial cultures. Vasospasm (or delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI)) is considered present if demonstrated on CTA or transcranial Doppler or based on strong clinical suspicion as global or focal neurological deterioration without other cause. Infarctions occurred after the treatment, due to any cause, were defined as visible ischemic lesions on a CTc.

2.5 Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are presented as means with range, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. Associations between potential risk factors and mortality were examined using univariate and multivariate logistic regression models. The multivariable models were adjusted for potential confounders and mediators, including age, sex, and hospital of admission, to ensure comparability between groups and valid estimation of independent risk factors for mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department. Although treatment guidelines and clinical standards are largely uniform across the four departments, differences in geographical catchment areas and regional variation in prehospital morbidity may contribute to inter-hospital differences in mortality (Stauning et al., 2025). Adjustment for department of admission therefore accounts for these structural differences between patient populations.

To reduce potential confounding related to withdrawal of care, additional analyses were performed among patients who underwent aneurysm occlusion. Both univariable and multivariable analyses were conducted. The multivariable analyses were adjusted for age, GCS score at admission, and time from ictus to aneurysm occlusion, as these variables are potential confounders of the association between the investigated risk factors and mortality (Stauning et al., 2022; Lantigua et al., 2015; Mahajan et al., 2023).

Results are reported as odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Missing data were handled using multiple imputation by chained equations. Twenty imputed datasets were generated with the R package MICE.

2.6 Statistical power and sample size

The study cohort was based on an existing retrospective dataset; therefore, no a priori sample size calculation was performed. The adequacy of the sample size was assessed based on the number of outcome events available for multivariable modelling. Among 1374 included patients, 302 deaths occurred. The final logistic regression model included 23 covariates, corresponding to 13.1 events per variable, which exceeds the conventional minimum threshold of 10 events per variable and supports the reliability of the model estimates.

2.7 Ethics

The study was approved by the Danish Stroke Register and the Data Protection Board of Copenhagen University Hospital (reference number p-2023-14241) and exemption from individual patient consent was waived. The pseudo-anonymized data were stored and handled in accordance with the Danish Data Protection Agency and the General Data Protection Regulations.

3 Results

3.1 Study population

Between January 1st, 2017, and August 31st, 2022, a total of 1576 patients with aSAH were identified in DanStroke. 150 patients were excluded as they were not admitted to a neurosurgical department. Additional fifty-two patients were excluded for further analyses due to missing information regarding vital status at discharge from the neurosurgical department, resulting in a final study population of 1374 patients included for analysis (Fig. S1 in supplementary). This corresponds to an estimated annual incidence of 5.4 per 100,000 person-years, based on a total population in Denmark with an age of 18 years or more of 4,526,363 individuals by January 1st, 2020 (Danmarks Statistik, 2020).

3.4 Unadjusted analyses

Demographic factors: Older age was associated with mortality (OR = 1.05 95% CI 1.04:1.06), whereas sex was not.

Cardiovascular comorbidities: Significant associations were observed between several cardiovascular comorbidities and mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department, including previous AMI (OR = 2.42, 95% CI 1.91:3.06), atrial fibrillation (OR = 3.62, 95% CI 2.82:4.64), hypertension (OR = 1.45, 95% CI 1.26:1.59) and peripheral arteriosclerosis (OR = 2.41, 95% CI 1.77:3.26). Diabetes (OR = 2.27 95% CI 1.79:2.86) and anticoagulant treatment (OR = 1.59 95% CI 1.36:1.76) were also significantly associated with a higher mortality.

Factors related to the haemorrhage and aneurysm: high WFNS score, high Fisher scale score, and low GCS score at the time of admission were all strongly associated with mortality during admittance to a neurosurgical department (all with p-values <0.0001).

Aneurysms larger than 12 mm and those located in the posterior circulation were significantly associated with increased mortality (both with p-values <0.0001).

Treatment modality: We found no significant difference in mortality between patients who underwent endovascular treatment and those who underwent surgical clipping (p = 0.29). As expected, a clinical presentation which was too poor for treatment of the aneurysm was associated with a substantially higher mortality (OR = 78.7 95% CI 45.3:147.0).

Neurological complications to aSAH: Rebleeding prior to aneurysm closure (OR = 3.54 95% CI 2.49:5.01) and infarction due to any cause (OR = 2.79 95% CI 2.11:3.69) were significantly associated with mortality during admittance to a neurosurgical department. Vasospasm (OR = 0.55 95% CI 0.38:0.77) was associated with lower mortality. Due to the limited number of non-surviving patients with ventriculitis (<5 cases), logistic regression analysis was not feasible.

3.5 Adjusted analyses

Cardiovascular comorbidities: None of the cardiovascular comorbidities remained significantly associated with mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department.

Factors related to the haemorrhage and aneurysm: Low GCS score (OR = 0.78 95% CI 0.76:0.81), high WFNS score (OR = 2.31 95% CI 2.08:2.59), high Fisher scale score (OR = 3.80 95% CI 2.94:5.02), aneurysms located in the posterior circulation (OR = 1.87 95% CI 1.41:2.28), and aneurysms larger than 12 mm (OR = 2.71 95% CI 1.92:3.81) remained significantly associated with mortality.

Neurological complications to aSAH: Rebleeding prior to aneurysm occlusion (OR = 3.73 95% CI 2.58:5.39) and infarctions (OR = 3.42 95% CI 2.53: 4.62) continued to show significant associations with higher mortality.

3.7 Unadjusted analyses

Demographic factors: Older age was associated with mortality (OR = 1.04 95% CI 1.03:1.06), whereas sex was not.

Cardiovascular comorbidities: Significant associations were observed between diabetes (OR = 2.33 95% CI 1.33: 5.61), hypertension (OR = 1.47 95% CI 1.03: 2.09), anticoagulant treatment (OR = 1.80 95% CI 1.15: 2.75) and mortality within the primary admission to a neurosurgical department.

Factors related to the haemorrhage and aneurysm: high WFNS score, high Fisher scale score, and low GCS score at the time of admission were all strongly associated with mortality during admittance to a neurosurgical department (all with p-values <0.0001).

Aneurysms larger than 12 mm and those located in the posterior circulation were significantly associated with increased mortality (both with p-values <0.0001).

Treatment modality and time until treatment: We found no significant difference in mortality between patients who underwent endovascular treatment and those who underwent surgical clipping (p = 0.29). Treatment after 24 h from ictus was associated with lower mortality (OR = 0.56 95% CI 0.38: 0.82). Additional analyses showed that patients with 3-12 were significantly more likely to undergo aneurysm occlusion within 24 h than patient with GCS 13-15 (76.3% vs. 50.9%, p < 0.0001, Table S4).

Neurological complications to aSAH: Rebleeding prior to aneurysm closure (OR = 2.89 95% CI 1.80:4.55), infarction (OR = 10.9 95% CI 7.39:16.3) and hydrocephalus (OR = 9.65 95% CI 5.14: 20.6) were significantly associated with mortality during admittance to a neurosurgical department.

3.8 Adjusted analyses

Cardiovascular comorbidities: Diabetes remained significantly associated with mortality (OR = 2.82 95% CI 1.33: 5.61).

Factors related to the haemorrhage and aneurysm: There was a significantly association between high WFNS score (OR = 1.89 95% CI 1.45: 2.49), high Fisher scale score (OR = 1.88 95% CI 1.35: 2.67), aneurysms located in the posterior circulation (OR = 2.11 95% CI 1.42: 3.10), aneurysms larger than 12 mm (OR = 1.67 95% CI 1.00:2.71) and mortality.

Neurological complications to aSAH: Rebleeding prior to aneurysm occlusion hydrocephalus, and infarctions continued to show significant associations with higher mortality (all with p < 0.0001).

4 Discussion

This nationwide, multicentre study of an unselected cohort comprising virtually all patients in Denmark diagnosed with aSAH over a five-and-a-half-year period demonstrated a mortality of 22.0% during the primarily admittance to a neurosurgical department. Notably, the mortality was 11.9% among patients whose clinical condition allowed treatment of the aneurysm. In the adjusted analyses, a low GCS, high WFNS grade, high Fisher scale score, aneurysm size greater than 12 mm, aneurysms located at the posterior circulation, rebleeding prior to aneurysm occlusion, and cerebral infarction due to any cause were all significantly associated with mortality during the primary neurosurgical admittance (all with p-values < 0.001). By including all patients with aSAH at the national level, irrespective of treatment eligibility, this study provides a comprehensive and representative assessment of mortality and associated risk factors.

4.1 Mortality

In the literature, in-hospital mortality is frequently used as an endpoint, with reported estimates ranging from 7.1% to 24.1% (Ikawa et al., 2020; Waweru and Gatimu, 2019; Dharia et al., 2020; Said et al., 2025; Ohta et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2023; Göttsche et al., 2022; Stienen et al., 2018; Schatlo et al., 2020; Øie et al., 2020; Stauning et al., 2022; Udy, 2020; Lantigua et al., 2015; Qureshi et al., 2005). However, the term is not consistently defined, complicating direct comparisons between studies. Differences in healthcare systems and variation in hospital length of stay influence the observation period underlying this measure. For example, Qureshi et al. reported a mean hospital stay of 11.9 days with a wide standard deviation of ±11.5 days, illustrating the variability in the timeframe used to capture in-hospital deaths (Qureshi et al., 2005).

Similar estimates have been reported in other studies including unselected patient populations. Schatlo et al. reported an in-hospital mortality of 20.1% among 1787 patients with aSAH, although the timeframe and hospital setting defining in-hospital mortality were not specified (Schatlo et al., 2020). Dharia et al. reported an in-hospital mortality of 21.4% in a cohort from 2015 with a mean hospital stay of 15.4 ± 14.3 days (Dharia et al., 2020). The highest reported mortality in an unselected cohort was described by Waweru et al. (24.1%) (Waweru and Gatimu, 2019). However, this study included only 158 patients from a single metropolitan area in Tanzania, representing a markedly different healthcare setting (Waweru and Gatimu, 2019). Overall, even though differences in healthcare systems and definitions of in-hospital mortality, the mortality observed in the present study is broadly consistent with previous reports while being based on a larger and more contemporary nationwide cohort.

Some studies have reported in-hospital mortality exclusively among patients undergoing aneurysm occlusion (Ikawa et al., 2020; Udy, 2020; Stauning et al., 2022). Ikawa et al. reported an in-hospital mortality of 12.2% among patients treated endovascularly, although the in-hospital observation period was not clearly specified (Ikawa et al., 2020). In the present study we found a mortality of 11.9% among patients who underwent aneurysm occlusion.

4.2 Risk factors for mortality

Cardiovascular comorbidities: In the present study, several cardiovascular-related factors, including previous AMI, atrial fibrillation, and diabetes were significantly associated with mortality in unadjusted analyses for the entire study population. However, following adjustment for age, sex, and hospital of admission, these associations vanished. These findings differ from those reported by Kim et al., who analysed in-hospital mortality in a cohort of 53,713 patients with aSAH and found that cardiac failure and myocardial infarction were associated with a significantly increased risk of in-hospital mortality (Kim et al., 2013). Importantly, that study did not adjust for demographic variables, which may account for the discrepancy between their results and ours.

A retrospective cohort study from 2012 including 4695 patients indicates that survivors after SAH have increased long-term mortality and stroke rates compared to a matched non-SAH population (Zaroff et al., 2012) indicating that cardiovascular vulnerability may have greater relevance in the long-term trajectory of these patients than during the acute in-hospital phase. Moreover, SAH-induced cardiac injury has also been associated with an elevated risk of death and heart failure hospitalisation (Ikawa et al., 2020; Schatlo et al., 2020). Notably, in these studies, cardiac failure typically represented a complication arising after the haemorrhagic event rather than a preexisting comorbidity. This distinction underscores the differing prognostic implications of chronic cardiovascular disease versus acute, aSAH-related cardiac dysfunction.

Factors related to the haemorrhage and aneurysm: In this study we found that aneurysms located in the posterior circulation were associated with higher mortality for the entire study population and for the subgroup who underwent occlusion of their aneurysm in both adjusted and unadjusted analyses (Table 2 + 3). This aligns with J. Göttsche et al. and Øie et al. who also found that aneurysms in the posterior complex had the highest mortality rates (Göttsche et al., 2022; Øie et al., 2020). Aneurysms located within the posterior complex are in close relation to the brainstem and the lower cranial nerves responsible for the control of swallow function and respiration, and their rupture can therefore rapidly become life-threatening due to a more direct influence on these structures (Dharia et al., 2020).

The adjusted analysis revealed that low GCS scores, high WFNS scores, high Fisher scale scores, and aneurysms size (larger than 12 mm) remained significantly associated with increased mortality. The robust associations between high WFNS and Fisher scale scores with mortality highlights the utility of these scoring systems in stratifying risk and guiding treatment decisions.

Treatment modality and time until treatment: Our data revealed that a substantial proportion of patients (57.7%) underwent endovascular treatment, while 29.7% underwent surgical treatment (Table 1). As mentioned, the treatment strategy for each patient with aSAH is in every department determined through close collaboration between neurosurgeons and interventional radiologists. In our study, there was no significant difference in mortality between treatment modalities; this finding remained consistent in a subgroup analysis adjusted for aneurysm location (p-value: 0.998). In recent years, the proportion of patients treated with endovascular techniques has increased, driven by advancements in technological instruments and improved accessibility (Mackey et al., 2016; Luther et al., 2020). However, the present data do not allow assessment of the extent to which this shift in treatment strategy has influenced the mortality.

In the subgroup of patients who underwent aneurysm occlusion, 609 out of 1201 underwent treatment within 24 h of ictus. Interestingly, treatment initiated more than 24 h after ictus was associated with lower mortality in the univariable analysis. This finding may reflect confounding by indication, as patients presenting with more severe haemorrhage are more likely to undergo prompt aneurysm occlusion while also having a higher baseline risk of mortality. Supporting this interpretation, patients with GCS 3-12 were significantly more likely to undergo aneurysm occlusion within 24 h than patients with GCS 13-15 (76.3% vs. 50.9%, p < 0.001, Table S4 in supplementary). However, the present dataset does not contain information on the reasons for delayed treatment and therefore cannot distinguish between patient delay (delayed presentation to medical care) and healthcare system delay (delayed diagnostic work-up or treatment).

Neurological complications to aSAH: Infarctions and rebleeding were significantly associated with higher mortality in both adjusted and unadjusted analysis (Table 2 + 3). A multicentre register-based cohort study from 2023 also identified rebleeding after aSAH as an independent predictor of in-hospital mortality (adjusted OR 2.2, 95% CI 1.2:4.1) (Panni et al., 2023), this finding is supported by other studies (Said et al., 2025; Stauning et al., 2022; Lantigua et al., 2015).

In the unadjusted analysis including all patients, vasospasm or DCI appeared to be associated with a lower risk of mortality. This finding most likely reflects a survivor bias. Cerebral vasospasm or DCI typically develop from day 3 after the initial haemorrhage, with the highest incidence occurring between days 8 and 11 following aSAH, although it may occur up to three weeks after ictus (Chalet et al., 2023). Consequently, patients who die early after the initial haemorrhage do not survive long enough to develop vasospasm, which may create the appearance of a protective association in analyses including all patients.

In addition, our broad definition of vasospasm/DCI may have captured patients without clinically significant ischemia, thereby increasing the proportion of survivors classified as having vasospasm and further attenuating the association with mortality.

In contrast, in the subgroup analysis restricted to patients who underwent aneurysm occlusion, vasospasm as associated with a non-significant increase in mortality. This is plausible, as vasospasm or DCI can lead to cerebral infarction, resulting in more extensive brain injury and a higher risk of death (Chalet et al., 2023; Alsbrook et al., 2023; Thompson et al., 2022; Veldeman et al., 2016).

In this study hydrocephalus was defined by placement of an EVD and not by radiological findings alone. Consequently, patients considered too severely affected to benefit from neurosurgical intervention may not have received an EVD despite having hydrocephalus. This may have resulted in under ascertainment of hydrocephalus among patients who died early, thereby attenuating the association between hydrocephalus and mortality in the analysis including all patients. Restricting the analysis to patients who underwent aneurysm occlusion likely reduced this bias, which may explain the stronger association observed in this subgroup in both adjusted and unadjusted analysis.

4.3 Limitations and strengths

This study has limitations. Most notably, the short-term outcome and lack of data to estimate cumulative mortalities or mortality rates per time unit are major limitations. Furthermore, missing data may introduce information bias due to incomplete or inaccurate data recording for some of the variables. Both vasospasm/DCI and hydrocephalus are complications that develop after the initial haemorrhage. Consequently, analyses including all patients are susceptible to survivor bias. Additionally, the observational study design precludes definitive conclusions regarding causality, and unmeasured confounding.

Even with these limitations, this nationwide unselected patient cohort comprises a unique data material. By including all patients with aSAH in an entire country, this study offers a reliable estimate of the mortality and associated risk factors during the primary neurosurgical admittance. Furthermore, the use of standardized clinical guidelines contributes to uniformity in patient management across centres, thereby enhancing the internal validity of the findings. As clinical practice in aSAH management largely follows international guidelines and standards, we regard the results of this study to be applicable to other institutions and countries.

5 Conclusion

In conclusion, this nationwide clinical register-based study showed that mortality after aSAH remained substantial, with 22.0% among all patients admitted to a neurosurgical department in Denmark and 11.9% among those who underwent closure of the aneurysm.

The principal contribution of this study is the confirmation and quantification of established risk factors in a large, real-world nationwide cohort. According to our findings, mortality was primarily associated with poor clinical condition at presentation, rebleeding, and cerebral infarction rather than pre-existing comorbidities or treatment modality. These findings highlight the complexity of aSAH management and the multifactorial determinants of mortality during the acute phase of treatment.
