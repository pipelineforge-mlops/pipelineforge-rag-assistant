Sequential autoimmune and neoplastic manifestations during long-term benralizumab therapy for severe eosinophilic asthma: A descriptive case report

Benralizumab, an anti-interleukin-5 receptor α monoclonal antibody, induces near-complete eosinophil depletion and provides substantial benefit in severe eosinophilic asthma. We report an 80-year-old woman with severe eosinophilic asthma who was treated with benralizumab from August 2020. Peripheral blood eosinophils rapidly decreased to 0/μL, asthma exacerbations ceased, and lung function improved. Over the subsequent four years, while asthma remained well controlled, she developed a temporally ordered sequence of clinical events, including elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis, biopsy-proven erythema nodosum, a radiologically characteristic pulmonary hamartoma, and early-stage hormone receptor-positive breast cancer. Erythema nodosum resolved completely after mastectomy without changes in immunosuppressive therapy, suggesting a possible paraneoplastic association. Among these events, inflammatory arthritis may represent the most biologically plausible finding potentially associated with prolonged eosinophil depletion, whereas the remaining findings are more appropriately interpreted descriptively and in the context of alternative explanations, including aging, background disease incidence, and coincidence. This case describes a rare temporal association in a single patient and does not establish causality. Rather than challenging the established favorable safety profile of benralizumab, it highlights the importance of careful long-term clinical follow-up and attention to newly emerging systemic manifestations during eosinophil-depleting biologic therapy, particularly in older adults.

• Long-term benralizumab therapy was followed by multiple systemic clinical events.

1 Introduction

Biologic therapies targeting type 2 inflammation have transformed the management of severe eosinophilic asthma. Among these, benralizumab, a humanized afucosylated monoclonal antibody against the interleukin-5 receptor alpha (IL-5Rα), induces rapid, near-complete depletion of peripheral and tissue eosinophils through antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, resulting in marked reductions in exacerbations and improvements in lung function and symptom control [1].

Although eosinophils are classically regarded as terminal effector cells in allergic inflammation, accumulating evidence suggests broader roles in immune regulation, tissue homeostasis, and tumor immunology [2,3]. However, the long-term systemic implications of sustained eosinophil depletion remain incompletely understood.

Here, we report an 80-year-old woman with severe eosinophilic asthma who, during long-term benralizumab therapy, developed a temporally ordered sequence of clinical events, including elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis, erythema nodosum, pulmonary hamartoma, and breast cancer. This case is presented as a descriptive clinical observation and may be of interest in considering careful long-term follow-up and attention to newly emerging systemic manifestations during eosinophil-depleting biologic therapy, particularly in older adults.

2 Case presentation

2.1 Background and asthma history

An 80-year-old woman with allergic rhinitis, eosinophilic chronic rhinosinusitis (ECRS), and eosinophilic otitis media developed asthma at age 60. She had never smoked and had no relevant occupational exposures. Endoscopic sinus surgery for ECRS was performed at age 65. She experienced 1–2 exacerbations per year requiring systemic corticosteroids, including one hospitalization for status asthmaticus. Peripheral blood eosinophils were persistently elevated (856/μL). Before biologic therapy, spirometry showed obstructive ventilatory impairment with an FEV1 of 1.27 L (72.2% predicted).

Benralizumab (30 mg subcutaneously every 4 weeks for three doses, then every 8 weeks) was initiated in August 2020 for severe eosinophilic asthma. Following treatment, blood eosinophils decreased to 0/μL, exacerbations ceased, and asthma control improved; FEV1 increased to 1.57 L (91.8% predicted) by early 2022.

2.2 Seronegative rheumatoid arthritis

In September 2022 (25 months after benralizumab initiation), the patient developed bilateral swelling and pain of the finger joints and pitting edema of both feet. Laboratory testing revealed elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), matrix metalloproteinase-3 (MMP-3) and C-reactive protein (CRP), with negative rheumatoid factor (RF), and anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies. Hand radiographs showed no bone erosion. She was initially diagnosed with remitting seronegative symmetrical synovitis with pitting edema (RS3PE) and treated with prednisolone 15 mg/day, resulting in improvement of edema but persistent arthralgia. The diagnosis was revised to elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis, and methotrexate plus iguratimod were introduced, achieving a partial response.

2.3 Erythema nodosum

In September 2023, painful erythematous nodules developed on the anterior shins. Physical examination identified tender subcutaneous swellings over both tibial surfaces. Skin biopsy demonstrated septal panniculitis with lymphohistiocytic infiltration, consistent with erythema nodosum (EN). Despite ongoing low-dose prednisolone (5 mg/day) for rheumatoid arthritis, the EN persisted. No new medications or intercurrent infections were identified. Representative clinical and histopathologic findings are shown in Fig. 2A and E.

2.4 Pulmonary hamartoma

In April 2024, an abdominal CT performed for unrelated reasons incidentally revealed a mass in the left lower lobe. Review of prior chest imaging from 2022 confirmed that the lesion was new. Chest CT demonstrated a 2.1-cm well-circumscribed nodule with internal fat attenuation, suggestive of a pulmonary hamartoma. Bronchoscopic biopsy was non-diagnostic, showing nonspecific fibrous and adipose tissue without atypia. Given the radiologic impression and absence of malignant features, surveillance imaging was planned. Radiologic features consistent with pulmonary hamartoma are shown in Fig. 2B and C.

2.5 Breast cancer and resolution of erythema nodosum

In December 2024, follow-up chest CT incidentally identified a left breast mass. Diagnostic work-up confirmed early-stage invasive ductal carcinoma (estrogen receptor–positive, progesterone receptor–positive, HER2-negative; low Ki-67). Left mastectomy was performed in January 2025, and no adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy was administered due to low-risk features. By February 2025, the patient's EN had completely resolved without changes in immunosuppressive therapy and has not recurred to date. Representative imaging and histopathologic findings are shown in Fig. 2D and F.

2.6 Summary

Over approximately 4.5 years after benralizumab initiation and sustained eosinophil depletion, the patient experienced a sequential emergence of immune-related and neoplastic conditions while asthma remained well controlled. The chronology raises the possibility of broader immunologic effects potentially associated with sustained eosinophil depletion. 3 Discussion

This case describes a rare temporal sequence of autoimmune, inflammatory, and neoplastic manifestations during long-term benralizumab therapy in an elderly woman with severe eosinophilic asthma. While asthma remained well controlled under sustained eosinophil depletion, the patient subsequently developed elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis, erythema nodosum, pulmonary hamartoma, and breast cancer. The descriptive value of this case lies in the chronological clustering of these events during otherwise successful biologic treatment. However, temporal association alone does not establish causality, and alternative explanations, including immunosenescence, multimorbidity, and background disease incidence, must be carefully considered.

Among the events observed in this patient, inflammatory arthritis is the finding for which some biologic plausibility and prior clinical observations exist. Eosinophils have been implicated in immune homeostasis, and mechanistic work has suggested crosstalk between regulatory T cells and immunoregulatory eosinophils in inflammatory arthritis. [1,2]. Clinically, new-onset inflammatory arthritis has been reported during anti–IL-5/IL-5R therapy, including rheumatoid arthritis during anti–IL-5 treatment for chronic eosinophilic pneumonia, and inflammatory arthritis signals have also been described in anti–IL-5 biologic exposure cohorts [3,4]. These observations raise the possibility that pathway-specific immune perturbation may occur in some patients receiving such therapies. In the present case, the emergence of elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis approximately two years after sustained eosinophil depletion is therefore notable. Previous reports have described rheumatoid arthritis and inflammatory arthritis during anti–IL-5/IL-5R therapy [5,6]. The present case should therefore be interpreted within this emerging clinical context. Reports specifically describing elderly-onset seronegative rheumatoid arthritis during benralizumab therapy for severe eosinophilic asthma appear to be limited. Nevertheless, this single case should be interpreted as a descriptive observation rather than evidence that benralizumab, or eosinophil depletion itself, directly triggered autoimmune disease.

The remaining events in this case are less readily interpreted in relation to eosinophil depletion and are more appropriately discussed descriptively. Erythema nodosum is more plausibly interpreted as a possible paraneoplastic inflammatory manifestation than as a direct consequence of eosinophil depletion, given its persistence despite ongoing low-dose corticosteroid therapy and its complete resolution after mastectomy without escalation of immunosuppression. Pulmonary hamartoma is a common benign lesion often detected incidentally on imaging, especially in older adults, and in the absence of supporting mechanistic or epidemiologic evidence, we consider it an incidental benign finding rather than evidence of immune dysregulation. Similarly, the diagnosis of early-stage hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in an 80-year-old woman must be interpreted cautiously in light of background age-related incidence. At present, long-term safety analyses and post-marketing data for benralizumab have not demonstrated a consistent malignancy signal [[7], [8], [9]]. Thus, neither pulmonary hamartoma nor breast cancer should be interpreted as supportive evidence for a broader mechanistic framework linked to eosinophil depletion.

Accordingly, the principal message of this report is not causal, but clinical. This case documents a descriptive temporal association between long-term eosinophil-depleting biologic therapy and subsequent systemic events in a single patient. Among these, seronegative rheumatoid arthritis appears to be the event with the greatest biologic plausibility for a possible association, whereas the remaining findings are better understood in the context of paraneoplastic inflammation, incidental benign pathology, background age-related incidence, or coincidence. Although altered immune homeostasis remains a possible interpretative consideration, this case does not provide evidence for a unifying mechanistic progression.

This report has several limitations. It describes only a single patient, lacks longitudinal immunophenotyping, and cannot distinguish treatment-related effects from age-related coincidence or background risk. No mechanistic biomarkers were available to support altered immune homeostasis, and the temporal sequence observed here may reflect the accumulation of unrelated conditions in an older patient with multiple comorbidities. Rather than supporting a causal conclusion, this case highlights the importance of careful long-term clinical follow-up during eosinophil-depleting biologic therapy, particularly in older adults. Attention to newly emerging systemic manifestations and whole-patient follow-up may be warranted even when respiratory control remains favorable. Further descriptive case accumulation and mechanistic studies are needed to clarify whether any subset of patients receiving eosinophil-depleting biologic therapy is predisposed to broader immune perturbation over time.
