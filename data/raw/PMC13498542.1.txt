Milk-derived extracellular-vesicles (mEVs) have emerged as a promising natural nanocarriers for nutraceutical and therapeutic applications, owing to their rich cargo of bioactive proteins, lipids, microRNAs, and metabolites, coupled with their inherent biocompatibility and stability. Their unique ability to withstand gastrointestinal degradation and cross biological barriers, such as blood-brain-barrier, while eliciting minimal immunogenicity provide a distinct advantage over synthetic delivery systems. Furthermore, mEVs can also be engineered or enriched with functional molecules, enabling the targeted delivery of nutraceuticals, chemotherapeutic agents, anti-inflammatory compounds, and gene regulators. Growing evidence demonstrates their capacity to modulate immune functions, support gut integrity, mitigate oxidative stress, regulate inflammatory processes, and influence systemic metabolic and neurophysiological pathways. However, their translational potential, key challenges, including scale isolation, optimize cargo loading, and comprehensive functional characterization, still limit their broader application. This review summarizes the biological properties, isolation strategies, and therapeutic prospects of mEVs, emphasizing their dual role as nutrition component and precision delivery platforms. Additionally, this review enhances our understanding regarding the beneficial application of milk-EVs as a natural, nontoxic and efficient nutraceutical carrier for next-generation nutraceutical and biomedical innovations.

Introduction

In recent years’ extracellular vesicles (EVs) have emerged as versatile and biocompatible platform for drug and nutraceutical delivery, attracting significant interest in nanomedicine and precision therapeutics. Among divers EV sources milk-derived extracellular-vesicles (mEVs) have gain prominence due to their natural origin, abundance and unique capacity to transport bioactive molecules across physiological barriers, bridging the gap between nutrition and advanced therapeutics (Dogan et al., 2025; Marsh et al., 2025). Milk, a complex bio-fluid contained nanosized EVs secreted by mammary epithelial cells, which facilitate intracellular communication, modulate immune responses and regulate metabolic pathways (Hata et al., 2010; Herwijnen et al., 2018). These attributes have positioned mEVs as promising candidates for emerging concept of “Farm to Pharmacy”, where naturally derived nutritional product is transformed into a clinically relevant delivery vehicle for bioactive compounds.

Milk-derived EVs typically 30 to 200 nm in diameter and possess a lipid bilayer encapsulating proteins, lipids, microRNAs (miRNAs), and other non-coding RNAs (Izumi et al., 2015). Their intrinsic gastrointestinal stability, attributed to membranes enrich with sphingomyelin and cholesterol, enable intact systemic circulation upon oral administration, highlighting their potential as non-invasive delivery systems (Munagala et al., 2016). Moreover, mEVs demonstrated low immunogenicity and high biocompatibility, critical for both pharmaceutical and nutraceutical applications (Samuel et al., 2017). Mechanistic studies have revealed that specific miR-184a, with bovine mEVs, can modulate DNA methylation, immune homeostasis and lipid metabolism in recipient cells illustaratig their biological activities and therapeutic relevance (Zhou et al., 2011). Beyond endogenous effects, mEVs have shown promise in delivering exogenous cargoes, including small molecules, peptides RNA therapeutics phytochemicals (curcumin, resveratrol, and quercetin) enhancing stability bioavailability and targeted delivery (Agrawal et al., 2017; Munagala et al., 2016).

The first scientific report on mEV was published in 2007 (Admyre et al., 2007), marking the beginning of a rapidly expanding research domain that now encompasses hundreds of studies across diverse species, including human, bovine, goat, camel, porcine, and equine milk (Adriano et al., 2021). Owing to their natural abundance since these species produce hundreds of liters milk daily, serves as a scalable and cost effective source of mEVs, offering yields far exceeding those obtained from cell cultures or other biological fluids. These mEVs typically exhibit a uniform nanoscale size of approximately 100 nm, a physicochemical feature highly advantageous for drug delivery and other therapeutic applications. Despite these benefits, their integration into biomedical research and clinical translation remains comparatively limited. Milk itself is a fundamental postnatal nutritional source for all mammals (Haug et al., 2007), and human breast milk plays a critical role in shaping neonatal immunity and intestinal microbial development (Torregrosa Paredes et al., 2014). Building on this biological relevance, contemporary literature has explored mEVs for optimized isolation strategies, drug loading, imaging, and therapeutic modulation. Importantly, the field has recently reached key translational milestones including the first clinical trial assessing mEVs based formulations for RNA therapeutics and anticancer agents, representing a critical translation millstone in the “From Farm to Pharmacy” trajectory.

The key advantage of mEVs is lies in their scalability and cost effectiveness. Unlike synthetic nanoparticles, milk is readily available and EVs can be isolated using ultracentrifugation, size-exclusion chromatography, or precipitation-based methods (Vashisht et al., 2017). This accessibility supports industrial scale production align with biomedicine, facilitate integration in to functional food and nutraceutical formulations. Furthermore, oral administration study, further demonstrate safe gastrointestinal transit, efficient uptake by intestinal epithelial cells, and systemic distribution via endocytosis, underscoring their applicability for chronic disease management and preventive healthcare (Manca et al., 2018).

Despite these advantages, critical challenges persist. Standardizing of EV isolation, characterization of cargo heterogeneity, and ensure batch-to-batch consistency remain significant hurdles to clinical translation (Arain et al. 2023b, 2024b). Ongoing research employing bioengineering strategies, advanced analytical tools, and regulatory frameworks is progressively addressing these limitations (Jiang et al., 2021). This review systematically examine mEVs, encompassing their biogenesis isolation, and characterization methodologies, intrinsic biological functions, and translational potential as natural nanocarriers. By integrating mechanistic insights, preclinical evidence, and early clinical findings, this review highlights mEVs as a promising platform that bridges dietary nutrition and precision medicine, advancing the vision toward alternative therapeutic intervention.

Comparative advantages of milk-derived EVs, over synthetic and other EVs

Milk-derived extracellular vesicles (mEVs) have emerged as a compelling natural nanoplatform when compared with synthetic nanocarriers and other natural EV sources due to their inherent biological origin, excellent biocompatibility, and functional molecular cargo. EVs constitute a diverse family of lipid-bilayer nanoparticles including exosomes, microvesicles, and apoptotic bodies, characterized by their unique biogenesis pathways and selective packaging of proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids (Barathan et al., 2024). Among them, mEVs stand out due to their abundance, cost-effective scalability, and safe oral availability, attributes not shared to the same extent by stem cell derived EVs (SC-EVs) or plant EVs (pEVs) (Sanwlani et al., 2020; Yang et al., 2023). Unlike synthetic nanocarriers such as liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles (PNPs), which are engineered to maximize drug-loading efficiency and physicochemical uniformity, natural EVs carry evolutionarily optimized membrane proteins and lipid signatures that confer high biocompatibility and lower immunogenicity (Du et al., 2023). mEVs also exhibit remarkable physicochemical resilience, with their phospholipid bilayer protecting regulatory RNAs, especially miRNAs, from enzymatic and pH-induced degradation, thereby facilitating passage through the gastrointestinal tract and systemic bioavailability, a stability profile generally superior to many synthetic nanocarriers (Feng et al., 2021). While synthetic vesicles offer reproducibility and customizable physicochemical properties, they lack the intrinsic targeting motifs, membrane transporters, and immune-evasive signatures naturally present in mEVs, although hybrid EV-liposome approaches have been proposed to overcome these limitations (Su et al., 2025).

In terms of targeting ability, safety, and functional delivery efficacy, distinct differences exist among mEVs, synthetic nanocarriers, and other natural EV systems. Natural EVs, including SC-EVs and mEVs, inherently express cell-derived adhesion molecules, tetraspanins, and lipids that participate in tissue tropism and intercellular communication, enabling them to deliver molecular cargos with high physiological relevance (Barathan et al., 2024) Moreover, mEVs support cross-species biological communication and can modulate metabolic, immune, and developmental pathways, enabling unique opportunities for oral nutraceutical and therapeutic applications (Sanwlani et al., 2020). Conversely, synthetic nanocarriers including PLGA and PEGylated nanoparticles offer unmatched tunability, large drug-loading capacity, and controlled release characteristics with well-established manufacturing pipelines (Elmowafy et al., 2023). However, their clinical deployment is often constrained by dose-dependent toxicity, complement activation, and unpredictable biodistribution without careful surface engineering. Compared with PNPs, mEVs provide superior biocompatibility and immune tolerance, yet their heterogeneity, batch-to-batch variability, and lower yield complicate standardization and regulatory alignment (Jiang et al., 2024). SC-EVs exhibit high intrinsic potency and superior targeted delivery due to trophic and regenerative cargos but are limited by donor variability, high production cost, and stringent regulatory hurdles (Karnas et al., 2023). Plant-derived EVs offer excellent safety and cost-effectiveness, and their innate phytochemicals enhance immunomodulatory effects; however, their non-mammalian lipid and protein composition may restrict predictable biodistribution and limit loading efficiency for mammalian therapeutic cargos (Kürtösi et al., 2024). Thus, while synthetic nanocarriers excel in design customizability, natural EVs including mEVs surpass them in physiological compatibility and evolved targeting cues.

Scalability, manufacturability, and translational feasibility further distinguish these delivery systems. Milk EVs benefit from an abundant raw material sources such as bovine or caprine milk making large-scale isolation substantially more feasible and cost-effective compared to SC-EVs, whose yields are inherently limited by cell culture capacity and donor-dependent variability (Barathan et al., 2024). Plant-derived EVs also offer a scalable platform due to the high biomass of plant tissues; however, their purification requires rigorous steps to eliminate plant-derived contaminants and to ensure consistent physicochemical quality. In contrast, synthetic nanocarriers can be industrially produced with high batch reproducibility, but this advantage is counterbalanced by their need for complex surface modifications to mimic EV-like targeting, increase circulation time, or reduce immunogenicity (Elmowafy et al., 2023). Hybrid EV-coated PNPs and EV-mimetic nanocarriers are increasingly recognized as a practical route to combine the biocompatibility and targeting properties of natural EVs with the manufacturing precision and drug-loading capacity of synthetic systems (Afzal et al., 2023). Overall, the choice among mEVs, synthetic nanocarriers, and other natural EVs must be driven by the intended administration route, required targeting specificity, regulatory constraints, and scalability needs. Biology, origin and biogenesis of milk-derived extracellular vesicles

Milk is a highly specialized biological fluid that supports neonatal development not only by supplying nutrients but also through bioactive communication mediated by EVs (Arain et al. 2023a). These vesicles form a heterogeneous population that includes exosomes (30–150 nm), microvesicles (100–1000 nm), and apoptotic bodies, each differing in size, mode of biogenesis, and molecular composition (Li et al., 2022). Quantitative profiling indicates that exosomes are the predominant subtype in milk, representing nearly 70–85% of the total EV population, whereas microvesicles contribute a smaller but functionally distinct fraction enriched in membrane-bound cytokines and pro-inflammatory mediators (Di et al., 2024; Jiang et al., 2021).

Milk EVs primarily originate from mammary epithelial cells (MECs) and immune cells residing in the mammary gland (Perrin et al., 2021). MEC-derived EVs are typically enriched in lactation-associated proteins, metabolic enzymes, and regulatory miRNAs such as miR-148a and miR-21, which play crucial roles in gut maturation, epithelial development, and immune tolerance in neonates. In contrast, EVs released from mammary immune cells contain immunomodulatory proteins (MHC-II, TLR adaptors) and miRNAs that help shape mucosal immunity (Théry et al., 2018). Advances in high-resolution proteomics and cell-specific markers now enable clearer differentiation of these EV subpopulations. Exosome biogenesis begins with inward budding of the endosomal membrane, generating intraluminal vesicles (ILVs) within multivesicular bodies (MVBs) (Arya et al., 2024). While the classical Endosomal Sorting Complexes Required for Transport (ESCRT) dependent machinery including TSG101, VPS4, and Alix plays a central role, recent evidence highlights the importance of ESCRT-independent pathways (Cruz Camacho et al., 2023). These include the syndecan-syntenin-Alix axis, ceramide-driven membrane budding, and Rab31-mediated trafficking, all of which have been implicated in milk-specific exosome secretion (Qiao et al., 2024). In contrast, microvesicles are produced by outward budding of the plasma membrane, a process governed by Ca²⁺ dependent cytoskeletal reorganization and phospholipid redistribution.

The abundance, molecular cargo, and biological activity of mEVs vary substantially across species, lactation stages, and maternal physiological conditions. Comparative studies in bovine, human, camel, and goat milk reveal species-specific enrichment of bioactive proteins and miRNAs (Mata et al., 2025). Notably, camel mEVs contain unique metabolic regulators associated with antidiabetic properties, setting them apart from other livestock species (Badawy et al., 2025). Together, these mechanistic insights highlight mEVs as a multifaceted intercellular communication system shaped by tightly regulated biogenesis pathways and the broader maternal biological environment.

Composition and physicochemical properties

Milk-derived EVs are characterized by a lipid bilayer membrane enriched in cholesterol, sphingomyelin, and ceramides, which provide rigidity and protect encapsulated cargo from enzymatic degradation (Subra et al., 2007). This bilayer encloses a diverse array of proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, and bioactive molecules, enabling EVs to function as intercellular messengers. The proteomic content includes canonical exosome markers such as tetraspanins (CD9, CD63, CD81), heat shock proteins (HSP70), and proteins associated with MVB biogenesis (TSG101, Alix) (Jankovičová et al., 2020). In addition, milk-specific proteins such as β-lactoglobulin and caseins are often detected, suggesting selective loading during secretion (Admyre et al., 2007). The nucleic acid cargo of mEVs has attracted particular attention for its regulatory potential. They are enriched in miRNAs (e.g., miR-148a, miR-30a, let-7 family), as well as mRNAs and long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) (Quan et al., 2020). Protected from RNase-mediated degradation by the lipid membrane, these RNAs can be transferred to recipient cells, where they may influence gene expression, immune modulation, and intestinal development (Zempleni et al., 2017). Physicochemical characterization shows that mEVs typically range between 50 and 150 nm, as determined by nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA) and transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Their surface charge (zeta potential) is usually negative under physiological conditions, which contributes to colloidal stability by preventing vesicle aggregation (Vaswani et al., 2017). A unique property of mEVs is their exceptional stability. They resist degradation across a broad pH range, remain intact in simulated gastric fluid, and are protected against enzymatic digestion (e.g., RNase A, trypsin) by their lipid bilayer (Liao et al., 2017). Moreover, they remain stable under refrigeration and freezing conditions. However, thermal treatments such as pasteurization and ultra-high-temperature (UHT) processing can compromise EV yield and structural integrity, likely due to membrane disruption (Arain et al. 2025b; Pieters et al. 2015). This inherent resilience highlights milk as a robust and scalable source of EVs, with promising applications in nutrition, therapeutics, and drug delivery.

Isolation and characterization of milk EVs

Milk EVs have emerged as critical mediator of intercellular communication and promising carrier for therapeutic molecules and biomarkers. Their isolation from the complex milk matrix require careful selection of methods that balance purity, yield, integrity, and scalability Table 3. Traditionally, differential ultracentrifugation (UC) techniques, remains the most widely employed approach, exploiting high centrifugal force to pallet vesicles based on size and density; however, it is time-consuming and may induce aggregation or structural perturbations (Tkach et al., 2018). Alternatives, strategies, including ultrafiltration and polymer based precipitation (polyethylene glycol), offer improved scalability and operational simplicity but often co-isolate non-vesicular components such as lipoproteins (Lőrincz et al., 2014). Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC), frequently combined with UC, enhance purity by separating EVs from soluble proteins and aggregates based on hydrodynamic radius (Böing et al., 2014). Immunoaffinity-based capture, using antibodies against canonical EV surface markers (CD9, CD63, CD81), provides high specificity for vesicle subpopulations but may reduce recovery and introduce antigen-selection bias (Vaswani et al., 2017). Importantly, emerging technologies such as microfluidic-based isolation and next-generation immunomagnetic systems are gaining traction for their capacity to provide high recovery efficiency, improved vesicle integrity, and potential for automation and industrial scalability (Li et al. 2025a; Meggiolaro et al. 2022). These approaches enable precise manipulation of small sample volumes and multiplexed targeting of EV subpopulations, offering significant advantages for large-scale biomanufacturing and translational applications. Industrial-grade purification strategies, including the integration of tangential flow filtration (TFF) with SEC, further allow scalable and reproducible EV enrichment suitable for therapeutic production while minimizing protein contamination and vesicle loss (Kawai-Harada et al., 2024).

Comprehensive characterization of mEVs relies on a multi-method framework to assess particle size, concentration, morphology, surface markers, and cargo composition. Nanoparticle Tracking Analysis (NTA) and Dynamic Light Scattering (DLS) provide quantitative and size distribution profiles, while Electron Microscopy (TEM/SEM), confirm vesicle ultrastructure and cup-shaped morphology (Théry et al., 2018; Vaswani et al., 2019). Atomic Force Microscopy (AFM) enables high-resolution surface topography under near-native conditions. Molecular analysis, including western blotting for canonical EV markers (CD9, CD63, CD81, TSG101, Flotillin-1) and cargo evolution, validate purity and assess milk protein contamination (Blans et al., 2017). Flow cytometry allows immunophenotyping of surface antigens on fluorescently labelled mEVs, and advanced proteomics via mass spectrometry offers unbiased insight into the protein cargo, elucidating functional potential (Zempleni et al., 2017). The choice of isolation and characterization methods is interdependent; techniques that maximize purity and recovery enhance the reliability of downstream analyses. Opportunities and challenges of milk EVs as nanocarriers

Milk is emerging as a prolific and scalable source of EVs, which represent a novel, biocompatible, and cost-effective platform for drug delivery and nutraceutical encapsulation. These natural nanocarriers, comprising exosomes and microvesicles, are intrinsically equipped to protect their cargo from enzymatic degradation in the gastrointestinal tract and facilitate its absorption, enabling efficient oral delivery of therapeutics like chemotherapeutic agents (paclitaxel), nucleic acids (siRNA, miRNA), and anti-inflammatory compounds (Agrawal et al., 2017; Zempleni et al., 2017). The significant opportunity lies in their inherent targeting potential, biocompatibility, and ability to cross biological barriers, including the intestinal epithelium and, potentially, the blood-brain barrier, which is largely attributed to surface proteins such as tetraspanins and integrins acquired from their parent cells (Betker et al., 2019; Lässer et al., 2011). However, the translation of mEVs into clinical applications is fraught with substantial challenges. A primary hurdle is the standardization of isolation and purification methods; techniques like ultracentrifugation, size-exclusion chromatography, and tangential flow filtration must be optimized to yield high-purity milk EV preparations free of contaminating proteins, casein micelles, and milk fat globules, which is critical for reproducibility and safety (Dkhil et al., 2024; Vaswani et al., 2017). Furthermore, a major unresolved challenge is the precise characterization of their pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, and potential for off-target effects, which can vary significantly based on the milk source (e.g., bovine, porcine, human) and the donor’s physiological status (Manca et al., 2018). The scalability of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) compliant production while maintaining batch-to-batch consistency and vesicle integrity is another significant obstacle (Somiya et al., 2018). Despite these hurdles, ongoing research into engineering mEVs through surface modification to enhance tissue-specific targeting and loading efficiency holds immense promise for overcoming these limitations, positioning mEVs as a next-generation nanocarrier platform for transformative oral biologic medicines (Aqil et al., 2017; Barathan et al., 2024).

Milk EVs for nutraceutical delivery

Milk derived EVs, particularly exosomes, have attracted considerable interest as natural nanocarriers for nutraceuticals delivery because they overcome several limitations associated with conventional oral formulations, including poor aqueous solubility, limited gastrointestinal stability, and low systemic bioavailability. Owing to their phospholipid bilayer, intrinsic biocompatibility, resistance to digestive enzymes, and ability to traverse biological barriers, mEVs efficiently protect and transport bioactive compounds such as curcumin, resveratrol, quercetin, epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), and other polyphenols (Agrawal et al., 2017; Betker et al., 2019; Somiya et al., 2018). Experimental studies consistently demonstrated that milk EV encapsulation minimizes premature degradation, improve aqueous digestion, enhances intestinal absorption through endocytosis and membrane fusion, prolong systemic circulation, thereby augmenting the antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory efficacy of encapsulated nutraceuticals compared with their free counterparts (Aqil et al., 2019; Timofeeva et al., 2023). These attributes position mEVs as an attractive platform for functional foods, precision nutrition, and targeted nutraceutical delivery, with opportunities for engineering surface ligands and cargo combinations to achieve tissue-specific delivery and personalized therapeutic interventions.

Despite these promising advances, several scientific and translational challenges must be critically considered before mEVs can be widely adopted as drug or nutraceutical delivery systems. First, drug-loading efficiency remains highly variable and depends on the physicochemical properties of the cargo and the loading strategy, with many approaches compromising EV membrane integrity or resulting in inconsistent encapsulation efficiency (Benedikter et al., 2017; Görgens et al., 2022). Second, the intrinsic heterogeneity of milk EV populations, influenced by animal species, lactation stage, diet, and isolation methodology, creates substantial batch-to-batch variability that complicates reproducibility, quality control, and therapeutic standardization (Lener et al., 2015; Reiner et al., 2017). Furthermore, scalable Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)-compliant production, purification, storage stability, and cost-effective manufacturing remain major bottlenecks for clinical translation, while incomplete understanding of pharmacokinetics, biodistribution, cargo release kinetics, long-term safety, immunogenicity, and potential off-target effects necessitates further investigation. Equally important, regulatory frameworks remain underdeveloped, with uncertainty regarding whether milk EV-based formulations should be regulated as biological therapeutics, nanomedicines, food ingredients, or combination products, thereby complicating product approval and commercialization.

Milk EVs for the delivery of therapeutics

Milk-derived EVs have emerged as highly promising natural nanocarriers for the oral delivery of diverse therapeutic agents owing to their inherent biocompatibility, gastrointestinal stability, low immunogenicity, and scalability. Their sphingomyelin- and cholesterol-rich lipid bilayer confers remarkable resistance to gastric acids and digestive enzymes, thereby maintaining structural integrity during gastrointestinal transit and facilitating effective mucosal uptake via surface proteins such as integrins. MEVs have been successfully engineered to encapsulate small molecule chemotherapeutics including paclitaxel and doxorubicin as well as natural anti-inflammatory compounds like curcumin and withaferin A, improving drug stability, reducing systemic and hepatorenal toxicity, and enhancing anticancer efficacy in xenograft models after oral administration (Agrawal et al., 2017; Betker et al., 2019). Furthermore, their innate anti-inflammatory properties, attributed to endogenous cargo, can be augmented by loading with anti-inflammatory drugs, offering a novel strategy for treating inflammatory bowel disease (Kandimalla et al., 2021; Rehman et al., 2025). In the realm of nucleic acid delivery, mEVs protect their payload from degradation and facilitate cellular uptake. Studies have successfully leveraged them for the delivery of siRNA and miRNA to achieve targeted gene silencing, such as knocking down oncogenes in vitro, and for transporting mRNA, showcasing their potential for protein replacement therapy (Aqil et al., 2019; Munir et al., 2020). Notably, their application extends to the delivery of complex biologics; mEVs can be loaded with exogenous bioactive peptides and therapeutic proteins (catalase), and their own native protein cargo, including milk proteins like β-lactoglobulin, can exert intended biological effects, such as immunomodulation (Reinhardt et al., 2013; Somiya et al., 2018). The ability to engineer mEVs through surface modification for active targeting further amplifies their utility, positioning them as a versatile, scalable, and efficient vehicle for next-generation nanomedicines.

Anti-inflammatory potentials of milk derived EVs

Natural bioactive compounds exert potent anti-inflammatory effects by modulating key molecular targets, such as NF-κB, MAPKs, and pro-inflammatory cytokines, thereby offering promising therapeutic avenues for managing inflammatory processes (Nabi et al. 2020a; Safdar et al. 2024). Milk derived exosomes and other EVs exert potent anti-inflammatory effects trough precisely defined molecular mechanism, beyond their broad or nonspecific immunomodulation (Arain et al. 2023a, 2025a). Their regulatory microRNAs, particularly miR-148a, miR-30a, miR-21, and miR-146b, target specific node of the NF-κB signaling axis (Husseini & Gilbert, 2025; Wang et al., 2026). For example miR-148a directly binds the 3′UTR of IKKβ, attenuating its translation and thereby reducing p65 phosphorylation and subsequent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6 (Patel et al., 2015). Similarly, miR-21 suppresses PDCD4, an upstream activator of NF-κB, while miR-30a modulates MyD88-dependent TLR signaling, collectively dampening inflammatory gene expression. Beyond miRNA-mediated repression, bovine mEVs regulate redox balance by inhibiting SOD1/SOD2-driven oxidative stress pathways, shifting macrophages toward an M2 anti-inflammatory phenotype and reducing LPS-induced damage (Arain et al. 2024a; Li et al. 2021). In intestinal epithelial model, mEVs reinforce mucosal integrity by upregulating tight-junction proteins (ZO-1, occludin, claudin-1) and suppressing the IKK–NF-κB axis, thereby decreasing epithelial permeability and inflammatory signaling (Zhang et al., 2023). These mechanistic findings align with in vivo evidence; orally administered EVs in murine colitis models reduce histopathological lesions and suppress colonic pro-inflammatory mediators, these effect facilitated by the structural stability of EVs as they pass through the gastrointestinal tract (Gao et al. 2024a; Hassan et al. 2023). Comparative analysis indicate that EVs from cow, goat, and donkey milk provide similar immunoregulatory outcomes, with donkey mEVs showing pronounced suppression of TNF-α, IL-6, and IL-1β at physiologically relevant concentrations (≈ 10 µg/mL), partly through modulation of enzymes involved in arachidonic acid metabolism (Mecocci et al., 2020). These results reveal a unified mechanistic framework: mEVs mitigate inflammation through coordinated regulation of molecular pathways (NF-κB, TLRs, NLRP3 inflammasome), and metabolic regulators.

Beyond the GIT, mEVs demonstrate tissue specific immunomodulatory properties that are increasingly well documented yet accompanied by areas of scientific uncertainty. In osteoarthritic chondrocytes, bovine EVs downregulate matrix-degrading enzymes (MMP-1, ADAMTS5) and suppress sGAG release via miR-148a-dependent repression of catabolic mediators and EV-associated TGF-β–driven anabolic signaling (Nawaz et al., 2018; Pieters et al., 2022). Goat mEVs complement these effects by downregulating IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and TLR2/4 expression while enhancing IL-10, highlighting their capacity to modulate both innate and adaptive immune responses.(De Ciucis et al., 2023) In neonates, human mEVs particularly from mother reduce inflammasome activity and pyroptosis, largely mediated by miR-146b targeting NLRP3 and ASC, thereby contributing to immune protection early in life (Vahkal et al., 2024).

However, evidence concerning the neuroactive effects of mEVs remains inconsistent. While several rodent studies suggest improved cognitive performance, (Zhou et al. 2022a) synaptic plasticity, (Vinuesa et al., 2019) and microglial quiescence, (Xiong et al., 2025) others report minimal or even contradictory outcomes. These discrepancies likely stem from divergences in EV dosage, species-specific miRNA composition, delivery route, and reliance on in vitro or rodent models that may not fully replicate human neurobiology. To address these uncertainties, future research should standardize dosing frameworks, conduct cross-species miRNA and proteomic comparisons, and employ human-relevant systems such as cerebral organoids or brain-on-chip platforms. Overall, the current evidence demonstrates that mEVs exert potent anti-inflammatory effects through highly specific, multi-targeted molecular pathways.

Antioxidant potentials of milk derived EVs

Oxidative stress, characterized by an imbalance between reactive oxygen species (ROS) production and endogenous antioxidant defenses, which plays a pivotal role in the onset and progression of cardiovascular, metabolic, and neurodegenerative disorders (Arain et al. 2018; Nabi et al. 2020b). In this context, mEVs have emerged as promising anti-oxidative agents due to their rich enzymatic cargo and finely coordinated molecular actions. Proteomic analyses consistently identify catalase (CAT), glutathione peroxidase (GPx), peroxiredoxins, and several superoxide dismutase isoforms within mEVs, notably SOD1 (cytosolic Cu/Zn-SOD) and SOD2 (mitochondrial Mn-SOD) (Hansen & Rasmussen, 2021; van Herwijnen et al., 2016). These isoforms directly neutralize superoxide radicals (O₂•⁻), preventing their conversion into highly reactive hydroxyl species and limiting downstream lipid peroxidation (Lässer et al., 2017; Rani et al., 2016; Yamada et al., 2012). Complementing this enzymatic machinery, mEVs also transport redox-regulatory microRNAs including miR-200c and miR-29b, which modulate the Nrf2–Keap1 pathway, a master regulator of antioxidant gene expression such as SODs, heme oxygenase-1, and additional phase-II detoxification enzymes (Benmoussa et al. 2016; Gao et al. 2024b). The structural stability conferred by the EV phospholipid bilayer protects these biomolecules from enzymatic degradation during digestion and facilitates targeted uptake via receptor-mediated endocytosis or membrane fusion, resulting in far greater bioavailability than free antioxidant compounds (Betker et al., 2019). Consistent with these mechanistic features, in vitro studies demonstrate that mEVs suppress intracellular ROS accumulation, reduce malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, and upregulate endogenous SOD and GPx activities in oxidatively challenged epithelial cells (Gao et al. 2024b; Liu et al. 2024).

However, despite potent antioxidant potential, conflicting data persist particularly regarding their effects in the brain. Some rodent studies report pronounced neuroprotective benefits, including attenuation of mitochondrial ROS and suppression of neuroinflammatory signaling, whereas others show minimal or contradictory outcomes (Melnik, 2021). These inconsistencies likely reflect heterogeneity in EV dosage, species of origin (bovine vs. human), developmental stage of recipients (neonatal vs. adult), and methodological variations such as isolation technique or thermal processing, which can alter EV stability and cargo integrity. These findings highlight the need for rigorous standardization in experimental design and deeper mechanistic profiling. In particular, distinguishing the tissue-specific actions of EV subpopulations enriched in SOD1 versus SOD2, or those carrying distinct redox-modulating miRNAs, will be critical for determining whether observed outcomes are cargo-dependent, dose-dependent, or context-dependent. Future studies should prioritize cross-species comparative models, systematic dose response analyses, and single-vesicle multi-omics to clarify the precise antioxidant signatures of mEVs and resolve contradictory evidence in complex organ systems such as the brain.

Immunomodulatory potential of milk derived EVs

The immunogenicity of exogenous EVs is primarily influenced by their parental cell source and molecular composition. Compared with autologous EVs, allogeneic or xenogeneic vesicles exhibit a higher likelihood of immune recognition and clearance by innate immune cells, particularly mononuclear phagocytes and may also trigger adaptive immune responses following repeated administration. This enhanced immunogenicity is partly attributed to the enrichment of major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules on the EV surface, which can present antigens and activate T cell-mediated pathways, thereby amplifying host immune reactivity (Bai et al. 2025a; Malsy et al. 2019).

Milk-derived EVs comprising exosomes and microvesicles enriched in immunoglobulins, TGF-β, lactadherin, lipids, mRNAs, and miRNAs, display exceptional resistance to gastrointestinal degradation, enabling systemic bioactivity following oral intake (Bai et al. 2025b). Mechanistic studies demonstrate that mEVs promote regulatory T-cell differentiation via TGF-β signaling and miR-148a mediated suppression of dendritic cell maturation through DNMT1 downregulation (Mecocci et al., 2021). In parallel, mEVs modulate innate immune responses by skewing macrophages toward an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype and suppressing NF-κB activation in epithelial cells, resulting in reduced secretion of IL-8, TNF-α, and other pro-inflammatory mediators (Ascanius et al., 2021). Nonetheless, their immunomodulatory effects are context-dependent; for example, miR-30a within mEVs can enhance NLRP3 inflammasome activity and antimicrobial defense, while EV-associated proteins contribute to epithelial barrier support by reinforcing tight-junction integrity (Zhang et al. 2024a).

Despite these beneficial properties, a comprehensive evaluation of the long-term safety and immunogenicity of mEVs remains limited. Although short-term studies generally report low toxicity and minimal immune activation, chronic administration has been associated with subtle yet measurable effects, including altered hepatic oxidative stress markers, shifts in renal clearance efficiency, and mild splenic immune stimulation (Abd El-Hack et al., 2026; Bahr et al., 2020; Nair et al., 2023). These findings highlight the need for detailed toxicological assessments incorporating longitudinal organ-function testing, hematological profiling, oxidative status evaluation, and histopathology to detect cumulative or delayed effects that may not be evident in acute studies. Another critical consideration is the potential for allergenic reactions. Milk EV preparations may co-isolate residual milk proteins particularly caseins, β-lactoglobulin, and other whey proteins which remain potent allergens for sensitized individuals. Reports of IgE-mediated mast-cell activation following exposure to insufficiently purified bovine mEVs underscore this risk (Cho et al., 2022). To ensure safety, purification workflows should incorporate advanced methods such as density-gradient ultracentrifugation, size-exclusion chromatography, and protease-based removal of surface contaminants. Additional strategies including enzymatic degradation of allergenic proteins or engineering low-allergen EV preparations may further minimize hypersensitivity potential. Overall, integrating mechanistic immunomodulatory insights with rigorous long-term toxicity, immunogenicity, and allergenicity evaluation is essential to support the responsible translation of mEVs as therapeutic agents, vaccine adjuvants, or nutraceutical nanocarriers.

Anti-cancer potential of milk derived EVs

The burgeoning field of extracellular vesicle research has unveiled the significant anti-cancer potential of mEVs, positioning them as novel biocompatible agents for cancer prevention and therapy. The biological cargo of these mEVs is notably rich in immunomodulatory and anti-proliferative molecules, including miR-148a-3p, miR-155, let-7 family miRNAs, and proteins like TGF-β and lactadherin (Sanwlani et al., 2020). A primary anti-cancer mechanism involves the oral administration of mEVs, which surprisingly survive gastrointestinal digestion and are absorbed into the systemic circulation, facilitating the direct delivery of their bioactive cargo to tissues (Askenase, 2021; Babaker et al., 2022). Once internalized by recipient cancer cells, mEVs miRNAs can downregulate key oncogenic pathways; for instance, miR-148a-3p directly targets and suppresses DNA methyltransferase 1 (DNMT1), leading to hypomethylation and reactivation of tumor suppressor genes, thereby inhibiting proliferation in various cancer cell lines (Melnik et al., 2023). Such EVs have been effectively loaded with a spectrum of anti-neoplastic agents including curcumin, celastrol, paclitaxel, doxorubicin, anthocyanidins, and resveratrol to enhance drug stability, improve intestinal permeability, reduce systemic toxicity, and augment tumor suppression in both in vitro cancer cell lines and in vivo xenograft and mouse models (Pullan et al., 2022; Timofeeva et al., 2023). Furthermore, mEVs exhibit potent immunostimulatory effects by promoting the differentiation of regulatory T-cells and modulating dendritic cell maturation, which can enhance anti-tumor immunity and counteract the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (Aiello et al., 2017). Beyond their endogenous cargo, mEVs inherent stability, low immunogenicity, and tropism for specific tissues make them exceptional natural delivery vehicles for chemotherapeutic drugs (paclitaxel, doxorubicin) or synthetic therapeutic nucleic acids (siRNAs), improving drug bioavailability and reducing off-target toxicity compared to synthetic liposomes (Timofeeva et al., 2023). Consequently, mEVs represent a compelling and sustainable frontier in oncotherapy, functioning both as intrinsic anti-cancer biotherapeutics and as engineered nanocarriers for targeted drug delivery.

Clinical applications of milk derived EVs

Milk-derived EVs are gaining attention as promising biocompatible platforms for clinical applications against a wide range of pathophysiological conditions (Fig. 3). Their natural stability, excellent biocompatibility, and capacity to cross biological barriers make them highly effective carriers for therapeutic drugs and nucleic acids. In addition, the intrinsic cargo of proteins, lipids, and miRNAs provides them with inherent immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and regenerative properties, underscoring their potential for treating diverse health disorders (Fig. 3).

Effects on brain health

Milk-derived EVs, have emerged as promising bioactive nanostructures capable of influencing brain health through neuroprotection, epigenetic regulation, and modulation of neuronal plasticity. In murine model, orally administered mEVs (2.85 × 10¹¹ particles/day), successfully crossed the blood-brain barrier (BBB), increased hippocampal dendritic complexity and improved both cognitive and motor functions, demonstrating their neuroprotective potential (Zhou et al. 2022a). Similarly, bovine milk exosomes at administered orally at 2 µg RNA equivalents/day also crossed the BBB and promoted hippocampal dendritic development, further supporting their role in neurodevelopment and neuronal function (Zhou et al., 2021).

Beyond structural and functional benefits, m EVs also exert epigenetic effects on resident immune cells of the central nervous system. Human microglial cells effectively internalized mEVs (100 µg/mL), resulting in altered expression of DNA methylation regulators, suggesting that these vesicles may modulate microglial phenotype through epigenetic reprograming (Wijenayake et al., 2024). Consistent with these findings human mEVs (10 µg/mL) improved neuronal survival and attenuated oxidative stress in in vitro models of premature encephalopathy, in a dose-dependent manner, further highlighting their therapeutic potential against neurodevelopmental injury (Anbeek, 2025).

Despite these promising findings emerging evidence indicates that the neurological effects of mEVs may depend on the metabolic context. It was reported that milk exosomes may synergize with experimentally elevated dietary galactose intake (0.4–0.5 g/kg body weight/day) to promote α-synuclein aggregation and accelerate pathogenic mechanism associated with Parkinson’s disease and type-2 diabetes mellitus (Melnik, 2021). Importantly, this hypothesis is based on conditions involving excessive galactose exposure and should not be interpreted as evidence that physiological galactose intake from a normal balanced diet or normal milk consumption exerts similar adverse effects. Instead, the proposed mechanism likely reflects a pathological interaction under conditions of metabolic overload, highlighting the importance of dietary context when evaluating the biological actions of mEVs. Supporting this context-dependent interpretation, supplementation with a milk fat globule membrane/EV-rich preparation (400 mg/kg/day) in aged rats produced only modest alteration in the brain lipidome and cognitive functions indicating that age-related neurological changes induced potent effects than EVs supplementation alone (Sprenger et al., 2025). Moreover, mEVs may also interact with other dietary factors that influence brain health. Early-life exposure to a high-fat diet (45% kcal from fat) impaired hippocampal synaptic plasticity by reducing long-term potentiation and dendritic spine density through microglia-derived exosome-like vesicles carrying pro-inflammatory cargo, thereby promoting neuroinflammation and cognitive dysfunction (Vinuesa et al., 2019). Overall, current evidence suggests that mEVs predominantly exhibit neuroprotective and neuromodulatory properties, whereas potential adverse outcomes appear to arise primarily under pathological metabolic conditions.

Effects on cardiac health

Bovine mEVs, administered orally at a dose of 1 × 10¹¹ particles/day, have been shown to exert cardioprotective effects by modulating endothelial function and reducing vascular inflammation, primarily through their microRNA cargo that regulates gene networks associated with cardiovascular health (Bedoya, 2020). Oral administration of mEVs at a dose of 1.2 × 10¹⁰ particles/day significantly alleviated heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) in mice, with therapeutic benefits linked to modulation of the gut microbiota, improved cardiac function, and reduced pathological remodeling (Zhang et al. 2024b). Furthermore, human mEV administered at 1 × 10⁹ particles/mL, were found to attenuate endothelial dysfunction in vitro by restoring nitric oxide bioavailability and reducing oxidative stress, highlighting their potential as natural interventions against vascular dysfunction (Cho et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2026). In addition to EVs, milk-derived bioactive peptides particularly those derived from casein and whey proteins have demonstrated antihypertensive effects via angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibition (Ashraf et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2025a). Clinical studies reported that daily supplementation with 3–52 mg/day of these peptides effectively reduced blood pressure in hypertensive patients (Phelan & Kerins, 2011). Similarly, oral administration of milk-derived nanovesicles loaded with Connexin 43 peptides at a dose of 1 × 10¹¹ particles/day effectively targeted ischemic myocardium, reducing infarct size and improving cardiac function in ischemia reperfusion injury models (Marsh et al., 2025).

Extracellular vesicles in general, particularly exosomes, have been reported to provide cardioprotection by reducing apoptosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation, while enhancing angiogenesis and tissue repair in cardiovascular diseases (Zhang et al. 2024c). These effects appear dose-dependent, with optimal cardioprotection observed at 10–100 µg/mL of exosomal protein in experimental models (Rezaie et al., 2019). Furthermore, IMTP-modified milk exosomes loaded with miR-146a (5 mg/kg, intravenous) significantly attenuated myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury in mice by inhibiting the NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby reducing inflammation and myocardial damage (Meng et al., 2024). Collectively, evidence indicates that mEVs and bioactive peptides exert dose-dependent cardioprotective effects by modulating endothelial function, gut microbiota, oxidative stress, inflammation, and signaling pathways, thereby improving cardiac performance and mitigating vascular dysfunction (Wang et al. 2025b).

Effects on kidney health

Research on the role of mEVs in kidney health is still limited, highlighting the need for further investigation to clarify their therapeutic potential. Recent studies, however, provide promising insights. For instance, IMTP-modified milk exosomes loaded with miR-146a (20 mg/kg, intraperitoneally) were shown to significantly alleviate myocardial ischemia reperfusion injury in mice by suppressing NF-κB signaling, thereby reducing inflammation and tissue damage (Meng et al., 2024). Likewise, oral administration of mEVs (1 × 10¹¹ particles/day) supported renal development in pediatric models by modulating nephron maturation and enhancing intercellular communication, suggesting potential benefits in pediatric kidney diseases through anti-inflammatory and regenerative pathways (Ergunay et al., 2024). In a related approach, administration of human renal tubular extracellular microvesicles (3 × 10¹¹ particles/rat, intravenously) reversed kidney ischemia reperfusion injury, improved renal function, reduced tubular damage, and promoted tissue repair, underscoring their therapeutic potential in acute kidney injury (Dominguez et al., 2018). Moreover, exosome-mediated delivery of the super-repressor IκBα (1 × 10¹⁰ particles/mouse) significantly attenuated kidney ischemia reperfusion injury in mice by inhibiting NF-κB activation, thereby decreasing tubular damage, apoptosis, and renal dysfunction (Kim et al., 2021). Emerging evidence suggests that while research on mEVs in kidney health remains limited, their anti-inflammatory, regenerative, and protective properties demonstrated across various models highlight their promising therapeutic potential in renal development and kidney injury.

Effects on pancreatic health

Research on the protective effects of mEVs on pancreatic health and diabetes mellitus remains limited. It was reported that milk exosomal miRNAs, particularly miR-29b and miR-21, have been reported to act as epigenetic regulators that suppress AMPK signaling and activate mTORC1, thereby promoting β-cell de-differentiation in type-2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM). Prolong intake of 0.5–1.0 L of cow’s milk daily may provide biologically relevant doses of exosomal miRNAs sufficient to influence these metabolic pathways (Melnik 2019b). Similarly, persistent milk consumption, due to bioactive molecules such as exosomal microRNAs and branched-chain amino acids, may contribute to T2DM pathogenesis by driving mTORC1 over-activation and β-cell dysfunction. Notably, daily intake exceeding 1–2 glasses of milk has been associated with increased risk factors for insulin resistance and T2DM development (Melnik 2019a). Beyond host-derived EVs, gut microbiota derived EVs also play a pivotal role in metabolic disorders. They influence host metabolism, inflammation, and insulin sensitivity, contributing to obesity and diabetes. Dietary interventions, including bioactive compounds, probiotics, and prebiotics, can beneficially modulate the release and composition of microbial EVs, thereby offering therapeutic potential against these disorders (Diez-Sainz et al., 2022; Yu et al., 2026).

Recent advances highlight milk exosomes as effective nanocarriers for antidiabetic therapeutics. Oral delivery of insulin-loaded milk exosomes at a dose of 50 IU/kg significantly reduced blood glucose levels in vivo by protecting insulin from enzymatic degradation, suggesting a promising non-invasive strategy for diabetes management (Dehghani et al., 2025). Similarly, liraglutide-loaded milk exosomes administered sublingually at 3 mg/kg demonstrated significant hypoglycemic effects in diabetic mice, enhancing the therapeutic efficacy of liraglutide (Xu et al., 2022). Furthermore, milk-derived exosomes enriched with specific microRNAs have shown potential in preventing T2DM associated with metabolic syndrome. Oral administration of miRNA-loaded exosomes at a dose of 100 µg/day improved insulin sensitivity and metabolic regulation in experimental models (Beuzelin & Kaeffer, 2018). Collectively, mEVs exhibit dual roles in pancreatic health and diabetes by potentially contributing to T2DM pathogenesis through mTORC1 over-activation while also offering promising therapeutic applications as nanocarriers for insulin, liraglutide, and miRNA-based interventions to improve insulin sensitivity and metabolic regulation.

Effects on lungs health

Milk-derived EVs have shown promising protective roles in maintaining lung health by modulating diverse molecular mechanisms. In a preclinical study, inhalation of glycyrrhetinic acid (GA) encapsulated in mEVs (GA-MEVs) at a dose of 1 mg/kg significantly alleviated idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in mice by reducing collagen deposition, oxidative stress, and inflammatory responses, with GA-MEVs demonstrating superior therapeutic efficacy and lung-targeted delivery compared to free GA (Ran et al., 2024). Similarly, another study, reported that breast mEVs contain miRNAs whose expression profiles differ markedly in mothers with asthma and atopy, with 59 EV-miRNAs differentially expressed at a false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05, suggesting an epigenetic link between maternal allergic conditions and infant respiratory health (Bozack et al., 2022). In another investigation, oral administration of 0.4 µg/day bovine mEVs for 14 days enhanced pulmonary inflammation and promoted M1 macrophage polarization in mice exposed to agricultural dust, indicating that mEVs may also act as immune modulators amplifying inflammatory responses under environmental stress (Nordgren et al., 2019). Conversely, human mEVs at a concentration of 1 × 10¹⁰ particles/mL preserved bronchial epithelial barrier integrity, promoted epithelial repair, and significantly reduced TLR3-induced pro-inflammatory cytokine release in vitro, underscoring their therapeutic potential in airway inflammatory conditions (Karra et al., 2022).

Moreover, colostrum-derived EVs exhibited strong antiviral activity by inhibiting human rotavirus and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV) infections in vitro, with significant antiviral effects observed at doses of 4–20 µg/mL through disruption of virus-cell interactions (Civra et al., 2021). Supporting the broader role of EVs in respiratory disorders, it was highlighted that EVs derived from airway epithelial cells, immune cells, and allergens play key roles in asthma pathogenesis by modulating immune responses, airway inflammation, and remodeling through the transfer of bioactive molecules such as proteins, lipids, and miRNAs (Alashkar Alhamwe et al., 2021). Finally, recent evidence demonstrated that oral supplementation with GEV Sod2 powder at 1 g/kg effectively protected against PM2.5-induced cardiopulmonary injury in mice by reducing oxidative stress, inflammation, and apoptosis, suggesting the potential of biovesicle-based Sod2 supplementation in mitigating air pollution–related lung damage (Zhang et al. 2025b). In summary, accumulating evidence indicates that mEVs exert multifaceted effects on lung health, functioning as protective, therapeutic, and immunomodulatory agents through antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, antiviral, and epigenetic mechanisms, while also showing potential for targeted delivery and environmental stress mitigation.

Effects on liver health

Milk-derived EVs have shown promising hepatoprotective effects through multiple mechanisms. Oral administration of mEVs at 200 mg/kg/day significantly preserved intestinal barrier integrity and reduced gut permeability by regulating tight junction proteins, which in turn alleviated liver inflammation and injury via the gut liver axis (Mun et al., 2025; Tong et al., 2023). Similarly, human mEVs at 5 mg/kg/day mitigated high-fat-diet-induced non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) in mice by reducing hepatic steatosis, oxidative stress, and inflammation, thereby improving overall liver function (Jiang et al., 2023). Recent study, further reported that mEVs at 100 mg/kg directly targeted hepatocytes to alleviate hepatic metabolic dysfunction by modulating endoplasmic reticulum stress, contributing to improved liver function and metabolic homeostasis. In another study, bovine colostrum-derived EVs (50 mg/kg/day) significantly attenuated non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) through modulation of gut microbiota composition and enhancement of intestinal barrier integrity (Li et al. 2025b; Zhang et al. 2025a).

Beyond metabolic disorders, camel milk-derived EVs (CM-EVs) have also demonstrated therapeutic potential in hepatic malignancies. Co-delivery of siVEGF and sorafenib via CM-EVs (2 mg/kg, i.v.) enhanced anti-hepatocellular carcinoma efficacy by simultaneously suppressing VEGF expression and downstream angiogenic signaling, resulting in superior tumor growth inhibition compared to single-agent therapies (Chen et al., 2025). In addition, CM-EVs at 100 mg/kg protected against hypobaric hypoxia-induced colonic injury in mice by modulating gut microbiota composition and metabolite profiles, underscoring their relevance as functional food components against hypoxia-associated intestinal and hepatic stress (Yang et al., 2025). Oral administration of mEVs at 1 × 10¹¹ particles/day further prevented liver fibrosis progression by inhibiting hepatic stellate cell activation, reducing collagen deposition and fibrotic marker expression (Reif et al., 2022). Moreover, CM-EVs at 50 mg/kg significantly alleviated high-fat-diet-induced obesity by lowering body weight gain, adiposity, and serum lipid levels through enhanced thermogenesis in brown adipose tissue and regulation of hepatic lipid metabolism (Wang et al., 2025c). Overall, mEVs, induce beneficial effects via diverse bioactive mechanisms including modulation of gut liver axis, hepatic metabolism, gut microbiota, angiogenic signaling, and fibrogenesis, exhibit strong hepatoprotective and therapeutic potential against metabolic, inflammatory, fibrotic, hypoxic, and malignant liver disorders.

Effects on bone health

Human breast milk-derived EVs have shown strong osteogenic potential, as treatment with 20 µg/mL significantly promoted osteoblast proliferation and differentiation through activation of BMP2/MAPK signaling pathways, thereby enhancing osteogenic markers and indicating their therapeutic role in bone health (Kwon et al., 2025). Similarly, bovine mEVs have been reported to promote bone regeneration; in vitro, they enhanced osteoblast proliferation and differentiation, while in vivo administration at 1 mg/kg/day in rats stimulated osteogenesis and improved bone health outcomes (Go et al., 2021). Oral supplementation of mEVs at 500 mg/kg/day effectively prevented bone dysfunction and metabolic alterations in obese mice fed a high-refined carbohydrate diet, improving bone microarchitecture, reducing systemic inflammation, and mitigating metabolic disturbances (Silva et al., 2025). In an ovariectomized mouse model, daily supplementation of mEVs at 100 mg/kg/day significantly suppressed osteoclastogenesis and reduced bone loss through modulation of gut microbiota, suggesting potential applications against postmenopausal osteoporosis (Hao et al., 2024). Consistently, oral administration of bovine milk-derived exosomes at 0.2 mg/kg/day improved bone microarchitecture and mineral density in ovariectomized mice, highlighting their potential as a dietary intervention for osteoporosis management (Yun et al., 2020). Another study revealed that milk EV supplementation at 0.2 mg/mouse/day accelerated osteoblast differentiation and bone nodule formation both in vitro and in vivo; however, prolonged use impaired bone matrix mineralization, suggesting a dose- and time-dependent dual role of mEVs in bone physiology (Oliveira et al., 2016). Furthermore, cow mEVs at 200 µg/mL markedly enhanced alkaline phosphatase activity and calcium deposition in MC3T3-E1 cells, confirming their ability to stimulate osteoblast differentiation and mineralization (Kwon et al., 2022). Supporting these findings, a recent meta-analysis demonstrated that food-derived EVs significantly improved bone mineral density, trabecular thickness, and bone strength in osteoporotic animal models, with oral doses between 1 and 100 mg/kg/day showing the most pronounced protective effects against bone loss (Shi et al., 2025). In summary, mEVs, whether from human or bovine sources, exhibit strong osteogenic potential by enhancing osteoblast activity, suppressing osteoclastogenesis, improving bone microarchitecture, and preventing osteoporosis in a dose dependent manner, underscoring their promise as therapeutic and dietary interventions for bone health.

Safety, toxicology, and regulatory aspects of milk-derived EVs

A comprehensive assessment of the safety, toxicology, and regulatory considerations of mEVs is essential to support their growing use as natural nanocarriers in nutraceutical and therapeutics applications. Although their origin from a widely consumed food source suggest an inherently favorable safety profile with animal studies demonstrating excellent biocompatibility, efficient intestinal uptake, and negligible acute toxicity, current evidence remains insufficient to define their long-term safety with confidence (Betker et al., 2019; Zempleni et al., 2017). Notably, concerns persist regarding their immunogenic and allergenic potential, as residual milk proteins (caseins, β-lactoglobulin) or lipids associated components co-isolates with EVs may provoke hypersensitivity reactions in milk-allergic population, necessitating the need for rigorous purification and molecular characterization (de la Torre Gomez et al., 2018; Lässer et al., 2017). These scientific uncertainties are compounded by an evolving and fragmented regulatory landscape, as agencies such as the U.S. FDA and EFSA do not yet provide EV specific guideline; instead milk EV-based products are classified variably as foods, supplements, biologics, or drugs depending on intended use, each requiring extensive validation of identity, purity, stability, safety, and efficacy (Herrmann et al., 2021; Witwer et al., 2019). These regulatory ambiguity, combined with technical challenges such as scaling GMP-compliant isolation, ensuring batch-to-batch reproducibility, and performing long-term toxicological evaluations, represents a major bottleneck to commercialization (Bout & Vromans, 2021; Liao et al., 2023). Collectively, these factors highlight the need for harmonized regulatory standards, improved analytical methods, and systematic toxicological studies to enable safe, reliable, and economically feasible translation of milk EV-based innovations. Future prospects

The future prospects of mEVs depend on resolving key challenges related to standardization, scalability, and translational readiness to harness their therapeutic and nutrigenomic potential. The critical priority is the harmonization of isolation and characterization methodologies, as existing variability across ultracentrifugation, size-exclusion chromatography, and precipitation-based procedures generates EV preparations with inconsistent purity and bioactivity, limiting reproducibility and cross-study comparability. Establishing rigorous quality benchmarks—such as validated EV markers (CD63, CD81, TSG101), the confirmed absence of contaminating milk proteins (e.g., caseins), and standardized quantification using nanoparticle tracking analysis or tunable resistive pulse sensing—is essential to advance the field (Théry et al., 2018; Vaswani et al., 2017). Concurrently, progress in large-scale bioprocessing is equally crucial requiring the development of Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP), cost-effective workflow that maintain vesicle integrity, potentially thorough tangential flow filtration, optimized purification pipelines, and advanced preservation or encapsulation systems (Wu et al., 2023). These innovations will directly support the design of thorough clinical trials evaluating the pharmacokinetics, bio-distribution, immunogenicity, and therapeutic efficacy of mEVs, particularly for oral delivery of nucleic acids, antioxidants, and anti-inflammatory cargo targeting disorders such as inflammatory bowel disease, metabolic dysfunction, and cancer (Samuel et al., 2021). Given their natural biocompatibility, stability, and bioactive molecular profiles, mEVs also hold strong promise for integration into personalized nutrition and nutrigenomics, where selectively sourced or engineered vesicles may deliver tailored miRNA or protein signatures to modulate gene expression according to an individual’s genetic architecture and nutritional demands (Zempleni et al., 2019). Realizing this vision requires a coordinated translational roadmap, linking dairy production system bioprocess engineers, regulatory agencies (FDA/EMA), and clinical researchers to address issues of sustainable sourcing, stringent quality control, regulatory classification (food, supplement, or drug), and human proof-of-concept (de Abreu et al., 2020). Such integrative efforts will be pivotal in transforming mEVs from a rapidly advancing research topic into clinically and commercially viable nano-therapeutics.

Conclusions

In conclusion, mEVs have emerge as a distinctive and highly promising class of natural nanocarriers with substantial potential for nutraceutical and pharmaceutical applications. Current evidence demonstrates that these bilayered vesicles, enriched with parent derived proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids, are orally bioavailable and exert cross-species biological effects including potent immunomodulatory, anti-inflammatory, and anti-cancer activities. Their inherent biocompatibility, gastrointestinal stability, and low immunogenicity offers clear advantages over synthetic nanoparticles particularly for oral drug delivery and mucosal targeting. Importantly, mEVs exhibit a dual functional role as bioactive agent themselves and as efficient delivery vehicle capable of protecting and transporting fragile therapeutic cargos such as siRNA, chemotherapeutics, and probiotic metabolites. While the future of mEVs as next-generation natural nanocarriers is highly promising, their successful clinical translation requires overcoming key obstacles including standardization, isolation protocols, scalable production, comprehensive bio-distribution profiling, and rigorous long-term safety assessments. Addressing these challenges will be critical to fully harnessing the therapeutic and technological potential of mEVs.
