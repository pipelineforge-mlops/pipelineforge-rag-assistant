Background

Many drugs prescribed for individuals with schizophrenia have anticholinergic effects. When these medications are used together, they can create a substantial overall Anticholinergic Burden (ACB).

Objective

The aim of this study was to assess the prevalence of ACB and its predictors among patients with schizophrenia at selected comprehensive specialized hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia.

Design

A multicenter cross sectional study was conducted.

Methods

The study included 406 patients with schizophrenia at selected specialized hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia between June and August 2022. Study participants were recruited using systematic random sampling. The Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden Score (ACBS) was used to assess ACB. Data were entered using EpiData version 4.6.0 and analyzed with SPSS version 26. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed to determine the predictors of high ACB, with variables having a p-value < 0.05 at a 95% confidence interval considered statistically significant.

Results

The prevalence of high ACB was 34.2%. Older patients [Adjusted Odds Ratio (AOR) = 3.26, 95% CI: 1.17, 9.08 P: 0.024], comorbidity [AOR =2.29, 95% CI: 1.24, 4.24, P: 0.008], polypharmacy [AOR = 2.54, 95% CI: 1.35, 4.76, P: 0.004], and Antipsychotic Polypharmacy (APP) [AOR = 2.15, 95% CI: 1.21, 3.82, P: 0.009] were significantly associated with ACB.

Conclusion

In this study, one-third of patients were exposed to high ACB. Old aged patients, those with comorbidity, polypharmacy, and APP need routine follow-up and screening in order to reduce high anticholinergic exposure.

Plain language summary

Many medicines used to treat schizophrenia can affect brain chemicals in a way that causes side effects such as dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, and memory problems. Taking multiple medications simultaneously can amplify their effects. This is called a high Anticholinergic Burden (ACB), meaning the combined impact of the medicines may increase the risk of unwanted side effects. As far as we know, no studies in Ethiopia have specifically looked at ACB or the factors that may increase the risk of high ACB in people living with schizophrenia. The study involved 406 people with schizophrenia who were receiving care at selected specialized hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia. About 34 out of every 100 patients (34.2%) had a high ACB. Older age, having other medical conditions, taking many medications at the same time (polypharmacy), and using more than one antipsychotic medicine together were strongly linked to a higher ACB.

Introduction

Schizophrenia is a chronic mental health condition that impacts about 1% of people worldwide. 1 In addition to the well-known positive and negative symptoms, cognitive impairment has come to light as an increasing concern in people with schizophrenia. 2 Due in large part to the fact that it usually starts in early adulthood and causes long-lasting social and career problems, the illness has a significant financial impact. Additionally, people with schizophrenia typically live 15 years shorter than the general population and have a 5%–10% lifetime risk of suicide, which is associated with a lower life expectancy. 3

Medications having a high Anticholinergic Burden (ACB) are routinely used to address the adverse effects of acute and long-term psychotropic usage, and psychotropic drugs typically used to treat schizophrenia frequently have potent anticholinergic effects. 4 ACB refers to the cumulative exposure of one or more medications that may have anticholinergic effects. Evidence currently available indicates that a higher ACB score or extended use of a particular medication with an anticholinergic effect has been associated with an increased risk of stroke, postoperative delirium, morbidity, mortality, and functional and cognitive impairment. 5

People with schizophrenia are also more susceptible to medical comorbidities, which in turn raise the likelihood that they may be exposed to other drugs that have anticholinergic effects. Furthermore, patients frequently exhibit polypharmacy, and those with persistent psychotic disorders are especially susceptible to prescription cascades, which raise the likelihood and severity of ACB. 4 A high ACB raises serious clinical issues. Recurrent falls, 6 delirium, 7 hospitalization, 8 and rapid cognitive decline and dementia4,9 have all been strongly associated with it. This is especially concerning in people with schizophrenia, who are already at risk for cognitive impairments. 10

To our knowledge, no research in Ethiopia has particularly examined the ACB and its predictors in individuals with schizophrenia. The outcomes will provide vital details to enhance clinical practice, direct deprescribing efforts, and eventually promote better pharmaceutical care for this susceptible group of patients. Therefore, the aim of this study was to assess the prevalence of ACB and its predictors among patients with schizophrenia.

Methods

Study design, period, and setting

A cross sectional study was carried out from June 1 to August 30, 2022, at the University of Gondar Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (UoGCSH), Felege-Hiwot Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (FHCSH), and Tibebe-Ghion Comprehensive Specialized Hospital (TGCSH). The psychiatry department at UoGCSH provides care for individuals with mental health disorders, serving both outpatient and inpatient populations. 11 The FHCSH delivers mental health services to 19,200 clients annually; TGCSH provides mental health care through outpatient departments, emergency, inpatient, and psychotherapy services. According to the monthly report from its psychiatry unit, TGCSH manages 4,864 outpatient clients annually. 12

Population and eligibility criteria

The source population comprised all adult individuals with schizophrenia who had regular follow-up at the outpatient psychiatric clinics of the comprehensive specialized hospitals in Northwest Ethiopia. The study population included all adult patients with schizophrenia attending the outpatient departments of UoGCSH, FHCSH, and TGCSH during the study period. Inclusion criteria were patients aged 18 years and above, those with sufficient insight to respond to oral questions (using the insight assessment tool), 13 and individuals with at least one prior follow-up visit. Patients with incomplete medical records were excluded from the study.

Sample size determination

The sample size was calculated using a single population proportion formula as follows: n=( Zα/2)2×p (1−p)d2

Where n is the desired sample size for a population, Z is the typical normal distribution set at 1.96 (which corresponds to a 95% CI), the P signifies that positive prevalence was utilized in calculating the optimal sample size, and d is the degree of accuracy 0.05 required (a marginal error is 0.05). Since this study was the first of its kind in Ethiopia, a proportion of 50% was used in the sample size calculation. Therefore, n=( 1.96)2×0.5 (1−0.5)0.052=384 . Thus, by adding a 10% non-response rate, the final calculated sample size was 422.

Sampling technique and procedure

The comprehensive specialized hospitals in Northwestern Ethiopia were selected using a random lottery method. The total number of schizophrenia patients on follow-up over a three-month period was obtained from patient registration records to allow proportional allocation of the sample across the study sites. Following this allocation, a systematic random sampling technique was employed to select study participants. The sampling interval (k) was calculated by dividing the total number of schizophrenia patients in the three-month period by the total sample size (2625 ÷ 422 ≈ 6). A random starting point between 1 and 6 was then selected. The number of patients in UoGCSH, FHCSH, and TGCSH was 1100, 1150, and 375, respectively. Accordingly, the sample size was proportionally allocated as 177 for UoGCSH, 185 for FHCSH, and 60 for TGCSH. Participants were then interviewed, and relevant data were concurrently extracted from medical charts for every sixth patient until the required sample size was reached.

Study variables

The main outcome variable was ACB. The independent variables included the sociodemographic characteristics of the participants (i.e., age, marital status, residence, educational level, and health insurance), clinical and medication-related variables (i.e., duration of illness, duration of treatment, number of admissions, number of pervious admissions, comorbidity, polypharmacy, and antipsychotic polypharmacy), and substance use.

Data collection instrument, procedure, and quality control

A structured questionnaire was adopted from previous literature 14 with some modifications for the context of the study participants. The data collection tool had three parts. The first part contains socio-demographic characteristics of the study participants, such as sex, age, marital status, residence, educational level, occupational status, and access to health insurance. The second section consisted of clinical and medication-related characteristics like duration of illness, presence of comorbidity, number of admissions, individual’s medication record, and duration of treatment. The third section consisted of a substance use assessment tool. ASSIST was used to briefly screen the use of psychoactive substances. The tool was developed and validated by WHO. 15

Data were collected by three psychiatric nurses at UoGCSH and FHCSH and two psychiatric nurses at TGCSH. In all study sites, the principal investigator coordinated the provision of all required materials. A pretest was carried out on 22 participants (5% of the calculated sample size) at the outpatient department of Dessie Comprehensive Specialized Hospital to identify potential issues with the data collection instrument and to assess the consistency of the questionnaire. The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines. 16

Operational definitions

Anticholinergic burden

Defined as the cumulative exposure to anticholinergic effects resulting from taking one or more medicines possessing anticholinergic properties.17–19

High and low ACB

Defined as cumulative scores of ≥3 and <3, 20 respectively, as measured by the Anticholinergic Cognitive Burden Score (ACBS). 19

Antipsychotic polypharmacy

Refers to the co-prescription of more than one antipsychotic drug for a patient. Polypharmacy was documented when a particular patient was prescribed more than one antipsychotic drug for a minimum duration of one month or longer.21,22

Current substance use

Using at least one of specific substance (alcohol, khat or cigarettes) for nonmedical purposes within the last 3 months according to the Alcohol, Smoking, and Substance Involvement Screening Tool (ASSIST). 15

Polypharmacy

The World Health Organization (WHO) defines polypharmacy as the consistent use of five or more medications. 23

Co-morbid illness

The existence of one or more medical conditions that frequently occur alongside the primary illness.

Data processing and analysis

The collected data were cleaned, coded, and entered into EpiData version 4.6.0, then analyzed using SPSS version 26. Descriptive statistics, including mean with Standard Deviation (SD), frequencies, and percentages, were used to examine the distribution of the data. Bivariable and multivariable binary logistic regression analyses were utilized to identify factors associated with high ACB. Model fitness was assessed using the Hosmer and Lemeshow test, which yielded a result of 0.729, indicating a good fit. Multicollinearity was evaluated, with the maximum Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) found to be less than 5, within the acceptable range. Odds Ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals were calculated for each variable, along with corresponding p-values, to assess the strength of associations. A p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Factors associated with anticholinergic burden among patients with schizophrenia

In the multivariable analysis, older age, comorbidity, polypharmacy, and APP were identified as predictors of high ACB. Accordingly, older patients were 3.26 times more likely to have a high ACB compared with younger patients (AOR = 3.26, 95% CI: 1.17, 9.08). Patients with comorbid conditions had a 2.29-fold higher likelihood of high ACB than those without comorbidities (AOR = 2.29, 95% CI: 1.24, 4.24). Similarly, individuals exposed to polypharmacy were 2.54 times more likely to experience high ACB compared with those not receiving polypharmacy (AOR = 2.54, 95% CI: 1.35, 4.76). Discussion

Psychotropic medications, particularly antipsychotics, are vital in the treatment of schizophrenia, have significantly improved the lives and outcomes of many individuals with the disorder, and remain a cornerstone of comprehensive management. Aiming at assessing ACB among patients with schizophrenia, the current study revealed that the prevalence of high ACB was 34.2%. Older patients, comorbidity, polypharmacy, and APP were significantly associated with ACB.

In this study, the prevalence of high ACB was 34.2%. This finding is lower than those reported in studies conducted in India (48.8%), 24 Lebanon (51.7%), 25 and France (61.7%). 26 These discrepancies may be attributed to differences in study populations, as the studies from India and Lebanon included older, hospitalized psychiatric patients. Variations in sample size may also partly explain the observed differences. Additionally, differences in clinical settings and prescribing practices may have contributed to the variation in ACB prevalence. These findings highlight the need for routine follow-up and targeted deprescribing interventions to reduce the burden of anticholinergic exposure.

Older patients were more likely to have high ACB compared with younger individuals. This finding aligns with previous evidence. 27 The increased exposure in older adults may be attributed to both patient- and prescribing-related factors, including polypharmacy, multimorbidity, and geriatric syndromes such as frailty, falls, urinary incontinence, and sleep problems, which collectively raise the risk of ACB burden. 28

Participants with comorbid conditions were more likely to have high ACB compared with those without comorbidities. This finding is supported by previous studies.29–31 Evidence from large population-based national registry data in older adults from Denmark and Taiwan also indicates that ACB is associated with an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events, with higher levels of burden corresponding to greater risk in a dose–response relationship.32,33 This is consistent with previous research indicating that anticholinergic medications may contribute to cardiovascular instability through pro-arrhythmic and pro-ischemic effects, as well as causing tachycardia and orthostatic hypotension, all of which can elevate the risk of ischemic stroke and mortality. 34 Therefore, patients with comorbid conditions should receive careful follow-up and medication review to minimize the potential adverse effects of ACB.

Patients on APP were more likely to have high ACB than those not treated with APP. This finding is consistent with previous evidence. 24 The combination of antipsychotics increases the need for anticholinergic medications to manage adverse effects, 35 which may subsequently worsen cognitive function, impair memory, and reduce overall quality of life. This situation can lead to a prescribing cascade, thereby increasing the cumulative medication-related risk—a major concern in the management of schizophrenia. 4

Respondents with polypharmacy were more likely to have high ACB compared with those not on polypharmacy. This finding aligns with results from previous studies.25,31 Medications with anticholinergic properties are especially prevalent among patients receiving multiple drugs, and even agents with minimal anticholinergic activity can cumulatively increase the overall ACB. 36

Limitations

The current study has some limitations. The results of this study are only applicable to the study areas. The assessment of substance use is subject to social desirability bias. Furthermore, due to the inherent limitations of a cross-sectional design, it was unable to determine causal relationships between the dependent and predictor variables.

Conclusion

In this study, around one-third of patients were exposed to a high ACB. Routine follow-up is particularly important for older patients and for those with comorbidities, polypharmacy, and APP. Regular screening for substance use, along with appropriate counseling and cessation support, should be incorporated into clinical practice. Future studies are recommended to explore the causal relationships between ACB and its potential predictor variables.
