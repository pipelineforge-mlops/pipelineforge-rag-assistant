==============================
Microglia-mediated inflammation plays a critical role in secondary brain injury following ischemic stroke. Glycogen synthase kinase-3β (GSK-3β) and hexokinase 2 (HK2) are involved in regulating microglial inflammatory responses and can promote the release of pro-inflammatory factors under ischemic and hypoxic conditions, thereby exacerbating brain damage. Early exercise intervention has been shown to improve post-stroke neuroinflammation, but whether it exerts this effect by modulating GSK-3β/HK2-associated inflammatory signaling in microglia remains unclear. This study aimed to investigate the effects of early exercise on microglial inflammation and the potential involvement of GSK-3β/HK2 signaling. A twenty-eight-day early exercise regimen was applied in a rat model of middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO), and complementary in vitro experiments were conducted using BV2 microglial cells under oxygen-glucose deprivation. Results demonstrated that early exercise significantly improved neurological outcomes and reduced brain tissue damage, accompanied by decreased expression of microglial pro-inflammatory factors (IL-6, TNF-α, CD86, and iNOS) and increased levels of anti-inflammatory factors (IL-10 and Arg1). In vitro, inhibition of GSK-3β in BV2 cells markedly alleviated inflammatory responses under hypoxic conditions. In conclusion, early exercise intervention can attenuate post-stroke neuroinflammation, potentially by modulating GSK-3β/HK2 signaling in microglia to regulate.

1 Introduction

Stroke is currently the second leading cause of death and the leading cause of long-term disability worldwide, with ischemic stroke accounting for approximately 80% of all cases (Yao et al., 2023, Guo et al., 2026). Following ischemic stroke, microglia rapidly respond to cerebral injury and contribute to post-stroke neuroinflammation through dynamic changes in inflammatory signaling and marker expression(Xia et al., 2024). Previous studies have shown that microglia can exhibit heterogeneous inflammatory states characterized by the differential expression of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory mediators, which are closely associated with neuronal apoptosis and secondary brain injury ((Chen et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2019)). Attenuation of excessive microglia-associated inflammatory responses has been shown to reduce neuronal apoptosis and alleviate pathological damage in ischemic brain tissue (Tuo et al., 2022).

Early exercise intervention has been reported to modulate microglia-associated inflammatory responses after ischemic stroke, accompanied by reduced levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6, and increased levels of anti-inflammatory cytokines, including IL-4 and IL-10 ((Li et al., 2022; Ke et al., 2011; Zhang et al., 2024). Together, these inflammation-related alterations are associated with attenuated post-stroke neuroinflammation(Kardam et al., 2024), supporting early exercise intervention as a feasible neuroprotective strategy after ischemic stroke.

Glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK−3β) plays an important role in regulating microglial inflammatory responses. Modulation(Ma et al., 2024) of GSK−3β related signaling pathways, such as AMPK/Akt/GSK−3β (Tang et al., 2024), GSK−3β/NLRP3 (Shaik et al., 2023), and GSK−3β/β-catenin (D. (Yang et al., 2020)), is associated with reduced production of pro-inflammatory factors and attenuation of brain tissue damage. Exercise has been reported to influence (Sanguesa et al., 2022)−3β signaling (X. (Zhao et al., 2025)). Hexokinase 2 (HK2) is closely associated with pro-inflammatory states of microglia (Codocedo et al., 2024) and can also be modulated by exercise (X. (Zhang et al., 2025)). Inhibition of GSK−3β has been reported to attenuate pro-inflammatory microglial responses (Liu et al., 2020). Together, these findings suggest that GSK−3β and HK2 may contribute to the regulation of microglial inflammatory activity, related to the potential mechanisms by which early exercise interventions regulate post-stroke neuroinflammation. Despite these insights, the precise role of the GSK−3β/HK2 axis in mediating the anti-inflammatory effects of early exercise remains to be fully elucidated.

Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate whether early exercise alleviates post-stroke neuroinflammation by modulating GSK−3β/HK2 signaling. We employed a rat model of middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) to assess the effects of a 28-day treadmill exercise regimen and utilized OGD/R-treated BV2 cells for mechanistic validation. Our results demonstrated that early exercise significantly improved neurological outcomes and shifted microglia toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype, an effect that was consistently replicated in vitro through pharmacological inhibition of GSK−3β. These findings clarify the molecular mechanisms of exercise therapy, providing potential targets for clinical post-stroke rehabilitation.

2 Materials and methods

2.1 Animals and grouping

Thirty-six male Sprague-Dawley rats, aged 6–8 weeks and weighing 290–310 g, were obtained from Beijing Huafukang Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (License No. SCXK Beijing 2024–0003). The animals were maintained under a controlled environment (room temperature of 23 ± 2 °C, relative humidity of 50% ± 10%, with a 12-hour light/dark cycle) and had free access to food and water. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals (NIH Publication No. 85–23, revised 1996) and were approved by the Ethics Committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (SYXK 2020–0010).

The rats were randomly assigned to three groups using a random number table: the sham-operated group (SHAM), the post-stroke exercise intervention group (EX-MCAO), and the post-stroke sedentary intervention group (SED-MCAO), with twelve rats in each group. Randomization and allocation were conducted under a blinded protocol, ensuring that experimenters, participants, and data analysts were unaware of group assignments throughout the study.

2.2 Middle cerebral artery occlusion model

The left middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) model ((Li et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021)) was induced in the three groups of rats using a modified Longa wire embolization method. Rats were placed in a transparent induction chamber and anesthetized with 5% (v/v) isoflurane in 100% oxygen at a total flow rate of 1 L/min. Once the righting reflex was lost, animals were transferred to a face-mask system on a heating pad (37 ± 0.5 °C), and the isoflurane concentration was immediately reduced to 2%. During surgery, the isoflurane concentration was adjusted between 1.5% and 2.5% according to the toe-pinch reflex and respiratory rate to maintain an adequate surgical plane of anesthesia. After discontinuation of anesthesia, the circuit was (C. (Li et al., 2022)) flushed with 100% oxygen for 1 min, and rats resumed spontaneous movement within 2–3 min (Ma et al., 2024). Following stroke induction, soft food and supplemental hydration were provided, with body weight and dehydration status closely monitored. Longa scoring was performed 24 h after surgery, and rats with scores ranging from 1 to 3 were selected for the experiment. For the SHAM group, all procedures were performed except for the insertion of the wire bolus.

2.3 Early exercise intervention

Based on our team’s previous research ((Li et al., 2022; Li et al., 2021)), the following exercise intervention was implemented. All rats underwent 3 days of pre-intervention treadmill training at a speed of 5 m/min to acclimate them to the treadmill, followed by randomization into groups. Beginning 24 h after MCAO, rats in the EX-MCAO group were placed on a small-animal treadmill (Model ZS-PT, Beijing Zongshi DiChuang Company, Beijing) and exercised at a speed of 12 m/min for 30 min per session, five sessions per week, for a total of 4 weeks. To minimize emotional stress and avoid confounding neuroinflammatory responses, no electrical shock was utilized in this study. Instead, rats were motivated to maintain the pace through light touching of the base of the tail. Rats in the SED-MCAO group were placed on the stationary treadmill for an equivalent period.

2.4 Weight and behavioral testing

Body weight and neurobehavioral assessments were performed on days 1, 3, 7, 14, 21, and 28 after MCAO. Neurological function and motor balance were evaluated using the modified neurological severity score (mNSS) (D. (Li et al., 2017)) and the beam walking test (Sawers and Hafner, 2018), with higher scores indicating greater neurological impairment.

2.5 Infarct area

After four weeks of exercise, rats underwent MRI scanning (3 T, MR750, General Electric, USA). Infarct areas were measured on T2-SPACE images by calculating the area of each slice and multiplying it by the slice thickness (2 mm) (Pei et al., 2013) to determine the infarct volume for each animal. The infarct volume ratio was calculated using the formula: (total volume of the contralateral hemisphere -non-infarct volume of the ipsilateral hemisphere) / total volume of the contralateral hemisphere.

2.6 Microglial cell culture

BV2 cells were seeded at a density of 1–5 × 10⁴cells/cm², and experiments were performed when the cells reached 70–80% confluence. The normal oxygen concentration was maintained at 21%. To simulate stroke, cells were exposed to 1% O₂in a hypoxic chamber. BV2 cells were cultured in Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (HyClone) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco) in a humidified incubator with 5% CO2 at 37°C. For the hypoxia treatment, the culture medium was replaced with serum-free DMEM (H. (Hu et al., 2023)). Cell viability and health status were assessed by microscopic examination of morphological integrity and adhesion, together with the recording of seeding density, time required to reach the target density at each passage, and CCK−8 assay.

2.7 GSK−3β inhibitor treatment of microglia

BV2 microglia were cultured in non-glucose DMEM and subjected to oxygen-glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R) in a hypoxia chamber. Cells were divided into three groups: Control, OGD/R, and OGD/R + GSK−3β inhibitor. For the experimental group, cells were treated with 2 µM of the GSK−3β inhibitor laduviglusib (CHIR−99021; MedChemExpress, MCE) once daily for three consecutive days during the OGD/R period (Naujok et al., 2014). Control and OGD/R groups were cultured under the same conditions in basic medium without the inhibitor, with an equivalent volume of DMSO added to match the experimental group.

2.8 Western blotting

Peri-infarct brain tissue was collected from the rats and homogenized in radioimmunoprecipitation assay (RIPA) buffer supplemented with phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride (PMSF; 100:1) (Solarbio, China) on ice for 30 min. Protein concentrations were determined using a BCA kit. Samples were fully denatured by boiling for 10 min, separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE), and transferred onto 0.22 µm polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% BSA for 1.5 h at room temperature, followed by incubation with primary antibodies at 4°C overnight: P-GSK−3β (Tyr216, 1:1500; MCE, YA194, USA), GSK−3β (1:1500; SAB, 21002, USA), HK2 (1:1500; SAB, 32115, USA), β-tubulin (1:1500; SAB, 52905, USA), CD86 (1:1500; SAB, 32223, USA), IL-6 (1:1500; Abcam, ab9324, Cambridge, UK), TNF-α (1:1500; SAB, 41504, USA), CD206 (1:1500; SAB, 42004, USA), IL-10 (1:1500; SAB, 38392, USA), and Arg1 (1:1500; SAB, 58584, USA). After washing, membranes were incubated with anti-rabbit horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody or anti-mouse horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (1:10,000; SAB, L3012 and L3032, USA) for 1 h at 37°C. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence (ECL) solution (Millipore, Massachusetts, WBKLS0500, USA) and imaged with a gel documentation system. Protein expression levels were normalized to the corresponding housekeeping protein. Band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

2.9 Immunofluorescence staining

Brain sections were prepared at a thickness of 10 μm. Brain tissue apoptosis was assessed using a TUNEL staining kit (Servicebio, Wuhan, G1502, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Frozen brain sections were incubated with proteinase K for 15 min at room temperature, then washed three times with PBS. Sections were subsequently incubated with TUNEL staining reagent for 1 h at 37°C and following counterstaining with DAPI (1 μg/mL, Sigma-Aldrich, D9542, USA) for 10 min at room temperature, sections were washed three times with PBS and mounted using antifade mounting medium. imaged using an Olympus IX73 inverted fluorescence microscope. Scanning was performed with a step size of 1.0 μm using a 20 × objective, covering a Z-stack thickness of 10–20 μm. For cell counting, DAPI-positive nuclei were regarded as the total cell population, and only cells with distinct nuclear DAPI staining were included for quantification. Approximately 200 cells were present in each field of view. Cells exhibiting overlap between DAPI (blue) and the target marker signals were considered double-positive. The percentage of double-positive cells was calculated as the number of colocalized cells divided by the total number of DAPI-positive nuclei in the same field of view. For each animal, three independent sections were selected, and at least three random fields per section were analyzed. The proportion of apoptotic cells was quantified by calculating the ratio of TUNEL-positive cells to DAPI-stained nuclei using ImageJ software. The striatum was the primary region of interest for imaging, under low magnification, the striatum was identified based on consistent anatomical landmarks, including the anterior commissure, corpus callosum, and lateral angle of the lateral ventricle. Subsequently, high-magnification imaging fields were uniformly restricted to a fixed relative position within the dorsolateral striatum. Cell counting was performed using ImageJ software, A total of approximately 200 cells were analyzed.

Frozen brain sections were permeabilized and blocked with 5% goat serum(Abcam, ab7481, Cambridge, UK) and 0.3% Triton X−100(Sigma-Aldrich, T9284) in PBS for 1 h at room temperature. Sections were then incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against iNOS (1:50, Abcam, ab15323, Cambridge, UK) and CD206 (1:100, Bio-Rad, catalog no. MCA2235GA, UK). After washing, sections were incubated with the appropriate fluorescent secondary antibodies AF488-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (SAB, L3016, Nanjing, China) and AF594-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (SAB, L3017, Nanjing, China) were applied at 1:500. for 1 h at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, and images were acquired using a Zeiss LSM880 confocal microscope. Fluorescence intensity and cell counts were analyzed using ImageJ software.

2.10 Statistical analysis

Data are presented as mean ± SD. Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. For comparisons among multiple groups, one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed. When variances were homogeneous, Bonferroni’s post hoc test was applied. When variances were unequal, Welch’s ANOVA followed by the Games-Howell post hoc test was used. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05, P < 0.01, and P < 0.001.

4 Discussion

In this study, we found that early exercise intervention ameliorated neuroinflammation after ischemic stroke in rats. Exercise reduced the expression of pro-inflammatory microglial markers such as CD86, iNOS, TNF-α, and IL-6, while increasing anti-inflammatory markers including CD206, IL-10, and Arg1, suggesting that the anti-inflammatory effects of early exercise on microglia are associated with alterations in GSK−3β/HK2-related signaling.

The traditional M1/M2 classification oversimplifies the continuous functional spectrum of microglia, compressing complex cellular functions into a binary opposition(Jin et al., 2023). However, microglial activation is in fact a dynamic continuum. Studies have shown that the transcriptional states of microglia exhibit high heterogeneity(Hasel et al., 2023), with various intermediate phenotypes existing between M1 and M2((Deczkowska et al., 2018; Yin et al., 2023)). Therefore, our study selected the expression levels of pro-inflammatory and anti-inflammatory factors to illustrate the effects of exercise intervention on neuroinflammation induced by microglia.

Glycogen synthase kinase−3β (GSK−3β) is a key regulator of microglial inflammatory responses (Zheng et al., 2017). Phosphorylation of GSK−3β at Tyr216 has been associated with enhanced pro-inflammatory activity, whereas modulation of this phosphorylation can reduce inflammatory signaling ((Krishnankutty et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2020)). In our study, early exercise decreased Tyr216 phosphorylation of GSK−3β, which coincided with reduced pro-inflammatory marker expression in microglia. Hexokinase 2 (HK2), a key enzyme involved in glucose metabolism, is closely associated with microglial pro-inflammatory states ((Fang et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2022)). Increased HK2 expression has been linked to enhanced production of pro-inflammatory cytokines and promotion of a pro-inflammatory microglial phenotype, while reduced HK2 expression can attenuate these responses ((Li et al., 2018)). The results of our study suggest that exercise-mediated changes in GSK−3β phosphorylation are accompanied by reduced HK2 expression in microglia, which together contribute to suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production and enhancement of anti-inflammatory responses, supporting the involvement of a GSK−3β/HK2-associated signaling pathway in the regulation of microglial inflammatory activity.

In vitro, pharmacological inhibition of GSK−3β in BV2 cells under oxygen-glucose deprivation similarly decreased pro-inflammatory marker expression, confirming that GSK−3β signaling is an important regulator of microglial inflammatory activity. These experiments do not directly model exercise but validate the mechanistic role of GSK−3β in controlling microglial responses.

This study provides a novel perspective on the mechanisms of early exercise, highlighting the potential involvement of the GSK−3β/HK2 axis in modulating microglial phenotypes. Despite these insights, certain limitations remain. The sample size was relatively small, and a SHAM-exercise control was not included. Additionally, the use of BV2 cells and male-only rats may limit the broader applicability of the findings. As microglial activation is highly context-dependent, further research is required to fully elucidate the temporal dynamics of these inflammatory mechanisms across different stroke stages. Future studies using primary cultures and multi-sex cohorts are warranted to validate and extend these preliminary results. A notable limitation of the Western blot analysis in this study is the use of β-tubulin as the loading control. Emerging evidence suggests that β-tubulin levels may be altered in response to various cerebral stimuli, including ischemic injury and physical exercise. Following stroke, cytoskeletal reorganization and microtubule dynamics are profoundly affected, potentially leading to fluctuations in β-tubulin expression. Furthermore, exercise-induced neuroplasticity may modulate tubulin polymerization and stability, thereby influencing baseline β-tubulin levels. This variability could introduce normalization errors and compromise the accuracy of relative protein quantification. Future studies should validate β-tubulin stability under identical experimental conditions or employ alternative loading controls—such as total protein staining (Sanguesa et al., 2022, Strohm and Majewska, 2024), GAPDH, or β-actin with confirmed invariability—to ensure robust and reliable Western blot normalization.

Taken together, our findings indicate that early exercise can attenuate neuroinflammation after ischemic stroke by modulating the GSK−3β/HK2 signaling axis and shifting microglial inflammatory activity. By linking physical intervention with microglial metabolic-inflammatory regulation, this study provides a novel theoretical basis for exercise-based rehabilitation and identifies the GSK−3β/HK2 pathway as a potential therapeutic target for post-stroke recovery.
