Background

Early-life allergen sensitization is associated with childhood asthma, but sensitization patterns are heterogeneous. This study aimed to characterize early-life sensitization phenotypes and evaluate their associations with school-age asthma, with exploratory machine-learning analyses examining model-derived allergen contributions.

Methods

Electronic medical records from Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (2014–2022) were used. Two samples were derived: a phenotype discovery sample (Sample A, n = 4989) and a nested case-control sample (Sample B, n = 435; 84 cases and 351 controls). In Sample A, latent class analysis (LCA) identified population-level sensitization phenotypes. In Sample B, pre-specified multivariable logistic regression models adjusted for age and sex evaluated associations of LCA-derived phenotypes and individual inhalant allergen sensitization with school-age asthma. Random Forest and XGBoost/SHAP were used as exploratory analyses to assess allergen feature importance and model-derived contributions to predicted asthma probability.

Results

LCA identified four sensitization phenotypes: predominantly non-sensitized, food-dominant, inhalant-dominant, and pet dander-predominant. After adjustment for age and sex, The inhalant-dominant phenotype was associated with higher odds of school-age asthma (adjusted OR = 3.14, 95% CI: 1.43–6.91), whereas the pet dander-predominant phenotype showed a positive but non-significant association (adjusted OR = 1.75, 95% CI: 0.94–3.26). In the pre-specified multivariable allergen model, dust mite sensitization remained significantly associated with asthma (adjusted OR = 3.30, 95% CI: 1.94–5.62). Exploratory analyses identified consistent model-derived contribution patterns for dust mite, whereas dog dander sensitization showed more variable patterns. XGBoost AUC decreased from an apparent AUC of 0.859 to a mean internally validated AUC of 0.636.

Conclusion

Early-life sensitization showed distinct population-level phenotypes, with the inhalant-dominant phenotype associated with school-age asthma. Dust mite showed the most consistent signal across association and exploratory prediction analyses. Dog dander findings were model-dependent and should be interpreted as exploratory prediction patterns. SHAP findings require external validation before clinical application.

Introduction

Asthma is one of the most common chronic inflammatory airway diseases in children with a steadily increasing prevalence worldwide.1 In China, the prevalence of childhood asthma has also increased substantially over recent decades.2 Allergic asthma represents the predominant phenotype, accounting for more than 80% of pediatric cases.3 Early life has been recognized as a critical window in asthma pathogenesis, during which environmental exposures and immune development interact to determine whether immune tolerance is established or allergic disease develops.4,5 In particular, early-life allergen sensitization, especially to inhalant allergens, has been identified as a key risk factor for persistent asthma.6,7

However, allergen sensitization is highly heterogeneous, and only a subset of sensitized individuals ultimately develop asthma. It has been increasingly recognized that sensitization should not be viewed as a single feature but rather as a collection of distinct patterns, some of which carry clear pathological significance while others represent “benign sensitization” associated with a favorable clinical prognosis.8 This heterogeneity extends beyond individual sensitization profiles to the broader trajectory of allergic disease development. The traditional concept of the “atopic march” describes a sequential progression from early-life eczema to food allergy and subsequently to asthma and allergic rhinitis. However, increasing evidence suggests that many children do not follow this linear trajectory, and allergic diseases may instead develop through multiple heterogeneous pathways.9,10 Together, these observations highlight the complexity of allergic disease development and the need to better characterize the diversity of sensitization patterns and their clinical implications.

Latent class analysis (LCA) has been increasingly applied to identify underlying sensitization phenotypes based on multiple allergen profiles.11 Allergen sensitization patterns vary considerably across geographic regions and populations, largely because of differences in climate, vegetation, humidity, environmental exposures, and living conditions.12 Previous studies have demonstrated that distinct sensitization patterns exist across populations and may be associated with different risks of allergic diseases.13–15 In particular, persistent inhalant sensitization and early multi-sensitization have been associated with increased risks of asthma and allergic airway diseases.16 However, findings regarding specific sensitization patterns and allergens remain inconsistent across studies. While some studies have identified house dust mite, grass pollen, cat, or pet-related sensitization as strong predictors of asthma,17,18 others have reported limited or no associations for certain food or pet-related sensitization patterns.19,20 These inconsistencies may reflect substantial heterogeneity across populations, geographic regions, age groups, and sensitization profiles. Therefore, further studies are needed to better characterize the complex relationships between sensitization patterns and asthma outcomes in different populations and regional settings.21

Beyond population-level sensitization patterns, allergen-specific associations with asthma also warrant further examination. Early-life house dust mite sensitization has been consistently associated with childhood asthma across diverse populations and is generally regarded as a relatively stable risk factor.22 In contrast, findings for pet-related sensitization — particularly dog allergens — have been markedly more variable across studies. Some studies report increased asthma risk associated with pet sensitization, whereas others find limited or no significant associations.23–25 These inconsistencies suggest that allergen-related associations may depend on population characteristics, exposure context, co-sensitization patterns, or other sources of heterogeneity. Conventional regression models estimate population-level associations, whereas flexible modeling approaches may provide complementary information on model-derived allergen contribution patterns.

Machine learning approaches have been increasingly applied in asthma research for risk prediction and identification of important clinical features.26 However, the interpretability of these models has remained a major challenge, limiting their translational value in clinical research. SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP), a model interpretation framework based on cooperative game theory, quantifies the contribution of each feature to individual model predictions at both global and individual levels,27–30 providing an interpretable framework for examining how allergen features contribute to model-predicted outcomes across individual observations.

Based on these considerations, this study aimed to investigate the association between early-life allergen sensitization and school-age asthma in a real-world pediatric cohort from Wuhan, China. We used routine allergen extract–based sIgE data derived from electronic medical records. In this study, we applied an integrated analytical framework combining population-level phenotype characterization and exploratory machine-learning interpretation. First, LCA was applied to identify population-level sensitization phenotypes, and multivariable logistic regression models evaluated their associations of LCA-derived phenotypes and individual inhalant allergen sensitization with school-age asthma. Second, Random Forest models were used as exploratory prediction-oriented analyses to assess the relative importance of allergen features for asthma classification rather than population-level associations. Finally, XGBoost-based SHAP analyses were performed to characterize model-derived allergen contribution patterns. These machine-learning analyses were exploratory and were not intended to infer causal effects, independent associations, or biological mechanisms.

Methods

Study Design and Data Collection

This retrospective study was conducted between August 2025 and January 2026. Clinical records of children who underwent allergen-specific IgE testing between August 2014 and August 2022 were retrospectively reviewed. Asthma outcomes were ascertained through electronic medical record review up to August 2025.

This retrospective study used electronic medical records from Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (2014–2022). An initial database (A0, n=5500) was established by identifying all children younger than 4 years who underwent serum allergen-specific immunoglobulin E (sIgE) testing during the study period. For children with repeated sIgE measurements, only the first test result was retained, yielding a cleaned database (n = 5424) for subsequent analyses.

Two analytical samples were subsequently derived from cleaned database:

Sample A (n = 4989): a cross-sectional phenotype discovery sample.

Sample B (n = 435): a nested case-control sample with retrospective electronic record linkage for risk modeling, which included 84 school age asthma cases (6–12 years) and 351 controls (6–12 years).

Sample B was constructed as follows: To ascertain asthma outcomes, we retrospectively retrieved subsequent clinical visit records for all individuals in the cleaned database through electronic record linkage using unique patient identifiers. We reviewed pediatric outpatient and inpatient records from school age (6–12 years) up to August 2025 (all data extraction was performed after ethics approval). Potential asthma cases were identified using ICD-10 codes for bronchial asthma and cough-variant asthma, followed by manual chart review to confirm diagnostic validity. Children were excluded if they had: asthma diagnosed before 4 years of age, uncertain asthma diagnosis, congenital cardiopulmonary diseases, prior allergen immunotherapy, or long-term systemic corticosteroid/biologic treatment.

Confirmed asthma cases were identified after manual chart review. Controls were frequency-matched to cases at a 1:5 ratio by sex and age at first sIgE testing. Subsequently, all candidate controls underwent manual chart review to exclude children with documented asthma diagnoses or clinical records suggestive of asthma-like symptoms (eg, recurrent wheezing, chronic cough, or dyspnea) during the period for which electronic records were available (up to August 2025). After eligibility review, 351 controls were retained, resulting in a final sample of 84 asthma cases and 351 controls.

Variables and Definitions

Serum allergen-specific IgE (sIgE) testing was performed in the clinical laboratory of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University using a commercially available immunoblotting assay. The same commercial reagent manufacturer was used throughout the study period, with a panel of 19 allergens before 2019 and 29 allergens from 2019 onward. Sensitization was defined as a serum sIgE level of ≥0.35 IU/mL according to the laboratory reference standard, and the same positivity threshold was applied throughout the study period.

Because the study period spanned 8 years (2014–2022) and the hospital’s allergen testing panel changed after 2019, we performed two preprocessing steps to ensure data consistency. First, pollen test results were aggregated into a single binary variable (pollen sensitization), as the specific pollen types and reporting formats (combined panels vs individual tests) differed between the two periods. Second, allergens that were measured only after 2019 (eg, peanut, soybean, silk) were excluded from the analysis, as they had no corresponding measurements in the earlier period. The final analysis included 12 core allergens that were consistently available across the entire study period.

A total of 12 allergens were included in the analysis, comprising food allergens (milk, egg white, nuts, seafood, meat, and fruit) and inhalant allergens (dust mite, cat dander, dog dander, pollen, mold, and cockroach). For allergens assessed using multiple related testing items, individual allergens were aggregated into composite categories. A composite allergen category was considered positive if any constituent allergen showed sensitization. All 12 core allergen variables were measured for every participant included in the analysis, and no missing values were present for these allergen variables. Allergen sensitization variables were analyzed as binary variables (sensitized vs non-sensitized). The detailed constituent allergens included in the composite allergen categories are presented in Supplementary Table 1.

Early-life clinical variables collected in Sample B included sex, age at first sIgE testing, birth status (preterm, term, or post-term), mode of delivery (cesarean versus vaginal delivery), and feeding history (breastfeeding, formula feeding, or mixed feeding). Polysensitization was defined as sensitization to two or more allergens. Asthma diagnosis was confirmed based on guideline-based clinical assessment through manual chart review.

Latent Class Analysis

Latent class analysis (LCA) was performed in Sample A using 12 allergen sensitization variables to identify sensitization phenotypes. Models with one to six classes were fitted using the poLCA package in R based on maximum likelihood estimation with the expectation-maximization algorithm. To reduce the risk of local maxima, multiple random starting values were used (nrep = 80), and the maximum number of iterations was increased (maxiter = 10,000). Model stability across different random seeds was additionally assessed. The optimal number of classes was determined based on Bayesian information criterion (BIC), entropy, smallest class proportion, model stability, and clinical interpretability. Individuals were assigned to latent classes according to maximum posterior probability (modal assignment). Conditional probabilities were used to characterize sensitization patterns across classes. The estimated class assignment model derived from Sample A was subsequently applied to Sample B for phenotype-based analyses without re-estimation of model parameters.

Missing data were limited to three early-life clinical covariates extracted from electronic medical records. Birth status was missing for 19 children (4.37%), mode of delivery for 18 children (4.14%), and feeding history for 18 children (4.14%). No missing data were present for allergen sensitization variables, age, sex, LCA classification, or asthma outcomes. Available-case analysis was used for descriptive comparisons involving these clinical covariates, and no imputation was performed because the proportion of missing data was low.

Statistical and Machine Learning Analyses

All statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.5.2). And a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize baseline characteristics in Sample B. Categorical variables, including age group, were presented as counts and percentages. Group comparisons between asthma and control groups were performed using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate.

Univariate logistic regression analyses were first conducted to describe the crude associations between candidate variables and asthma outcomes. To avoid reliance on significance-based variable selection, multivariable logistic regression models were constructed based on clinical relevance and prior knowledge. For the phenotype-based analysis, LCA-derived sensitization phenotypes were included as the main exposure, with adjustment for age and sex. For the allergen-specific analysis, age, sex, and the six major inhalant allergen sensitizations assessed in this study (dust mite, pollen, cat dander, dog dander, mold, and cockroach) were simultaneously included in the multivariable model. Adjusted odds ratios (ORs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were reported. A Random Forest model was constructed using the 12 allergen variables as predictors and asthma status as the outcome. The model was implemented using the ranger package in R with probability estimation enabled (probability = TRUE). The number of trees was set to 2000, and the random seed was set to 2026. The remaining model parameters were set to the default values of the ranger package, including mtry = floor(sqrt(p)), splitrule = “gini”, and min.node.size = 1. No formal hyperparameter tuning or additional class-imbalance handling was performed. Variable importance was evaluated using permutation importance. Model discrimination was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis and area under the curve (AUC). Internal validation was performed using 1000 bootstrap resamples to estimate optimism and derive an optimism-corrected AUC.

XGBoost was implemented using the xgboost package with the same 12 allergen sensitization variables. The objective function was set to binary: logistic, the evaluation metric to logloss, and the number of boosting rounds to 100 (nrounds = 100). Other hyperparameters were set to the package defaults, including max_depth = 6, eta = 0.3, subsample = 1, colsample_bytree = 1, min_child_weight = 1, and gamma = 0. No formal hyperparameter tuning or additional class-imbalance handling was performed. Model performance was evaluated using internal five-fold cross-validation. No random seed was prespecified for the XGBoost model. SHAP values were subsequently calculated to provide exploratory, model-derived explanations of allergen contributions to XGBoost-predicted asthma probability. Because all predictors were pre-defined binary sensitization variables with no outcome-derived preprocessing or feature selection, no additional preprocessing steps were required within the cross-validation procedure.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University (approval No. WDRY2025-K193, dated August 19, 2025). Informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design. Patient data were handled confidentially and in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Discussion

Sensitization Phenotypes

In Sample A, we identified four distinct sensitization phenotypes, including pet dander–predominant, inhalant-dominant, food-dominant, and predominantly non-sensitized patterns, indicating substantial heterogeneity in early-life allergen sensitization. Previous latent class analyses conducted in different populations have also identified heterogeneous sensitization patterns, although the dominant allergens varied across studies. Studies from Western populations commonly reported phenotypes characterized by food allergens, pollen, or mixed inhalant sensitization,31 whereas multicenter studies from China more frequently identified dust mite- or pollen-related sensitization patterns. Regional differences within China have also been observed, with dust mite sensitization predominating in southern regions and pollen sensitization more common in northern regions.32 These findings suggest that sensitization phenotypes may be influenced by geographic environment, climate, allergen exposure profiles, and population characteristics.

In our single-center cohort from central China, the predominantly non-sensitized phenotype represented the largest subgroup, whereas inhalant-related sensitization phenotypes showed stronger associations with asthma risk, particularly patterns involving dust mite sensitization. These findings are consistent with previous studies suggesting that inhalant allergen sensitization plays a more important role in childhood asthma development.

Differential Patterns of Dust Mite and Pet Dander Sensitization Across Analytical Approaches

Across the regression-based association analyses and exploratory machine-learning analyses, dust mite sensitization showed the most consistent allergen-related signal. In the pre-specified multivariable logistic regression model, dust mite sensitization remained significantly associated with school-age asthma. It was also ranked as the most important allergen feature in the Random Forest model and showed relatively consistent positive model-derived contributions in the SHAP analysis.

This finding is consistent with previous studies identifying early-life dust mite sensitization as a major risk factor for childhood asthma.33 Compared with other allergens, previous studies have suggested that dust mite sensitization may be associated with more persistent airway inflammation and stronger type 2 immune responses, which may partly explain its relatively stable association with asthma risk.34–36

In the present study, pet dander-related findings were less consistent across analytical approaches. In the pre-specified multivariable logistic regression model, neither cat nor dog dander sensitization was significantly associated with asthma. In contrast, dog dander sensitization was ranked highly by the machine-learning model despite not showing a statistically significant association in the pre-specified multivariable logistic regression model. This discrepancy should not be interpreted as evidence of an independent etiological effect. Rather, it may reflect co-sensitization patterns, collinearity between pet sensitization, nonlinear relationships, statistical interactions, or model-dependent feature importance.

SHAP analysis was further used to provide an exploratory interpretation of the XGBoost model and to examine how allergen variables contributed to model-predicted asthma probability at the individual-observation level. While dust mite sensitization showed relatively consistent positive model-derived contributions, dog dander sensitization demonstrated substantial variability in both the magnitude and direction of SHAP contributions across individual observations. However, the XGBoost model showed a substantial decline in performance after internal validation, indicating potential overfitting. The SHAP-derived patterns should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive findings. Therefore, these SHAP patterns should be interpreted as exploratory descriptions of model prediction behavior rather than causal effects, independent associations, or biological mechanisms. In particular, the variable SHAP pattern observed for dog dander sensitization should not be interpreted as evidence that dog dander has different biological effects across individuals, or that it is protective in some children and harmful in others.

Previous studies have reported inconsistent associations between pet sensitization and asthma, with some studies suggesting increased asthma risk, whereas others observed weak or no significant associations. Such variability may reflect differences in environmental exposure, co-sensitization profiles, timing of sensitization, host immune tolerance, characteristics of study populations and the strength of sensitization.37–40

Interestingly, compared with other inhalant allergens, cat and dog dander sensitization did not show a consistent increase across age groups in our cross-sectional analyses, whereas most other inhalant allergens showed higher sensitization prevalence in older age groups. These findings further suggest that pet-related sensitization patterns may be more variable and context-dependent than dust mite sensitization.

Taken together, these findings suggest that conventional regression and interpretable machine-learning analyses may provide complementary, but distinct, information. Regression models estimate population-level associations, whereas machine-learning methods describe model-derived prediction importance and contribution patterns.

Limitations

Several limitations should be considered. First, this was a retrospective study based on children who underwent serum sIgE testing at a tertiary hospital, which may introduce indication-related selection bias. These children were more likely to have allergic symptoms or clinical suspicion of allergic disease than the general pediatric population, limiting the generalizability of the findings to community-based populations. The retrospective design also limits causal inference. Second, asthma outcomes were ascertained from electronic medical records within a single tertiary hospital. Some children may have received asthma diagnoses outside this hospital system or after the available observation period, potentially leading to outcome misclassification. In addition, because the inclusion period spanned several years, follow-up opportunity may have varied across participants, which could have resulted in unequal outcome ascertainment. Because allergen sensitization patterns may change with age, the distribution of LCA-derived phenotypes may partly reflect age-related maturation of sensitization. Although the age distribution of the four phenotypes was explored descriptively, age was not incorporated into the LCA model, and residual age-related influence on phenotype assignment cannot be excluded. Third, the relatively limited number of asthma cases reduced statistical power for subgroup, interaction, and complex machine-learning analyses, particularly for pet dander-related findings and allergens with low sensitization prevalence or wide confidence intervals. Given the multiple allergen-related comparisons, borderline associations should therefore be interpreted cautiously. The XGBoost model showed reduced performance after internal validation, indicating potential overfitting, SHAP results are model-dependent and may be influenced by correlations among variables; therefore, the observed contribution patterns should be interpreted cautiously. Finally, allergen sensitization was assessed using allergen extract-based sIgE testing and dichotomized as positive or negative, rather than using component-resolved diagnostics (CRD) or semi-quantitative sIgE levels. CRD could provide more precise molecular profiles (distinguishing genuine sensitization from cross-reactivity), and sIgE titers or grades might offer additional dose-response information. Nevertheless, extract-based dichotomized testing remains the most widely used approach in routine clinical practice.

Conclusion

In this retrospective pediatric cohort, early-life allergen sensitization patterns were heterogeneous, and LCA identified four distinct sensitization phenotypes. The inhalant-dominant phenotype was associated with increased odds of school-age asthma, while dust mite sensitization showed the most consistent association across the pre-specified regression analyses and exploratory machine-learning analyses. In contrast, the contribution patterns observed for dog dander sensitization were model-dependent and should be interpreted as exploratory model-derived prediction patterns rather than evidence of individual-level biological heterogeneity or protective effects. Overall, these findings highlight the potential value of integrating population-level sensitization phenotyping with exploratory machine-learning approaches to characterize complex allergen-related signals, while underscoring the need for external validation before clinical application.
