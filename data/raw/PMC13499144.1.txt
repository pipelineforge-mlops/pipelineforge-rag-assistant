Antegrade intramedullary nail and plate combination technique for proximal humeral non-union: A case report

Proximal humeral nonunion remains a challenging condition, particularly after failed plate fixation, because of the compromised bone stock within the humeral head and loss of medial calcar support. Under these conditions, it is often difficult to achieve sufficient mechanical stability while preserving the humeral head. We report a case of proximal humeral nonunion successfully treated using the nail and plate combination technique. A 61-year-old man sustained a high-energy proximal humeral fracture in a motorcycle accident and underwent initial plate fixation at a different institution. The plate failed subsequently, resulting in varus displacement of the humeral head fragment and nonunion. Revision surgery was performed using an antegrade intramedullary nail combined with an anatomical proximal humeral locking plate and cancellous iliac bone grafting. This strategy was selected to restore medial calcar support, enhance axial stability, and increase the number of screws engaging the humeral head fragment without interfering with the implant. Postoperative imaging confirmed satisfactory alignment and stable fixation at the nonunion site. Bone union was achieved without displacement, and solid union was confirmed at the final, 2-year follow-up visit. At the same visit, shoulder function was favorable, with a Constant–Murley score of 82 points and preservation of a wide range of motion. The nail and plate combination technique may represent a useful treatment option for proximal humeral nonunion in selected patients, providing complementary mechanical stability that is difficult to achieve with single-implant fixation alone while preserving the humeral head.

Introduction

Proximal humeral fractures account for approximately 4–5% of all fractures and represent the third most common osteoporotic fracture, occurring predominantly as low-energy injuries in older women [1]. In contrast, younger patients more frequently sustain proximal humeral fractures from high-energy trauma, particularly motorcycle accidents [1]. The reported incidence of nonunion after proximal humeral fractures ranges from 1.1% to 10% [2]. Risk factors for nonunion include surgical neck fractures, fractures with greater than 33% translation between the humeral head and shaft, and metaphyseal comminution [2], [3]. In a systematic review, Oldrini et al. evaluated the outcomes of proximal humeral fractures treated with anatomical locking plates [4]. The incidence of nonunion was 1.9%, accounting for only 6.4% of all complications; however, approximately 26% of these nonunion cases required reoperation, indicating a potentially severe clinical course [4]. When surgical treatment of a nonunion is required after failed plate fixation, substantial bone stock reduction in the humeral head often results from multiple screw removals, leading to cancellous bone defects. When combined with bone loss at the nonunion site, these defects make it particularly challenging to achieve sufficient fixation stability [5]. Here, we report a case of post-fracture proximal humeral nonunion that was successfully treated using the nail and plate combination technique, which combines antegrade intramedullary nailing with an anatomical proximal humeral locking plate to enhance fixation stability.

Case presentation

Initial injury and primary surgery

A 61-year-old man presented to our institution with persistent pain and restricted range of motion in the right shoulder. Five months prior to presentation, he sustained an injury to his right upper arm in a fall from a motorcycle and was transported to a local hospital on the same day. His medical history was notable for hypertension. He was a high school teacher, and the injury involved his dominant arm. Initial presentation revealed no open wounds or clinical findings suggestive of neurological or major vascular injury. Plain radiographs and computed tomography (CT) demonstrated extensive fractures classified as AO/OTA types 11C3 and 12B3 (Fig. 1A–C). Open reduction and internal fixation were performed at the previous hospital 12 days after injury via a deltoid-splitting approach, using a proximal humeral internal locking system plate (DePuy Synthes) (Fig. 1D, E). During postoperative follow-up, breakage of the proximal portion of the plate was observed; however, the patient was managed conservatively without revision surgery. Referral and preoperative evaluation

At the initial visit, active shoulder elevation was limited to 120° (Fig. 2A). Plain radiographs and CT scans revealed union of the humeral shaft fracture; however, plate failure was associated with varus displacement of the humeral head fragment and nonunion of the proximal humerus (Figs. 2B, C; 3A–C). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed no evidence of humeral head osteonecrosis or apparent rotator cuff tear (Fig. 3D, E). Revision surgery

Surgery was performed in the beach-chair position using a deltopectoral approach. All remaining implants were removed, and the nonunion site was debrided until bleeding from both the humeral head fragment and the shaft was confirmed. After reduction of the varus displacement of the humeral head fragment, approximately 1 cm of shortening was performed at the bone defect, and fixation was achieved with an antegrade intramedullary nail (ANN Proximal Humeral System, Zimmer Biomet) (Fig. 4A, B).Fig. 4 Intraoperative fluoroscopic images and postoperative radiographs.

Postoperative course and outcomes

Postoperative CT confirmed satisfactory alignment of the humerus and stable fixation at the nonunion site (Fig. 5A–D). Early range-of-motion exercises were initiated, and low-intensity pulsed ultrasound was applied to facilitate bone healing. At the 2-year follow-up, radiographs demonstrated solid union of the nonunion site without displacement (Fig. 5E, F). The postoperative range of motion of the affected shoulder (contralateral side in parentheses) was as follows: flexion of 175° (180°), extension of 45° (45°), external rotation of 50° (55°), and internal rotation to the level of the first lumbar vertebra (twelfth thoracic vertebra) (Fig. 6). Discussion

Treatment options for proximal humeral nonunion include nonoperative management, revision osteosynthesis with bone grafting using either a plate or intramedullary nail, augmentation with structural bone grafts, and shoulder arthroplasty in cases where preservation of the humeral head is not feasible [8], [9]. The Non-Union Scoring System (NUSS) is a useful tool for selecting the optimal treatment strategy, as it incorporates fracture characteristics, bone quality, previous surgical procedures, soft-tissue status, and patient-related systemic factors [10]. The NUSS comprehensively quantifies the difficulty of treating nonunion by scoring bone-related factors (such as bone quality, bone loss, and alignment), soft-tissue factors (including scarring, skin defects, vascularity, and infection), and patient-related factors (such as ASA classification, diabetes, smoking status, and medication use) [10]. This scoring system helps determine whether a nonunion can be managed with standard reconstruction aimed at promoting bone healing or whether more complex reconstruction or alternative treatment strategies are required [10]. In the present case, the NUSS score was 15, indicating that standard surgical treatment for nonunion, namely, revision fixation with preservation of the humeral head, was the most appropriate first-line option.

The key factors for successful internal fixation in proximal humeral nonunion surgery include restoration of medial calcar support and securing sufficient bone stock within the humeral head fragment [8], [9]. Insufficient medial calcar support may result in varus displacement of the humeral head fragment and mechanical failure of fixation; reconstruction of the medial support is therefore important, and the use of fibular strut grafts or intramedullary nails is reportedly effective [11], [12]. In contrast, strategies to secure adequate bone stock within the humeral head fragment include bone grafting into the humeral head, augmentation with injectable bone cement through screw channels, and increasing the number of screws inserted into the humeral head fragment [13], [14], [15].

In the present case, an intramedullary nail was selected as the primary fixation device to restore medial calcar support and provide axial stability. Bi et al. reported that the combination of an intramedullary nail with a small plate in complex proximal humeral metaphyseal fractures enhances stability against varus, shear, and rotational forces, thereby reducing displacement at the fracture site [16]. Similarly, in the present case, additional plate fixation was considered necessary to augment stability beyond intramedullary nailing alone and to reinforce the bone stock within the humeral head. The use of a small plate or conventional anatomical proximal humeral locking plate may be associated with limitations in the number of screws that can be inserted into the humeral head, as well as the risk of implant interference with the intramedullary nail. Accordingly, particular attention was paid to the structural characteristics of the A.L.P.S. Proximal Humerus Plating System. This system allows fixed-angle insertion of proximal locking screws into the humeral head, providing multidirectional subchondral support and reducing the risk of intra-articular screw penetration and varus collapse [17]. In addition, the plate can be positioned lower on the humerus, thereby minimizing the risk of subacromial impingement [18]. Together, these design features permit independent insertion of proximal locking screws from both the intramedullary nail and plate into the humeral head fragment without mutual interference (Fig. 7). In practice, provisional plate fixation after intramedullary nail insertion allows confirmation, using Kirschner wires inserted through the proximal screw guides, that the planned screw trajectories do not interfere with the nail or proximal locking screws. This approach facilitates the placement of a greater number of screws from the plate into the humeral head fragment. Accordingly, use of the nail and plate combination technique with this plate system may enhance fixation stability, prevent varus displacement, and secure sufficient bone stock within the humeral head fragment, even in challenging cases of proximal humeral nonunion.Fig. 7 Implant configuration using a bone model.

Proximal humeral nonunion treated with humeral head–preserving revision osteosynthesis reportedly yields postoperative Constant scores of approximately 75–84 points, according to a systematic review by Zastrow et al. [19]. The Constant score of 82 points achieved in the present case is comparable to these reported outcomes and suggests that satisfactory functional results can be achieved with humeral head–preserving revision fixation in appropriately selected patients. In contrast, the same systematic review reported lower postoperative Constant scores for arthroplasty-based treatments, with mean scores of 66.8 ± 11.4 points for hemiarthroplasty or total shoulder arthroplasty (TSA) and 59.4 ± 6.6 points for reverse TSA, supporting the role of arthroplasty as an alternative treatment option when preservation of the humeral head is not feasible [19]. Although the DASH score in the present case remained relatively high at 43.3 points, this may reflect the patient's high functional demands in daily activities, including his occupation as a high school teacher and sports team advisor, which may have influenced patient-reported outcome measures.

However, this technique has important limitations. In cases of humeral head osteonecrosis, extensive bone loss, severe osteoporosis, or a history of infection, sufficient fixation stability and reliable bone healing may not be achievable with this approach. In such cases, alternative treatment strategies, including shoulder arthroplasty, should be carefully considered.

Conclusion

The nail and plate combination technique, which combines an intramedullary nail with an anatomical proximal humeral locking plate, may serve as a useful treatment option for proximal humeral nonunion requiring restoration of medial calcar support and securing adequate bone stock within the humeral head fragment. This technique can provide complementary mechanical stability that is often difficult to achieve with single-implant fixation alone.
