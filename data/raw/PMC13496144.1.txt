Objective

To develop a scientifically sound and practical electronic health (eHealth) literacy intervention plan for patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS), and to provide a reference for its clinical implementation.

Methods

This study utilized the Integrated Model of e-Health Use (IMeHU) as the theoretical framework and constructed an evidence-based model based on the PICO principles. A preliminary draft of the intervention protocol was developed through a comprehensive literature review, and an expert panel meeting was convened to evaluate its feasibility and scientific validity. Subsequently, expert feedback was collected via a questionnaire survey and analyzed accordingly. On this basis, a small-scale pilot study was conducted to gather patient feedback. Ultimately, the intervention protocol was refined and revised by integrating expert opinions with patient feedback, leading to the finalization of the e-health literacy intervention program for patients with OSAHS.

Results

The final eHealth literacy intervention program comprises 3 first-level, 8 second-level, and 22 third-level indicators. A single round of expert consultation was conducted with 10 experts. The questionnaire response rate was 100%, the expert authority coefficient was 0.90, the coefficient of variation ranged from 0.09 to 0.27, and the Kendall coefficient of concordance was 0.185 (P < 0.05).

Conclusion

This study focuses on the systematic development and rigorous expert validation of the intervention. Through evaluation and optimization, the resulting eHealth literacy intervention program for OSAHS patients features comprehensive content and a rigorous structure, fully demonstrating the feasibility of the proposed program with expert endorsement.

Obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS) is a chronic disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of apnea, hypopnea, hypercapnia, and hypoxemia resulting from upper airway obstruction during sleep.1 Clinically, it presents with loud snoring, sleep fragmentation, and excessive daytime sleepiness, which significantly impair patients’ daily functioning and quality of life.2 If left untreated, OSAHS can progress to severe complications, including pulmonary hypertension, coronary heart disease, and cerebrovascular accidents, thereby posing serious health risks. Currently, clinical practice predominantly focuses on therapeutic efficacy, often overlooking the critical role of self-management; this oversight frequently leads to suboptimal treatment outcomes.

The rapid integration of the Internet and healthcare has accelerated the development of digital tools, including electronic medical records, wearable devices, mobile health applications, and telemedicine. Consequently, there is growing interest in electronic health literacy (eHealth)—defined as the ability to seek, find, understand, and appraise health information from electronic sources and apply the knowledge gained to address or solve a health problem.3,4 While existing literature has largely focused on the determinants of eHealth literacy and scale validation, intervention studies designed to actively enhance patients’ eHealth literacy remain scarce.

Effective management of OSAHS requires not only clinical intervention but also long-term patient adherence to healthy behaviors, such as weight control and sleep position training. However, the complexity and variable quality of online health information make patients vulnerable to misinformation, potentially leading to cognitive biases. Patients with high eHealth literacy can effectively search for online health information, distinguish valid information, and apply it to their daily lives. This improves their self-efficacy, enhances their self-management capabilities, and ultimately promotes their quality of life. Therefore, developing a targeted eHealth intervention is not only timely given the digital health landscape but also vital for improving self-management, adherence, and quality of life while reducing complication risks.

Currently, research on eHealth literacy interventions has been widely conducted in fields such as HIV,5 cardiovascular diseases,6 psychological disorders,7 and patient caregiving.8 However, studies focusing on eHealth literacy interventions for patients with OSAHS remain limited. In clinical practice, continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) is the primary treatment for OSAHS.9 The vast majority of patients do not require long-term hospitalization; instead, they rely on home-based self-management. However, with the rapid advancement of technologies such as the internet, artificial intelligence, and virtual reality, the quality of online health information has become highly variable.

Therefore, it is particularly necessary to develop eHealth literacy interventions tailored for patients with OSAHS. This paper summarizes the intervention strategies applied in other diseases and constructs an eHealth literacy intervention plan tailored to the specific characteristics of OSAHS, based on the Integrative Model of eHealth Use (IMeHU).10 Furthermore, the scientific validity of this proposed plan was evaluated through expert consensus meetings. Ultimately, this study aims to provide an effective reference for improving patients’ eHealth literacy levels and enhancing their self-management capabilities.

Serials and Methods

Literature Research

Establish Evidence-Based Issues

The PICO framework was employed to formulate an evidence-based problem, which subsequently guided the establishment of the literature inclusion criteria. Population: The target population for the application of evidence consisted of patients diagnosed with OSAHS. Intervention: The intervention measures focused on improving eHealth literacy. The intervention themes encompassed eHealth information orientation, eHealth information efficacy, and social support. Comparison: The control group received routine care, while the experimental group underwent an eHealth literacy intervention program. Outcome: Outcome measures included eHealth literacy levels, self-efficacy, self-management ability, and information self-efficacy.

Theoretical Basis

The Integrative Model of eHealth Use (IMeHU) was developed by Bodie et al (Figure 1) and is grounded in the Lily Model of eHealth literacy established by Norman et al.4 This model connects macro factors within the social structure to the fundamental components of eHealth literacy, including health literacy and computer literacy, as well as personal electronic health usage and health outcomes. The theoretical framework posits that macro factors in the social structure influence an individual’s eHealth literacy, which in turn affects micro factors such as motivation and the effectiveness of eHealth utilization. These micro variables ultimately shape an individual’s health knowledge, beliefs, and behaviors. Based on the core dimensions of IMeHU, this study adopted these dimensions as intervention themes and utilized them as first-level and second-level indicators to guide the construction of the program. Furthermore, by synthesizing the best evidence through a literature review, a preliminary draft of the eHealth literacy intervention program for patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea (OSAHS) was formulated. Figure 1 The Integrative Model of eHealth Use (IMeHU).

The diagram illustrates the factors influencing eHealth literacy and outcomes. It begins with socioeconomic status, personality traits, demographic characteristics and online usage experience, which lead to health literacy (eHealth literacy) and computer literacy. These contribute to health information orientation and health information efficacy. The next stage involves online health behaviors, information retrieval and retention and eHealth attitudes. Finally, these lead to health knowledge (disease knowledge), health beliefs and health behaviors (self-management).A diagram of factors influencing eHealth literacy and outcomes.

Retrieval Strategy

Using the “6S” evidence model, we conducted a comprehensive search across several databases, including China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Medical Network, VIP Journal Database, China Biomedical Literature Database (SinoMed), PubMed, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library. The search strategy involved the integration of subject terms with free-text terms. The search terms included: “Obstructive Sleep Apnea-Hypopnea Syndrome”, “OSAHS”, “OSAS”, “Health Literacy”, “e-Health Literacy”, “Electronic Health Literacy”, “E-Health Literacy”, “Digital Health Literacy”, “Media Health Literacy”, “Mobile Health Literacy”, “Technology Health Literacy”, “Intervention”, “Randomized Controlled”, “Randomized Controlled Trial”, “RCT”, and “Quasi-experiment”. The search period extended from the inception of the databases until 2025 July 10 (Supplementary Material 1).

Taking the PubMed database as an example, we developed the following search strategy:

#1: “Obstructive sleep apnea hypopnea syndrome”[Title/Abstract] OR “OSAHS”[Title/Abstract] OR “OSAS”[Title/Abstract]

#2: “eHealth literacy”[Title/Abstract] OR “electronic Health Literacy”[Title/Abstract] OR “Digital Health literacy”[Title/Abstract] OR “Media Health literacy”[Title/Abstract] OR “Mobile Health”[Title/Abstract] OR “technology health”[Title/Abstract] OR “internet”[Title/Abstract] OR “web”[Title/Abstract]

#3: “intervention”[Title/Abstract] OR “randomized controlled”[Title/Abstract] OR “randomized controlled trial”[Title/Abstract] OR “RCT”[Title/Abstract] OR “quasi experiment”[Title/Abstract]

#4: #1 AND #2 AND# 3

The search strategy involved independently retrieving literature related to OSAHS, eHealth literacy (and its related concepts), and intervention studies. These three sets of results were then combined using the logical AND operator to identify studies that met all criteria.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria: Articles must focus on an intervention aimed at enhancing eHealth literacy or a related theme that encompasses specific intervention content. Outcome measures should include assessments of eHealth literacy or digital health literacy. The research design must be either a randomized controlled trial or a quasi-experimental study. Nursing interventions should consist of health education, training, and comprehensive or personalized intervention strategies. The language of the articles must be either Chinese or English.

Exclusion criteria: Subject mismatch: Literature not directly pertaining to intervention measures aimed at enhancing eHealth literacy was excluded. Non-research literature: Case reports, reviews, conference proceedings or abstracts, research proposals, opinion pieces, policy documents, and other forms of non-research literature were not considered. Literature with quality issues: Publications that had been duplicated, contained incomplete original data, or for which the full text was unavailable were excluded to maintain the quality and reliability of the research data.

Criteria for Quality Assessment

Appropriate quality appraisal tools were selected based on the specific types of included literature. Randomized controlled trials (RCTs), quasi-experimental studies, systematic reviews, and expert consensus papers were evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) critical appraisal checklists (2016). Widely recognized in evidence-based medicine, these tools effectively assess the methodological quality and reliability of diverse study designs. Additionally, clinical practice guidelines were assessed using the Appraisal of Guidelines for Research and Evaluation (AGREE II) instrument.

Literature Quality Assessment Process

A comprehensive search strategy was developed by consulting authoritative literature and methodologies, tailored specifically to the research questions and objectives. This strategy entailed selecting appropriate databases (eg, PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library), identifying relevant search terms (including disease nomenclature, interventions, and study designs), and constructing search strings. Concurrently, explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria were established through extensive discussion. Studies were included if they employed appropriate designs, targeted patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS), and addressed eHealth literacy interventions. Conversely, studies were excluded due to poor methodological quality, incomplete data, or duplicate publications.

During literature screening and data extraction, two researchers with systematic training in JBI evidence-based nursing independently screened studies and extracted data based on the established criteria. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion to reach a consensus; if disagreements persisted, a third researcher was consulted to make the final decision. Similarly, during quality appraisal, the two researchers independently evaluated the studies using the designated tools. Any inconsistencies were resolved through team discussions to reach a final consensus, taking into account the research objectives and practical context.

Evidence Extraction and Integration

Two researchers independently performed evidence extraction using a pre-designed form. The extraction encompassed basic study information (eg, author, publication year, study type), specific details of the intervention measures, evaluation indicators, and results. The principle guiding evidence integration prioritized high-quality evidence, evidence-based findings, and the most recently published studies, taking into account research quality, evidence level, and publication date. The specific judgment criteria are as follows: High-quality evidence is primarily determined by literature quality evaluations, such as those with relatively high scores in randomized controlled trials. Evidence-based findings adhere to the classification standards established by evidence-based medicine. The assessment of the latest published evidence is based on the publication year of the literature. A third researcher conducted proofreading, clarified ambiguous sections, and developed the initial intervention measures.

Expert Meeting

The expert panel meeting method involves convening domain experts with in-depth knowledge of the research topic to conduct a comprehensive analysis and evaluation through structured discussions. To ensure the scientific rigor of the proposed program, this study employed this method by inviting nursing experts specializing in respiratory and critical care medicine.

Research Content

Experts in relevant fields were invited to evaluate the operability, rationality, and scalability of the initial eHealth literacy intervention plan for patients with OSAHS. Their insights were instrumental in refining and enhancing the program.

Expert Selection

According to Hansson,11 the recommended size for an expert panel ranges from 4 to 16 members. After team discussions, 10 experts from diverse disciplines were recruited for this study. The selection criteria were as follows: (1) over 10 years of clinical or research experience in the relevant field; (2) a Master’s degree or higher for physicians, and a Bachelor’s degree or higher for nurses; (3) an intermediate professional title or above.

Meeting Theme

The expert meeting primarily addressed the appropriateness of the intervention plan, the suitability of its duration, and the alignment of the intervention timing with clinical standards. Additionally, the clarity and scientific basis of the content were evaluated. The discussion also encompassed the feasibility and scalability of the plan in clinical practice, alongside specific recommendations for optimizing the intervention strategy.

Meeting Implementation Process

One week prior to the meeting, relevant materials—including the project introduction, invitation letter, semi-structured interview outline, discussion topics, initial draft of the intervention plan, and feedback form—were emailed to the experts, and logistics were coordinated accordingly. Hard copies of the draft and feedback forms were distributed on-site. With the experts’ consent, a postgraduate student was assigned to audio-record the session, document opinions, and collect the forms. During the meeting, a researcher used a PowerPoint presentation to outline the project background, the development process of the intervention plan, and key discussion points. The researcher’s clinical supervisor then served as the moderator, facilitating a discussion on each component to gather expert feedback while detailed notes were taken. The findings were synthesized immediately after the session.

Statistical Analyses

The questionnaire data collected from the previous expert meeting was entered into Excel 2021 for organization, and the results of the inquiry were statistically analyzed with SPSS 25.0 software. Categorical data were expressed as frequencies and percentages (%), while normally distributed continuous data were presented as mean ± standard deviation (M ± SD). Expert enthusiasm was evaluated based on the questionnaire response rate and the rate of opinion submission. Expert authority was quantified using the authority coefficient, with values ≥0.7 indicating reliable results.12 The degree of coordination among expert opinions was assessed using the coefficient of variation and Kendall’s coefficient of concordance.12 A P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The Criteria for Item Selection

Retention: Items with a mean importance ≥3.5 and a coefficient of variation (CV) ≤0.25 were retained.

Modification: Items with a mean score ≥3.5 but a CV >0.25, or items with valuable qualitative comments from experts, were discussed and modified by the research team.

Deletion: Items with a mean score <3.5 and a CV >0.25 were excluded.

Quality Control

(1) Intervention items were developed in strict accordance with evidence-based nursing principles. Drawing on pertinent literature and clinical experience, the research team drafted the initial plan through multiple discussions, documenting all viewpoints and suggestions to ensure its scientific validity and rationality.

(2) Experts were rigorously screened based on the established selection criteria and recruited only after providing informed consent. Detailed records of their basic information and the selection process were maintained, and the research purpose, content, and requirements were communicated via formal invitation letters or emails.

(3) Following the expert meeting, two researchers independently reviewed and entered the collected questionnaires. The review process involved assessing questionnaire completeness and verifying data accuracy and consistency. To ensure precise data entry, a double-entry method was employed. Additionally, a data audit mechanism was established to regularly verify the input data, enabling the timely identification and correction of errors.

Pilot Study

Objectives

The primary objective of the pilot study was to evaluate the feasibility and rationality of the intervention, and to make appropriate modifications to the protocol.

Methods

Five patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome (OSAHS) who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria were recruited from a tertiary Grade-A hospital in Inner Mongolia for the pilot study. Based on the caregivers’ experiences and feedback, as well as the issues identified during implementation, the intervention protocol was further revised and refined. Ultimately, the final version of the eHealth literacy intervention protocol for patients with OSAHS was established.

Results of Literature Analysis

A literature review revealed that existing interventions primarily focus on enhancing patients’ eHealth literacy by providing disease education, conducting remote disease monitoring, teaching patients to correctly use online interactive tools, accurately identifying internet misinformation, seeking social support from family and peers, and improving their ability to search for online health information. The research tools commonly used include assessments of patients’ current disease cognition, eHealth literacy scales, social support scales, and self-management ability scales. The research teams mainly consist of medical staff and university faculty in computer science and information technology. The intervention formats are predominantly face-to-face training, supplemented by online and telephone follow-ups. The intervention timeline generally involves collecting baseline demographic data and assessing the current status of eHealth literacy before the intervention, followed by follow-up assessments at 1 month and 3 months post-intervention.

Formation of the Initial Intervention Plan

Primary and secondary indicators were established based on the core competencies of IMeHU. Integrating literature review findings and team input, a preliminary eHealth literacy intervention protocol for patients with OSAHS was developed. The intervention was delivered through a combination of face-to-face training and online modules. The initial session was conducted on the day of hospital admission or immediately after diagnosis, lasting 20 to 30 minutes per session. Patients were followed up for 3 months post-intervention.

Discussion

Development Process of the Preliminary eHealth Literacy Intervention Program for OSAHS Patients

Initially, guided by the IMeHU theoretical framework, this study established the primary and secondary indicators of the program. Combined with a literature review, the specific intervention content, methods, implementation duration, and follow-up schedules corresponding to each indicator were clarified, thereby forming the preliminary draft of the eHealth Literacy Intervention Program for OSAHS patients. Subsequently, an expert consensus meeting was organized to evaluate the draft, collecting professional feedback through expert consultations and questionnaire surveys. Following the meeting, qualitative opinions and quantitative data were synthesized for analysis and group discussions, based on which the preliminary draft was revised. Finally, a small-scale pilot study was conducted to gather patient feedback. The intervention program was further optimized based on these insights, ultimately yielding the final version of the eHealth Literacy Intervention Program for OSAHS patients.

The Scheme Developed from the Comprehensive Use Model of eHealth is Both Scientific and Reliable

Drawing upon a literature review and clinical practice, this study employed an expert meeting to design an eHealth literacy intervention program for patients with OSAHS, grounded in the comprehensive use model of eHealth. Ten experts specializing in respiratory medicine, respiratory nursing, and nursing management were selected. To ensure scientific integrity, all experts possessed over 15 years of clinical experience, with five holding master’s degrees or higher and nine possessing senior professional titles. The valid questionnaire recovery rate was 100%, indicating active expert participation. The expert authority coefficient was 0.90 (exceeding the 0.80 threshold), confirming the reliability of the results. Six experts provided constructive suggestions, and expert opinions were largely consistent. The coefficient of variation ranged from 0.00 to 0.27, and Kendall’s coefficient of concordance was 0.185 (χ2 = 59.144, P < 0.05), demonstrating a high degree of consistency and coordination among the experts.

The Scheme Developed Based on the Comprehensive Use Model of eHealth is Feasible

Previous studies indicate that patients often lack network security knowledge22 and demonstrate inadequate abilities to discern, inquire about, and utilize health information.23 Most elderly individuals have only a rudimentary understanding of the Internet and smart devices, often perceiving physician consultations and paper medical reports as more trustworthy. Consequently, they frequently struggle to use health information technology independently. Although family support is vital for enhancing eHealth literacy, the actual support provided by most families remains relatively low.

Grounded in the comprehensive use model of eHealth, this study highlights a positive correlation between social support and eHealth literacy.24 Accordingly, health information orientation, health information effectiveness, and social support were established as primary indicators for the intervention plan. The intervention taught patients with OSAHS how to operate basic electronic devices to enhance their Internet skills. Additionally, patients were educated on eHealth literacy and the “Five Principles of Safe eHealth Search” to foster accurate eHealth beliefs and attitudes. By contrasting the traditional medical model with the eHealth model, the study aimed to rebuild patients’ confidence in electronic information.25 Furthermore, doctor-patient communication groups and family WeChat groups were established to strengthen connections among patients, medical staff, and families, ultimately improving patients’ health information-seeking behaviors.26 The protocol has been validated through a pilot study. Patient feedback indicates that the protocol is user-friendly and highly instructive. Based on the pilot study results, the research team conducted targeted optimizations and adjustments to the protocol’s details, further enhancing its scientific rigor and clinical applicability. The results showed a high overall acceptance rate among the participating patients. This demonstrates the feasibility of the proposed program with expert endorsement.

The Scheme Developed Based on the Comprehensive Use Model of eHealth is Innovative

Distinct from previous generic or unidimensional intervention models, this study developed a targeted, comprehensive management protocol for OSAHS. By deeply focusing on the pathophysiological characteristics of OSAHS patients, the protocol successfully achieved a paradigm shift from generic intervention to precision management. Furthermore, this study innovatively introduced an AI-empowered strategy: by integrating specialized AI training with an intelligent management platform, we utilized digital means to effectively bridge the gaps of information asymmetry and delayed monitoring in traditional nursing, thereby providing patients with full-chain, intelligent health support.

Challenges Facing Digital Interventions

Research indicates27 that integrating Artificial Intelligence (AI) into the healthcare sector represents a transformative development with immense potential, poised to fundamentally revolutionize patient care, diagnostics, and treatment modalities. However, implementing digital interventions in clinical settings requires a comprehensive examination of the practical implementation challenges and ethical dilemmas involved. First, patient privacy and data security face severe tests. The high sensitivity of medical data necessitates the establishment of a robust protection framework throughout the entire data lifecycle, including collection, transmission, and storage.28 Second, the risks of online misinformation and algorithmic ethics cannot be ignored. In an open internet environment, AI-generated health advice may contain misleading content. Therefore, it is imperative to strengthen patient education, making it clear that AI information cannot replace professional medical diagnoses and must be used prudently under medical guidance. Furthermore, the issue of digital inequality urgently demands attention. A lack of diversity in algorithmic training data may lead to service biases against minority or specific groups; meanwhile, some elderly or low-income individuals may be excluded from the dividends of digital healthcare due to a lack of smart devices or digital literacy.29 The DeepSeek model adopted in this study features a comprehensive privacy protection mechanism, which can effectively safeguard patient privacy and security. To address the potential risk of misinformation in its generated content, this intervention specifically incorporates an information verification step. Furthermore, it emphasizes that patients must prudently refer to online information under medical guidance, thereby effectively mitigating the risk of being misled. Additionally, from a long-term perspective, we call for the introduction of more detailed privacy protection laws and regulations at the national level to provide a solid institutional safeguard for patient data security.

The Inadequacy of eHealth Literacy Intervention Programs for Patients with OSAHS

Grounded in IMeHU, the initial draft of the intervention plan was developed and refined through expert consultations. However, it remains in the preliminary exploration phase and has yet to be clinically validated. Future efforts should focus on evaluating and optimizing the plan via action research to ascertain its feasibility and effectiveness. Future eHealth literacy programs could also be integrated with objective OSAHS risk stratification methods, including biomarker-based or machine learning predictive models, to support individualized follow-up and treatment adherence.30

Conclusion

This study utilized an eHealth literacy intervention plan for patients with obstructive sleep apnea-hypopnea syndrome, developed through expert consultations and informed by literature analysis and clinical practice. The plan comprises three primary, eight secondary, and 22 tertiary indicators, preliminarily demonstrated its practicality, scientific rigor, and reliability. However, the current study primarily focuses on the development of the intervention protocol. Constrained by time and resources, only a single round of expert consultation was conducted, which may to some extent compromise the rigor and reliability of the protocol. Furthermore, since the protocol has currently undergone only small-scale pilot testing in clinical settings and has not yet been implemented on a large scale, it is insufficient to comprehensively evaluate its clinical outcomes and long-term efficacy; its actual effectiveness requires further validation. In the future, large-scale, multi-center randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are urgently needed to scientifically and objectively assess the clinical application value of this protocol.
