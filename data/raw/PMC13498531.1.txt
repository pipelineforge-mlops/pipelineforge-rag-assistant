In this study, we investigated the tumor-accumulating efficacy of photodynamic therapy (PDT) mediated by an aluminum chloride phthalocyanine-loaded nanoemulsion (AlClPc-NE) against breast cancer (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231) and non-tumorigenic (MCF-10A) cell lines. The optimized AlClPc-NE formulation exhibited a mean hydrodynamic diameter of 168 nm, zeta potential of − 29 mV, low polydispersity (0.25), encapsulation efficiency of 81.10%, and estimated stability of 12 months. The system effectively preserved the drug photoactivity, achieving a robust singlet oxygen quantum yield (ΦΔ = 0.52). In vitro assays confirmed excellent dark biocompatibility of the nanocomposites. Upon irradiation, AlClPc-PDT exhibited potent phototoxicity. Crucially, a selective therapeutic window was observed 24 h post-treatment at a light density of 1000 mJ/cm2, significantly reducing tumor cell viability by over 50% while preserving non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cells. This enhanced efficacy is attributed to the optimized cellular internalization and specific subcellular localization promoted by nanoemulsions. Translating these findings to an in vivo orthotopic breast cancer model, AlClPc-PDT significantly suppressed tumor progression, reducing the tumor mass by approximately 50%. Ultimately, the AlClPc-NE platform emerged as a highly localized therapeutic strategy, prioritizing tumor eradication while limiting the potential side effects in healthy tissues.

Highlights

A stable AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion with high encapsulation efficiency and preserved photoactivity was successfully developed for photodynamic therapy applications.

AlClPc-NE promoted enhanced cellular internalization and selective phototoxicity against breast cancer cells while preserving non-tumorigenic cells 24 h post-treatment.

The nanoemulsion maintained the photosensitizer in an active monomeric state, achieving a high singlet oxygen quantum yield (ΦΔ = 0.52).

In vivo photodynamic therapy mediated by AlClPc-NE significantly suppressed orthotopic breast tumor progression, reducing the tumor burden by approximately 50% under these conditions.

Introduction

Breast tumors have numerous classification methods owing to their highly diffuse and heterogeneous molecular characteristics. While the World Health Organization (WHO) divides them into 20 subgroups, clinical practice typically relies on five subgroups based on molecular and histological characteristics: (1) Luminal A, which expresses hormone receptors for progesterone (PR) and estrogen (ER), but is negative for human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) with low expression of Ki-67; (2) Luminal B, which is also PR and ER positive but expresses HER2 and has high levels of Ki-67; (3) triple-negative tumors, which lack PR, ER, and HER2 expression and are generally associated with mutations in BRCA 1 and 2 genes; (4) HER2-enriched, which are PR and ER negative but HER2 positive; and (5) normal-like, which shares the molecular profile of Luminal A associated with a worse prognosis [1–4]. Owing to these distinct characteristics and the invasive nature of certain subtypes, therapies are increasingly personalized and based on a combination of local and systemic treatments. This integration, known as multimodal therapy, involves surgical resection (mastectomy), chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and endocrine therapy [3, 4].

Photodynamic Therapy (PDT) has emerged as a promising localized approach based on the combination of two individually non-toxic components: light and a photosensitizer (PS), which interact to induce the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS) in the targeted tissues. PDT is a highly specific cancer treatment that minimizes systemic side effects, as the PS typically has a short half-life and is activated only by a specific wavelength, confining cytotoxicity exclusively to the irradiated area [5, 6]. Aluminum chloride phthalocyanine (AlClPc) is a highly efficient PS used in PDT; however, its extreme hydrophobicity requires the use of nanocarriers to maintain its photochemical and photophysical properties in physiological environments [7–10]. The encapsulation of PS into nanoparticles prevents molecular aggregation and enhances tumor targeting through the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect. In this phenomenon, nanovehicles passively accumulate in the tumor region by extravasating through the leaky vasculature typical of inflamed and neoplastic tissues [11–13].

In pharmaceutical research, the successful clinical translation of hydrophobic drugs relies heavily on the design of delivery systems [14, 15]. In this context, lipid-based nanoemulsions have gained widespread acceptance. Unlike aqueous-core systems (such as liposomes), oil-in-water (O/W) nanoemulsions feature a lipophilic core that provides an ideal environment for solubilizing highly hydrophobic molecules such as AlClPc [16, 17]. These nanosystems are biodegradable, biocompatible, and exhibit low systemic toxicity. Furthermore, their high surface area and nanoscale dimensions facilitate transport across biological membranes, increasing drug bioavailability, and promoting controlled release.

In breast cancer therapy, lipid-based nanocarriers have been extensively validated to improve the therapeutic index of chemotherapeutics, drastically reducing typical side effects, such as cardiotoxicity [18–21]. For PDT applications, studies involving lipid nanosystems carrying photosensitizers have demonstrated rapid cellular uptake, enhanced ROS production, and the induction of specific apoptotic pathways [22–25]. Notably, recent studies investigating AlClPc-loaded formulations have shown that apoptosis is the preferential cell death pathway activated by PDT in various aggressive cancer models [26]. While previous studies have explored AlClPc delivery through various nanocarriers, including traditional liposomes and polymeric nanoparticles, many of these systems face limitations, such as drug leakage, premature aggregation, and poor long-term stability, due to the extreme hydrophobicity of the photosensitizer [7, 9, 27]. To overcome these critical bottlenecks, the present study introduces a highly optimized oil-in-water (O/W) nanoemulsion specifically designed to accommodate AlClPc within a lipophilic core [16]. This architecture not only provides superior colloidal and long-term stability but also prevents photosensitizer self-aggregation, ensuring a high singlet oxygen yield. By meticulously evaluating this platform, we aim to highlight its distinct translational advantages: a proven temporal window of selective phototoxicity that spares non-tumorigenic cells (MCF-10A) and robust in vivo efficacy capable of suppressing tumor progression in an orthotopic breast cancer model without systemic dark toxicity.

In this study, we developed and evaluated a stable oil-in-water nanoemulsion designed to encapsulate and deliver the photosensitizer aluminum chloride phthalocyanine (AlClPc). We investigated the physicochemical and spectroscopic properties of the AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion (AlClPc-NE) to ensure its structural stability and preserved photoactivity. Ultimately, we evaluated the therapeutic and translational potential of this nanotechnology platform for PDT using in vitro cytotoxicity and subcellular localization assays against breast cancer (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231) and non-tumorigenic (MCF-10A) cell lines, followed by comprehensive in vivo validation in an orthotopic breast tumor model.

Materials and methods

Nanoemulsion preparation

The oil-in-water (o/w) AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion (AlClPc-NE) was prepared by spontaneous emulsification and quantified as described by Siqueira-Moura et al. [28]. Briefly, the organic phase contained medium-chain triglycerides, natural soy phospholipids, and AlClPc (Sigma-Aldrich Co., St. Louis, MO, USA) was heated to 55 °C and subsequently added to the aqueous phase containing Poloxamer 188 (Sigma-Aldrich Co., St. Louis, MO, USA). The organic solvent was then removed by evaporating at 60 °C. Control formulations without photoactive compounds (blank NE) were prepared under identical conditions.

All formulations were characterized regarding their mean hydrodynamic size, polydispersity index (PdI), and zeta potential at 25 ± 2 °C using a Zetasizer® Nano ZS (Malvern Instruments, UK). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD) of three independent batches. Furthermore, the size, PdI, and zeta potential were monitored over 90-day period to assess the stability of the formulation.

Physicochemical characterization of formulations

The hydrodynamic diameter of the particles and the polydispersity index (PdI) were determined by photocorrelation spectroscopy on the Nano ZetaSizer™ equipment (ZEN3600, Malvern Instruments, UK), in the He–Ne laser operating mode at 633 nm, scattering angle of 173°, and temperature of 25 °C. AlClPc/NE was diluted 1:100 (v/v) in filtered Mili-Q water (membrane diameter 0.22 µm). The zeta potential of the particles was determined by electrophoretic mobility using the same equipment.

The amount of AlClPc in the formulation was determined using UV–vis absorption spectroscopy (Ultrospec 7000, GE, Switzerland) and calculated using the maximum absorbance (ʎmax) of AlClPc at 674 nm [29]. Briefly, 0.06 mL of the formulations were diluted in DMSO (analytical grade, Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA) in a 1:1 ratio (v/v) and subjected to an ultrasound bath (Bransonic Ultrasonic Cleaner – 2210R-MTH) for 30 min. Subsequently, the solution was transferred to a quartz cuvette with an optical path of 1 cm, and the volume was adjusted to 2 mL with tetrahydrofuran (analytical grade, Sigma-Aldrich, MO, USA). The encapsulation efficiency (EE%) of free AlClPc was determined after ultracentrifugation of 0.4 mL of the formulations on a 30kDA filter (Microcon Ultracel YM-100, Millipore, Ireland) at 12,857 × g for 1 h at 4 °C (Eppendorf, centrifuge 5810 R, Hamburg, Germany). Atomic force microscopy (AFM)

AFM images were obtained 30 days after the formulation production. The equipment used was a Scanning Probe Microscope (SPM-9600, Shimadzu Corporation, Japan) in tapping mode. Analyses were performed at 25 °C using silicon tips (PPP-NCH, Nanosensors, Switzerland). The values obtained were determined by the SPM Offline software (Shimadzu Corporation, Japan). Samples were diluted 5:1000 (v/v) in filtered Milli-Q water (0.22 µm diameter). Then, 5 μL of the dilution was dripped onto the center of the mica on a freshly cleaved surface, allowing the sample to run along the slightly inclined surface and dry at room temperature.

Cell culture

The breast cancer cell lines used in this study were: MCF-7 (ATCC HTB-22TM), MDA-MB-231 (ATCC HTB-26TM) and MCF-10A (ATCC CRL-10317TM) were cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute medium (RPMI1640) (GIBCO-BRL, Life Technologies, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Cultilab, Brazil), 100 U/mL penicillin (GIBCO-BRL, Life Technologies, USA), and 100 μg/mL streptomycin (GIBCO-BRL, Life Technologies, USA). The cells were maintained in a humidified 5% CO2 incubator at a constant temperature of 37 °C.

Phototoxicity assay and determination of cell viability

Initially, the cells were cultured in 24-well culture plates at a density of 70,000 cells/well and remained in culture for 24 h at 37 °C under 5% CO2. For nanoemulsion treatment, cells were incubated in a medium containing the formulation in the presence or absence of AlClPc (0.5 µmol/L). After 3 h of incubation, the cells were washed with PBS, and the RPMI was replaced. The wells were then irradiated in isolation using a diode laser system (Eagle, Quantum Tech, Brazil) operating at 675 nm, coupled to an optical fiber at a power of 0.20 W (spot size area of 1 cm2) at the following irradiance rates: 200, 700, and 1000 mJ/cm2, which corresponded to exposure times of 1 s, 3.5 s, and 5 s, respectively. Cell viability was measured 24 and 72 h after light exposure using the resazurin assay. Briefly, resazurin sodium salt (Sigma Aldrich, USA) was added to 25 µg of the sample. mL−1 in RPMI, and the cells were incubated for 4 h. Absorbance was measured using a microplate reader at 570–600 nm (Safire2, TECAN, Austria). Cell viability was calculated as previously described [33]. All parameters were evaluated in triplicates (n = 3). Flow cytometry analysis was performed to complementarily acquire cell viability data. Briefly, the contents of the wells were collected, and the culture medium was stored in an Eppendorf tube for further analysis. The cells were detached from the wells using trypsin and added to the previously collected medium. The samples were centrifuged at 1,000 rpm and 4 °C for 5 min in an Eppendorf 5810R Centrifuge (Eppendorf AG, Hamburg, Germany). The supernatant was removed, and the precipitate was resuspended in a 1:1 RPMI solution (Gibco, USA) without phenol red/ViaCount reagent (Guava Technologies) and incubated for 10 min at room temperature in the dark. Samples were analyzed by flow cytometry using the Guava® EasyCyte™ 8HT Benchtop Flow Cytometer (Guava Technologies, Hayward, CA, USA) and Guava CytoSoft 4.2.1 Software Environment, with a total of 5000 events acquired for each sample. Cell sublocalization assay by confocal microscopy

Approximately 20,000 cells/well were added to a glass slide (13 mm) placed in a 24-well plate. The cells were culture for 24 h at 37 °C under 5% CO2. They were then treated with 0.5 µmol/L AlClPc (AlClPc-NE) for 3 h. Untreated cells were used as the control. After treatment, the cells were washed with PBS (1X) and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (Electron Microscopy Sciences) diluted in PBS (1X). Following a series of five washes with PBS (1x) containing 100 mM glycine (Sigma-Aldrich), the cells were permeabilized with a solution of 0.1% Triton X-100 (Sigma-Aldrich) diluted in PBS (1X) for 10 min at room temperature. After washing, the cells were incubated for 15 min with Alexa Fluor 488 phalloidin (Life Technologies) diluted in PBS at a ratio of 1:20. A further series of washes with PBS (1X) was performed, followed by a wash with distilled water and drying. Coverslips were transferred to glass slides and fixed using ProLong® Gold Mount Medium Reagent (Life Technologies) containing 4′, 6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI). The slides were observed using a Leica TCS-SP8 confocal microscopy system (Leica Microsystems, Mannheim, Germany). The operating conditions and image collection were selected according to the fluorophore present, with fixed excitation for a predetermined emission. Emission/excitation (641/780 nm) was used to detect the AlClPc. For phalloidin, an actin filament marker, excitation/emission wavelengths of 495/518 nm were used. For the nuclear marker DAPI, the excitation and emission conditions were 358/461 nm. The fluorescence quantification of the images obtained was performed using the ImageJ® software, in which the images were normalized and analyzed in triplicate, in addition to using the same parameters for the analysis area for all of them.

In vivo studies

All experimental procedures were conducted in accordance with the Ethical Principles in Animal Experimentation established by the National Council for Animal Experimentation Control (CONCEA) and were approved by the Ethics Committee on Animal Use (CEUA) of the Faculty of Philosophy, Sciences and Letters of Ribeirão Preto (FFCLRP), Ribeirão Preto (Protocol No. 2020.1.539.59.9). A total of 20 female C57BL/6 J wild-type mice (eight weeks old) were sourced from the Central Animal Facility of the University of São Paulo and used in this study. The animals were randomly assigned to groups (n = 5 per group) and maintained in a temperature-controlled environment (20–22 °C) with a 12 h light–dark cycle and ad libitum access to water and standard chow.

Tumor induction and nanoparticle administration

Mammary adenocarcinoma was induced using the EO771 cell line (originally from ATCC, VA, USA) generously provided by Prof. Dr. Catarina Raposo Dias Carneiro (FCF/UNICAMP, Brazil). Prior to tumor induction, EO771 cells were cultured and maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin–streptomycin. For orthotopic transplantation, female C57BL/6 wild-type mice were anesthetized via intraperitoneal injection of ketamine (10 mg/kg) and xylazine (80 mg/kg). Subsequently, 5 × 105 cells resuspended in 50 μL of Matrigel were inoculated into the fourth right mammary fat pad, following the protocol established by Nachat-Kappes et al. [34]. Tumor progression was monitored for eight days post-induction. Upon confirmation of tumor establishment, the animals were randomly assigned to four experimental groups (n = 5 per group): saline control (Control), AlClPc-NE + Light control, and AlClPc-NE + Light. AlCPc-NE was prepared in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), whereas the control group received an equivalent volume of vehicle (PBS) to ensure uniform handling stress. All procedures were performed under ketamine/xylazine anesthesia. Fourteen days after the initial injection, the mice were euthanized, and blood was harvested via cardiac puncture.

In vivo bioluminescence imaging

To evaluate the tumor burden, luciferase-transduced EO771 cells were detected using an In Vivo Imaging System (IVIS). Mice were anesthetized with 3% isoflurane (Abbot Laboratories) and administered an intraperitoneal bolus of d-luciferin (150 mg/kg). During image acquisition (exposure time: 5–10 min), the animals were maintained under 1.5% isoflurane sedation within the dark chamber of an IVIS® Lumina LT Series III (Xenogen Corp, PerkinElmer, USA). Bioluminescent signals were processed using Living Image Software (v. 4.3.1). The region of interest (ROI) tool was employed to quantify the radiance, with the data expressed in absolute units of photons/s.

Statistical analysis

Three independent experiments were performed for each parameter (n = 3). The results are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). Only in vivo studies were included, with a sample size of five animals per group. The control of each experiment was compared to the respective data from the treated groups using a mixed-model analysis of variance (ANOVA). Bonferroni post-hoc tests for multiple comparisons were used to investigate statistical significance. All statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism version 7.0a (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA). Data were considered statistically significant when the P value was < 0.05 [35, 36].

Results and discussion

Physicochemical analysis of formulations

The AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsions (AlClPc-NE) were characterized using dynamic light scattering (DLS), nanoparticle tracking analysis (NTA), and electrophoretic mobility measurements, as summarized in Table 2 and Fig. 1. DLS analysis revealed a reduction in the hydrodynamic diameter upon AlClPc incorporation, decreasing from 220.6 ± 15.9 nm (NE) to 168.8 ± 5.1 nm (AlClPc-NE). This trend was corroborated by NTA measurements, which showed mean particle sizes of 161.5 ± 6.6 nm for NE and 125.0 ± 3.6 nm for AlClPc-NE. A marked decrease in the polydispersity index (PdI) was also observed after AlClPc incorporation, from 0.353 ± 0.02 (NE) to 0.154 ± 0.01 (AlClPc-NE), indicating improved size homogeneity. The zeta potential values remained negative for both formulations, with a slight increase in magnitude from − 48.1 ± 3.4 mV (NE) to − 42.1 ± 3.0 mV (AlClPc-NE), suggesting preserved electrostatic stability. The nanoemulsion exhibited an encapsulation efficiency of 81.1% and a drug-loading capacity of 0.06%, demonstrating the capacity of the system to retain the photosensitizer. Accelerated stability analysis (Fig. 1B) showed reduced sedimentation velocities for AlClPc-NE across all size fractions (D10, D50, and D90), indicating an enhanced resistance to phase separation. Spectroscopic analysis (Fig. 1C) confirmed the successful incorporation of AlClPc, with a characteristic absorption band at approximately 674 nm. Functional evaluation (Fig. 1D) demonstrated a linear oxygen consumption profile under irradiation, confirming the preserved photoactivity of the encapsulated compound. Morphological analysis by AFM (Fig. 1E) revealed spherical nanostructures for both systems, with AlClPc-NE displaying a more uniform particle distribution than NE. Overall, AlClPc incorporation reduced the particle size, improved homogeneity, and enhanced colloidal stability while preserving the photophysical and functional properties of the system.

The incorporation of AlClPc induced significant changes in the physicochemical and biophysical properties of the nanoemulsion, indicating enhanced structural organization and colloidal stability of the system. The reduction in the hydrodynamic diameter and polydispersity index suggests the formation of a more homogeneous droplet population, which is associated with a lower incidence of kinetic instabilities, such as coalescence, flocculation, and Ostwald ripening [37]. In nanostructured systems, low dispersity is also associated with greater predictability of colloidal behavior and improved pharmaceutical reproducibility [16].

At the nanoscale, modifications in the interfacial organization directly affect parameters such as surface energy, Brownian mobility, and particle–particle interactions. In this context, the reduction in droplet size observed after AlClPc incorporation suggests more efficient molecular packing at the O/W interface. The high surface area of these nanostructures favors intermolecular interactions between phospholipids and hydrophobic aromatic compounds, contributing to greater structural cohesion and stability of the dispersed system [17, 38]. In addition, particles smaller than 200 nm exhibit increased surface contact with biological membranes, which may favor cellular interactions and a more homogeneous distribution within biological microenvironments [27].

The supramolecular properties of AlClPc also contributed to the observed behavior. Its π-conjugated aromatic macrocycle promotes hydrophobic and π–π interactions with phospholipid acyl chains, increasing the lipid packing density and reducing interfacial defects [39]. Nonpolar metallophthalocyanines (MPc), owing to their symmetric planar structure and negligible dipole moment, preferentially align parallel to the bilayer, maximizing the hydrophobic and π–π interactions [40]. Simultaneously, the axial Al–Cl coordination introduces steric constraints that limit extensive H-type aggregation, favoring a more homogeneous distribution of molecules within the lipid interface [41]. This aspect is particularly relevant for photodynamic systems because extensive phthalocyanine aggregation is associated with reduced photophysical performance and lower reactive oxygen species generation efficiency [13, 42].

The maintenance of highly negative zeta potential values after AlClPc incorporation indicates that structural reorganization occurred without compromising the electrostatic stabilization of the nanoemulsion. In colloidal systems, a high surface charge increases the repulsive interactions between particles and reduces the attractive forces associated with aggregation and sedimentation [43]. Therefore, the preservation of the surface charge indicates the maintenance of the energetic barrier required for the kinetic stability of the dispersion. Studies on lipid nanoparticles have demonstrated that the combination of electrostatic stabilization and interfacial organization is directly associated with improved physicochemical stability during storage [44].

The enhanced stability of AlClPc-NE over time and under accelerated conditions further supports the presence of a more organized nanometric system. The reduction in the sedimentation rate is directly associated with the smaller particle size and the predominance of Brownian motion over gravitational forces. In lipid nanoemulsions, smaller and more homogeneous particles exhibit lower susceptibility to gravitational separation and greater resistance to physical destabilization [45]. Furthermore, more compact interfaces reduce internal molecular mobility and hinder the droplet fusion process.

Another relevant finding was the preservation of the characteristic Q-band of AlClPc, associated with the maintenance of system functionality. The persistence of this band suggests the predominance of monomeric or weakly associated species within the lipid environment, indicating that the nanoemulsion restricted extensive aggregation of the photosensitizer. This behavior directly affects photodynamic efficiency because the aggregation state of phthalocyanines influences the quantum yield and reactive oxygen species generation [27, 46].

Collectively, these results demonstrate that AlClPc incorporation directly modulates the molecular and interfacial organization of nanoemulsions. The combination of reduced particle size, low dispersity, preserved surface charge, and increased resistance to sedimentation indicates enhanced colloidal stability and improved structural organization of the system. These characteristics are particularly relevant to the dynamic behavior of photobiological effects, in which physicochemical stability and preservation of photoactivity are critical determinants of the formulation performance.

Subcellular localization and internalization analysis

Confocal laser scanning microscopy (CLSM) was used to evaluate the internalization and spatial distribution of AlClPc delivered by the nanoemulsion (NE) system. As illustrated in Fig. 2A, AlClPc-NE accumulated distinctly in the perinuclear region across all cell lines (MCF-7, MDA-MB-231, and MCF-10A). This localized distribution contrasts significantly with the pattern observed for free AlClPc, which exhibited a diffuse fluorescence signal throughout the cytoplasm (Fig. 2B). The specific perinuclear localization promoted by the NE system is a critical finding, as it suggests that the nanocarrier facilitates the delivery of the photosensitizer near vital organelles, which may enhance the efficiency of photodynamic therapy upon irradiation. This behavior is highly modulated by aluminum-phosphate complexation at the vesicle interface, which stabilizes the system and favors the delivery of AlClPc in its active form [47]. Furthermore, no morphological alterations or disruptions in actin filaments (labeled with phalloidin) were detected in any lineage in the absence of red light activation. This confirms the dark biocompatibility and safety of the AlClPc-NE platform prior to photodynamic treatment. Quantitative fluorescence analysis further supported the superiority of the nanoemulsion. In all tested lineages, the fluorescence intensity of AlClPc was significantly higher when delivered via NE than that of the free drug. This enhanced signal indicates that the NE system successfully prevents AlClPc self-aggregation in a physiological environment, promoting its entry into cells in the active monomeric form [48]. The nanoemulsion carrying AlClPc appears to promote a more efficient delivery of AlClPc to the cells, probably due to the controlled release of the drug, which in turn decreases the self-aggregation of AlClPc and improves its entry into the cells [48]. Thus, AlClPc-NE can promote the photodynamic properties of AlClPc and consequently improve the treatment rates via PDT. The mechanisms of cellular selectivity still lack further molecular evaluations; however, the indications that the present work presents can be considered promising in the context of an efficient and selective treatment for breast cancer.

Effects of AlClPc-PDT on MCF-7, MCF-10A and MDA-MB-231 cell lines

To investigate the cytotoxic impact of AlClPc-mediated PDT on the selected breast cell lines, cell viability rates were monitored 24 and 72 h post-treatment. Initially, the data were analyzed using a resazurin-based reduction assay (Alamar Blue). As illustrated in Fig. 3, no significant differences in cell viability were observed among the CT, CTE, CTV, and CTL groups in the absence of light, confirming the biocompatibility of these components.Fig. 3 In vitro cell viability of non-tumorigenic (MCF-10A) and breast cancer (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231) cell lines following AlClPc-NE-mediated photodynamic therapy. A Cell viability was evaluated using the resazurin reduction assay (Alamar Blue) at 24 and 72 h post-treatment. B Cell viability was assessed using flow cytometry analysis (Guava ViaCount) at 24 and 72 h. The experimental conditions included dark controls (CT, CTE, and CTV), light control (CTL irradiated at 1000 mJ/cm2), and PDT treatment groups irradiated at light densities of 200, 700, and 1000 mJ/cm2. Data are expressed as the mean ± SD of three independent replicates (n = 3). Statistical significance is indicated by *(P < 0.005) and P < 0.01 compared to the respective control groups

In contrast, the AlClPc-NE PDT group exhibited a significant, dose-dependent reduction in viability compared to the control group (P < 0.05) at light densities of 700 and 1000 mJ/cm2. After 24 h of treatment, a clear selective profile was observed: while the malignant MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells showed a substantial reduction in viability (averaging over 50%), the non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cells remained largely unaffected, showing no significant difference from the controls.

However, at the 72h mark, a potent and widespread phototoxic effect was recorded across all cell lines, with MCF-10A survival rates also declining significantly to 59.5% (700 mJ/cm2) and 37.6% (1000 mJ/cm2). These results indicate that although the AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion effectively triggers potent phototoxicity upon activation, the therapeutic window for selective tumor targeting is most prominent within the first 24 h post-irradiation.

Additionally, the resazurin-resorufin assay (commercially available as CellTiter Blue ® and Alamar Blue®) is based on an oxidation–reduction indicator that fluoresces and changes color when reduced by living cells [49]. Viable cells with active metabolism can reduce resazurin, a blue compound, to form resorufin, a pink molecule with an absorbance at 570 nm [33, 49]. Dye reduction is promoted by NADH reductase and carnitine dehydrogenase [50, 51]. However, the authors demonstrated that chemical functional groups (thiols, amines, amides, and carboxylic acids) may interfere with colorimetric assays involving resazurin and formazan dye in the absence of metabolic activity [52]. This lack of clarity about the reduction site may make the use of resazurin reduction data in cytotoxicity studies difficult to interpret the acquired information.

To further confirm the results of the resazurin-based assay, we performed flow cytometry using the Guava ViaCount assay. This assay distinguishes viable cells from dead cells based on the differential permeability of the cell membrane to the DNA-binding dyes used [53, 54]. (i) The “nuclear dye” stains nucleated cells, allowing for the determination of total cell counts. (ii) The “viability dye” stains dead cells [53]. As depicted in the lower panels of Fig. 3, the AlClPc-NE dark control groups (CT, CTE, and CTV) maintained robust cell viability across all lineages. The viability of malignant MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231 cells remained above 91% at both 24 and 72 h post-treatment. For non-tumorigenic MCF-10A cells, viability in the dark groups was approximately 75 and 87% at 24 and 72 h, respectively. According to the ISO 10993–5 standard (“Biological evaluation of medical devices—Tests for in vitro cytotoxicity”), viability reductions that remain above the 70% threshold indicate a lack of cytotoxic potential. These data confirm that the AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion is highly biocompatible and non-toxic in the absence of light-activation.

However, upon irradiation at a light density of 1000 mJ/cm2, flow cytometry data at 72 h revealed a drastic and widespread cytotoxic effect. The survival rates plummeted, nearly eradicating the MDA-MB-231 lineage (0.94%) and triggering massive cell death in the MCF-10A lineage (~ 8.95%). These profound reductions at the 72 h mark are consistent with the resazurin assay results, demonstrating the overwhelming phototoxic efficacy of the PDT system at high light densities over extended periods.

While the 2D in vitro model demonstrated high toxicity for both healthy and tumor cells at 72 h under maximum light density, previous complex models suggest a more nuanced biological response. For instance, a study employing a three-dimensional (3D) culture model of the MCF-7 tumor line demonstrated a ~ 50% reduction in cell viability when exposed to AlClPc-PDT at 1000 mJ/cm2 [55]. Conversely, another study by our group showed that healthy fibroblasts subjected to photomodulation with an AlClPc nanoemulsion (0.5 μM) at 1.2 J/cm2 in 3D conditions successfully modified signaling responses, promoting collagen synthesis and actin network proliferation, without lethal toxicity [35]. Together, these data suggest that while the AlClPc-NE system is an exceptionally potent phototoxic agent at 1000 mJ/cm2 in 2D monolayers, its clinical translation may rely on optimizing light dosimetry and leveraging the 3D tumor microenvironment to maximize selective tumor eradication.

In vivo analysis

To evaluate the therapeutic potential of the AlClPc-NE platform, an in vivo orthotopic breast cancer model was established using luciferase-expressing EO771 cells in C57BL/6 wild-type mice. Tumor progression and response to photodynamic therapy were monitored longitudinally using noninvasive in vivo bioluminescence imaging (IVIS). For in vivo photodynamic therapy, the AlClPc-NE formulation was administered intraperitoneally at a dose of 25 µmol/kg. Following a 4 h interval to allow for optimal tumor accumulation via the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect, the tumor region was irradiated using a 675 nm diode laser system at a power of 0.20 W, applying a single energy density of 1000 mJ/cm2 (corresponding to an exposure time of 5 s for a 1 cm2 irradiated area).

As demonstrated in the quantitative photon flux analysis (Fig. 4), tumors in the saline control and light-only groups exhibited aggressive exponential growth between days 8, 10, and 12 post-induction. In stark contrast, the cohort subjected to the complete PDT protocol (AlClPc-NE + Light) exhibited profound and statistically significant suppression of tumor growth (P < 0.01) by day 12. While the AlClPc-NE dark group displayed a slight modulation in tumor progression, the light-activated system provided unequivocally superior therapeutic efficacy, confirming that tumor destruction is directly driven by the photodynamic reaction rather than the inherent toxicity of the nanoparticles. These quantitative findings were visually corroborated by bioluminescence radiance maps (Fig. 4, bottom panels). The luminescent signal, which correlates directly with the viable tumor burden, expanded drastically in the control subjects across the observational window. In contrast, the luminescent area in the AlClPc-NE + Light group was visibly contained. By quantifying the total photon flux (Ph/s) within the regions of interest (ROI), which directly correlates with the viable tumor cell mass, we calculated an overall reduction in tumor burden of approximately 50% at the final experimental endpoint (day 12) compared to the untreated control group. This significant tumor suppression, coupled with tumor-accumulating cellular phototoxicity, aligns with our previous investigations utilizing other metal-based nanoplatforms, which also demonstrated pronounced selective toxicity against MCF-7 cells and robust in vivo efficacy in breast cancer models [56]. The robust in vivo tumor suppression demonstrated the therapeutic efficacy of AlClPc-NE. Although its nanoscale dimensions (~ 168 nm) and negative charge (− 42.1 mV) suggest passive tumor targeting via the Enhanced Permeability and Retention (EPR) effect [57, 58], direct pharmacokinetic and biodistribution studies have not been conducted. Therefore, EPR-mediated accumulation remains a proposed mechanism rather than an experimentally validated one. Future investigations utilizing in vivo tracking are required to map the biodistribution of the system. Nonetheless, once localized, the nanoemulsion effectively preserved the monomeric state of the photosensitizer, ensuring a high singlet oxygen yield (ΦΔ1O2 = 0.52) upon laser irradiation. Ultimately, these in vivo results aligned perfectly with the in vitro findings, demonstrating that the AlClPc-NE platform provides a highly effective multimodal approach with promising tumor-selective potential for breast cancer treatment. Additionally, no adverse clinical changes or statistically significant body weight variations (p > 0.05) were observed within or between any experimental groups throughout the 12-day study period (Supplementary Information, Fig. S1). Collectively, the robust in vivo tumor suppression observed in the orthotopic model effectively bridges our physicochemical and in vitro findings, confirming the translational potential of the AlClPc-NE platform. By successfully encapsulating hydrophobic phthalocyanine within a stable, biocompatible nanoemulsion, we overcame the aggregation limitations of free photosensitizers while retaining a high singlet oxygen yield. The dual nature of the system, displaying excellent safety in the absence of light and potent, selective phototoxicity upon irradiation, has been consistently demonstrated from non-tumorigenic cell monolayers to a complex in vivo tumor microenvironment. Together, these milestones strongly position the AlClPc-loaded nanoemulsion as a highly promising and minimally invasive candidate for tumor-accumulating breast-cancer therapy.

Conclusions

In this study, we successfully developed a stable aluminum chloride phthalocyanine-loaded nanoemulsion (AlClPc-NE) with promising tumor-selective potential as a photodynamic therapy (PDT) platform for breast cancer. This formulation overcame the hydrophobicity of the photosensitizer and preserved its photoactive properties, generating a robust singlet oxygen yield (ΦΔ = 0.52). In vitro, the system demonstrated a distinct therapeutic window at 24 h, effectively eradicating malignant cells (MCF-7 and MDA-MB-231) while preserving non-tumorigenic mammary cells (MCF-10A) under optimal light densities. These findings were successfully translated in vivo using a murine model, in which the complete PDT protocol significantly suppressed tumor progression without inducing dark toxicity. However, we acknowledge certain limitations of the present study. The current findings primarily rely on in vitro assays and a single orthotopic animal model. Therefore, comprehensive in vivo biodistribution profiling, mechanistic internalization, long-term safety assessments, and broader translational feasibility studies should be established in future investigations. Ultimately, the AlClPc-NE platform showed promising tumor-selective potential, prioritizing tumor eradication while demonstrating a favorable therapeutic window.
