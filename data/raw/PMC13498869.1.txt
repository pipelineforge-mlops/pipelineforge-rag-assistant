ABSTRACT

Background

Immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy is a progressive immune-mediated chronic kidney disease with a high prevalence and disease burden in East Asia, including China. In a prespecified interim analysis (IA) of the global phase 3 VISIONARY trial, treatment with sibeprenlimab led to significant placebo-adjusted reduction in 24-hour urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (uPCR-24h) of 51.2% (P < .0001) at 9 months. Here, we report the interim efficacy and safety findings for sibeprenlimab for patients with IgA nephropathy enrolled from mainland China as part of the main global population.

Methods

Patients (N = 102) were randomized 1:1 to receive sibeprenlimab 400 mg or placebo subcutaneously Q4W for 26 doses. Patients who completed the 9-month uPCR-24h assessment were included. The primary endpoint was change in uPCR-24h from baseline at 9 months. The key secondary endpoint of annualized estimated glomerular filtration rate slope over 24 months will be reported at trial completion. Other secondary and exploratory endpoints included spot urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (spot uPCR), proteinuria remission, haematuria resolution, reduction in biomarker levels [including galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1) and a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL)], and safety.

Results

By the China IA data cutoff (January 2025), 42 patients from mainland China completed the 9-month uPCR-24h assessment (sibeprenlimab, n = 25; placebo, n = 17) and were included in the efficacy analysis. At 9 months, a reduction from baseline in uPCR-24h of 63.1% was observed in sibeprenlimab-treated patients versus a decrease of 3.1% in the placebo group, corresponding to a placebo-adjusted reduction of 61.9% (95% confidence interval 43.2%–74.5%). Spot uPCR declined by week 4 and was sustained through month 12. Sibeprenlimab reduced Gd-IgA1 (67.3%) and suppressed APRIL levels (97.7%) from baseline at week 48. The incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events was similar between treatment groups.

Conclusions

Interim efficacy and safety results for the mainland China cohort were consistent with the IA of the global cohort of the VISIONARY trial (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05248646).

KEY LEARNING POINTS What was known:

IgA nephropathy is a leading cause of primary glomerulonephritis worldwide, with a high prevalence and disease burden reported in East Asian populations, including China.

The cytokine APRIL is a key driver of IgA nephropathy pathogenesis, which is characterized by mesangial deposition of pathogenic Gd-IgA1-containing immune complexes.

In a prespecified IA of the phase 3 VISIONARY trial, sibeprenlimab, a selective APRIL blocker, significantly reduced proteinuria at 9 months (primary endpoint) and was generally well tolerated in patients with IgA nephropathy, establishing its efficacy and safety in a large global cohort of patients.

This study adds:

These results expand on the global VISIONARY trial findings regarding the evidence of proteinuria reduction with sibeprenlimab in patients from mainland China with IgA nephropathy.

Treatment with sibeprenlimab was well tolerated in patients from mainland China with IgA nephropathy, and safety results were consistent with those observed in a global patient population.

Potential impact:

The findings of this analysis support the role of selective APRIL blockade as a potential therapeutic approach in patients from China with IgA nephropathy.

This trial helps reinform the importance of therapy beyond supportive care in IgA nephropathy and provides region-specific evidence to inform clinical practice and patient management for patients with IgA nephropathy in China.

Current findings highlight the role of targeted approaches to immune-mediated kidney injury.

INTRODUCTION

Immunoglobulin A (IgA) nephropathy is a progressive immune-mediated chronic kidney disease and the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide, typically diagnosed in adults aged 20–40 years [1–3]. The majority of patients with IgA nephropathy may progress to kidney failure within 10–15 years of diagnosis [4]. Notably, even patients previously considered low risk (i.e. <1 g/d proteinuria) have a meaningful risk of progression [4].

IgA nephropathy has a heterogeneous clinical presentation and disease course, with higher prevalence and more aggressive disease observed in East Asian populations [5, 6]. In China, IgA nephropathy accounts for 30%–53% of primary glomerular diseases and is reported in up to 40% of patients undergoing renal biopsy [1, 7, 8]. Real-world clinical practice in China reflects a diversifying treatment landscape for IgA nephropathy, incorporating renin–angiotensin system (RAS) inhibitors, sodium–glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), mycophenolate mofetil, hydroxychloroquine, corticosteroids, tonsillectomy, targeted therapies, and traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) [9, 10]. Among these, corticosteroids and RAS inhibitors remain the cornerstone of care, with reported prescription rates of ∼70% [9]. Mycophenolate mofetil and hydroxychloroquine have demonstrated clinical benefit in China, although comparable evidence supporting their efficacy in patients from other regions remains limited [10]. Despite the use of widely adopted therapies and optimized supportive care, up to 24% of Chinese patients progress to kidney failure within 6 years [11].

IgA nephropathy is characterized by mesangial deposition of pathogenic galactose-deficient IgA1 (Gd-IgA1)-containing immune complexes, leading to proteinuria, an early marker of disease activity, and progressive kidney injury [12]. The 2025 Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes IgA nephropathy guideline [10] and the 2025 clinical practice guidelines for adults with IgA nephropathy in China [13] support managing the generic response to nephron loss and the specific underlying drivers to limit pathogenic IgA and immune complex-mediated kidney injury. This approach may be particularly relevant in China, where many patients continue to progress despite supportive care alone, suggesting that underlying immune-mediated disease drivers remain poorly addressed.

Sibeprenlimab is a humanized IgG2 monoclonal antibody that selectively blocks the cytokine a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL), a key driver of IgA nephropathy pathogenesis, that mediates antibody class switching in mature B cells and prolongs plasma cell survival, leading to the production of IgA and pathogenic Gd-IgA1 [2].

In the phase 2 ENVISION trial (NCT04287985), sibeprenlimab reduced proteinuria, stabilized estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), suppressed serum APRIL, and decreased Gd-IgA1 levels, with an acceptable safety profile [14, 15]. The phase 3 VISIONARY trial (NCT05248646) is an ongoing trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of sibeprenlimab in combination with supportive care to preserve kidney function in patients with IgA nephropathy [16]. In a prespecified interim analysis (IA) of the global VISIONARY trial, sibeprenlimab led to a 51.2% (P < .0001) placebo-adjusted reduction in 24-hour urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (uPCR-24h) at 9 months and was well tolerated [2]. These findings supported its accelerated approval by the US Food and Drug Administration (25 November 2025) for reduction of proteinuria in adults with primary IgA nephropathy at risk of disease progression [17].

Here, we report the interim efficacy and safety analysis for sibeprenlimab in the mainland China cohort of the VISIONARY trial.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Full methods, including the trial protocol and statistical analysis plan of the global VISIONARY trial, have been published previously [2].

Trial design and patients

VISIONARY is a multicentre, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled pivotal phase 3 trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of 400 mg of sibeprenlimab administered subcutaneously (SC) every 4 weeks for ∼24 months in adult patients with IgA nephropathy. The trial is being conducted at 240 sites across 31 countries [2], including 25 trial sites in China. It is sponsored by Otsuka Pharmaceutical Development and Commercialization and is overseen by an academic steering committee.

The protocol was approved by the institutional review board or ethics committee at each participating site before trial initiation (Supplemental Table S1). The study was conducted in accordance with the International Council for Harmonisation Guideline for Good Clinical Practice and the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients provided written informed consent.

Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive sibeprenlimab 400 mg SC or placebo every 4 weeks for up to 100 weeks (26 doses). As described previously [2] and presented in Fig. 1, the key eligibility criteria comprised adult patients with biopsy-confirmed IgA nephropathy, a uPCR-24h ≥0.75 g/g or 24-hour urine protein excretion ≥1.0 g/d, and a screening eGFR ≥30 ml/min/1.73 m2. Patients were required to be on a stable, maximally tolerated dose of angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor and/or angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) therapy for at least 3 months prior to screening. Use of a stable SGLT2i regimen initiated at least 3 months before screening was permitted. For trial sites in China, patients receiving TCM without immunosuppressive or immunomodulatory properties were eligible to participate at the discretion of the investigator. A list of permitted TCMs and further details on prohibited medications or therapies, including prohibited TCMs, are provided in the Supplementary material.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in uPCR-24h at 9 months. The key secondary endpoint of annualized eGFR slope over 24 months remains blinded to maintain trial integrity and is therefore not presented here. Secondary endpoints included safety and pharmacodynamic (PD) measures, including changes in serum IgA, IgG, and IgM. Exploratory endpoints comprised change in spot urine protein-to-creatinine ratio (spot uPCR), haematuria by dipstick, serum Gd-IgA1 level, serum concentrations of free APRIL, and proteinuric remission (urine total protein <0.5 g/d at 12 months).

Statistical analyses

For the China IA, no formal sample size calculation was performed. Interim efficacy and safety analyses were conducted in accordance with the statistical analysis plan, using the same statistical methods and intercurrent event handling as for the previously published global IA [2] (see Supplementary material for additional details). However, due to the small sample size of the datasets for the analyses, the stratification factors were removed, there was no type I error control for efficacy analysis, and supplementary, sensitivity, and subgroup analyses for the primary endpoint of uPCR-24h were not conducted. For the efficacy analysis, missing data were handled under a ‘missing at random’ assumption. Specifically, missing uPCR-24h values at month 9 were imputed within each treatment group using the multiple imputation approach. No analyses addressing intercurrent events were performed due to the small sample size.

The global cohort (n = 320) was defined as the IA cohort of the main global population (N = 510) and comprised the first 62.5% of patients randomized within the main global population who had the opportunity to complete the 9-month uPCR-24h assessment by the IA data cutoff (4 September 2024). Up to 51 patients from mainland China had the opportunity to complete the 9-month uPCR-24h assessment by the China IA data cutoff (3 January 2025). Of these, 42 patients who were part of the prespecified global analysis population were included in the mainland China cohort for the interim efficacy analysis reported here. Safety and PD analyses included all randomized patients from mainland China who received at least one dose of sibeprenlimab (N = 102). Figure 1 summarizes the analysis populations and study timeline; additional patient disposition details are provided in the Supplementary material.

An analysis of covariance model was used for the primary endpoint to compare the effect of treatment on uPCR-24h [2]. Spot analysis of uPCR was conducted with the use of a mixed model for repeated measures. Proteinuric remission was analyzed with a logistic regression model. Demographic and baseline characteristics, safety, and PD markers were summarized with descriptive statistics [2].

RESULTS

Patients

Overall, 156 patients were screened from mainland China, and 102 patients were randomized. The randomized population included 42 patients from China who were initially in the main global population and 60 patients enrolled through an extended China enrolment program that was initiated to ensure regional representation. The remaining 54 patients were not randomized because of screening failures (failed inclusion and exclusion criteria, 34.0%; other, 0.6%).

At the IA data cutoff, 42 of the 102 randomized patients (sibeprenlimab, n = 25; placebo, n = 17) were included in the China interim efficacy analysis; patients enrolled in the study extension were excluded. Among the 42 patients, 3 had discontinued treatment, due to patient choice (n = 2) or other reasons (n = 1) (Fig. S1).

Demographic and baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the sibeprenlimab and placebo groups and were similar to the global cohort (Table 1). The median age (range) was 36 (23–58) years, 52.4% of patients were female, 100% of patients received pretrial angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors and/or ARBs, and 47.6% received pretrial SGLT2i. The mean eGFR was 80.6 ml/min/1.73 m2, the median uPCR-24h was 1.3 g/g, and the median time from initial kidney biopsy to randomization was 1.9 years. Baseline kidney biopsy characteristics are available in Table S2.

Effects on proteinuria

At 9 months (week 40), a reduction of 63.1% [95% confidence interval (CI), 52.1% to 71.6%] from baseline in uPCR-24h was observed in sibeprenlimab-treated patients compared with a reduction of 3.1% (95% CI, −31.0% to 28.3%) in patients receiving placebo, corresponding to a placebo-adjusted reduction in uPCR-24h of 61.9% (95% CI, 43.2% to 74.5%). The antiproteinuric effect was consistent with, and numerically greater than, the 51.2% placebo-adjusted reduction in uPCR-24h (96.5% CI, 42.9% to 58.2%; P < .0001) observed in the global cohort [2] (Fig. 2A). At 12 months (week 52), the uPCR-24h decreased by 61.4% (95% CI, 48.1% to 71.3%) from baseline in patients receiving sibeprenlimab, compared with a 20.4% reduction (95% CI, −12.7% to 43.8%) in the placebo group, corresponding to a placebo-adjusted reduction of 51.5% (95% CI, 23.1% to 69.4%).

In the mainland China cohort, sibeprenlimab resulted in a rapid reduction in spot uPCR versus placebo at 4 weeks, with a substantial difference observed at 8 weeks that was sustained through month 12 (week 52). At month 9, patients receiving sibeprenlimab had a 65.2% reduction in spot uPCR from baseline (95% CI, 54.7% to 73.2%), compared with a 12.5% reduction with placebo (95% CI, −19.1% to 35.8%), corresponding to a placebo-adjusted reduction of 60.2% (95% CI, 40.3% to 73.4%; Fig. 2B).

The efficacy results for change in 24-hour urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio were consistent with those observed for proteinuria (Table S3).

Remission of proteinuria and resolution of haematuria

Remission of proteinuria (<0.5 g/d at 12 months) occurred in 6 of 14 patients (42.9%; 95% CI, 17.7% to 71.1%) who received sibeprenlimab compared with 1 of 11 patients (9.1%; 95% CI, 0.2% to 41.3%) who received placebo (Table S4). At baseline, 20 of 25 patients in the sibeprenlimab group (80%) and 16 of 17 patients who received placebo (94%) had haematuria (trace results or greater on a dipstick test). At week 48, the number of patients with haematuria had decreased to 4 of 18 in the sibeprenlimab group (22.2%) and 12 of 14 in the placebo group (85.7%), showing greater resolution of haematuria with sibeprenlimab (Fig. 3).

Biomarkers

Greater reductions in biomarker levels were observed with sibeprenlimab compared with placebo. Mean (standard deviation; SD) % reductions in serum Gd-IgA1 were 46.9% (15.2%) at week 4, 65.2% (12.7%) at week 24, and 67.3% (10.2%) at week 48 (Fig. 4A). Mean (SD) % reductions in APRIL levels were 92.1% (27.4%) at week 4 and 97.7% (0.8%) at week 48. Minimal changes were observed with placebo (Fig. 4B). At week 48, among patients receiving sibeprenlimab, mean (SD) serum IgA, IgG, and IgM levels decreased from baseline by 67.1% (7.7%), 32.0% (10.3%), and 74.4% (4.6%), respectively (Fig. S2). Minimal changes were observed with placebo.

Safety

At the IA data cutoff, the incidence of treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) in the safety population (N = 102) was comparable between groups (sibeprenlimab, 78.7%; placebo, 72.7%). Serious TEAEs were reported for 4.3% of patients in the sibeprenlimab group (none of which were treatment related) and 5.5% in the placebo group. No deaths were reported (Table 2).

TEAEs with an incidence ≥2% in the sibeprenlimab group were highest in the MedRA v27.0 system organ class of ‘infections and infestations’, occurring in 51.1% of patients in the sibeprenlimab group and 29.1% in the placebo group. The most commonly reported TEAE in both treatment groups was upper respiratory tract infection: 29.8% in the sibeprenlimab group (8.5% treatment related) and 18.2% in the placebo group (1.8% treatment related). In the sibeprenlimab group, the majority of these events were mild in severity (23.4%), and all resolved without any changes in sibeprenlimab treatment dose, with no incidences of potentially opportunistic infections, such as herpes and candidiasis.

Injection site reaction TEAEs were reported for 5 of 47 patients (10.6%) in the sibeprenlimab group and 3 of 55 patients (5.5%) in the placebo group. The most frequently reported injection site reaction TEAE was injection site pain (sibeprenlimab, 6.4%; placebo, 3.6%). All of the injection site reaction TEAEs were mild in severity. No patients in the sibeprenlimab group experienced postbaseline IgG <400 mg/dl, and accordingly, no infection-associated TEAEs were reported at these levels.

DISCUSSION

The management of IgA nephropathy in China remains challenging, with limited approved disease-modifying therapies and substantial residual risk of progression despite optimized supportive care [9, 18]. Consequently, many patients continue to experience persistent proteinuria and disease activity, reflecting the limitations of current treatment strategies. While the phase 3 VISIONARY trial demonstrated significant proteinuria reduction in the global cohort at 9 months and consistent treatment effects across multiple prespecified subgroups, region-specific evidence remains important given the known geographic and ethnic differences in the clinical course of IgA nephropathy. This subgroup analysis provides region-specific data from mainland China, demonstrating that the efficacy and safety profile of sibeprenlimab observed in the global IA is consistent in this population. These findings extend the global results to a region with high disease burden and differences in clinical characteristics and treatment practices, supporting the potential applicability of sibeprenlimab in this setting.

Baseline demographic and disease characteristics, including time since biopsy, were broadly comparable between the mainland China cohort and the global cohort, which may account in part for the similar efficacy and safety outcomes observed. An imbalance in sex distribution between treatment arms was observed in the China subgroup, with a higher proportion of females in the treatment arm than in the placebo arm. However, this likely reflects random variation, as sex was not a stratification factor and the subgroup sample was relatively small. Population pharmacokinetic analyses in the global cohort did not identify sex as a clinically meaningful covariate influencing exposure or the primary outcome, suggesting that this imbalance is unlikely to have materially impacted the treatment effect.

In this analysis, efficacy results in the mainland China cohort were consistent with those observed in the global cohort, with numerically greater antiproteinuric effects of sibeprenlimab at 9 months (61.9% vs 51.2%) [2]. While the findings of this IA offer no direct comparisons, the observed reduction in proteinuria may suggest a magnitude of effect broadly consistent with that for currently approved therapies in China, including budesonide [19], atrasentan [20], and iptacopan [21], and investigational therapies currently in phase 3 trials, such as telitacicept [22]. However, these findings are descriptive, and cross-trial comparisons are limited by differences in study design, patient populations, and endpoints.

Consistent with findings in the global cohort, sibeprenlimab demonstrated a rapid reduction in spot uPCR by week 4 compared with placebo, with the treatment effect sustained through month 12 [2]. At week 48, higher rates of haematuria resolution and proteinuric remission were also observed compared with placebo.

By week 48, treatment with sibeprenlimab was associated with marked reductions in Gd-IgA1 and with decreases in serum immunoglobulins, including IgA, IgG, and IgM levels, whereas minimal changes were observed with placebo. These effects were generally similar in magnitude and direction to those reported with other therapies targeting related pathways [23–26]. Despite reductions in biomarkers, the safety profile of sibeprenlimab showed no apparent evidence of adverse effects at the time of analysis. In addition, the biomarker responses were comparable to those of the global cohort, with near complete suppression of APRIL following the first administration of sibeprenlimab, which remained suppressed throughout the treatment period [2].

Sibeprenlimab was well tolerated, with an overall incidence of TEAEs comparable to the incidence with placebo, and low rates of serious adverse events, none of which were considered treatment related. The safety profile was consistent with that observed in the global cohort [2]. While infections were numerically more frequent in the sibeprenlimab group, these events were largely mild, most commonly upper respiratory tract infections, and resolved without dose modification. Overall, the safety profile is consistent with the selective mechanism of APRIL blockade, which primarily impacts later stages of B-cell development and plasma cell survival [27, 28].

In IgA nephropathy, haematuria may reflect injury to the glomerular filtration barrier caused by immune complex–mediated inflammation, and has been associated with kidney function decline and higher risk of disease progression [29, 30]. In this analysis, fewer patients treated with sibeprenlimab were positive for dipstick haematuria (trace or greater) compared with placebo (22.2% vs 85.7%) at week 48. These findings are also directionally consistent with the lower percentage of patients with dipstick-positive haematuria observed with sibeprenlimab in the global cohort. Together, these observations may provide additional evidence that APRIL inhibition with sibeprenlimab could attenuate active glomerular inflammation and downstream tubular injury beyond its established effects on proteinuria.

This analysis has several limitations. As the trial is ongoing, these data are interim and use proteinuria as a surrogate endpoint; therefore, longer follow-up (up to 24 months) is needed to fully characterize treatment effects on kidney function, including eGFR, consistent with current approaches in IgA nephropathy trials [19, 31, 32]. In addition, haematuria was assessed using dipstick testing, because urine microscopy data were limited. Microscopy, typically used as a confirmatory test, was available only in a subset of patients, and dipstick is a semiquantitative measure and subject to variability. Overall, these findings should be interpreted as early indicators of treatment effect, pending longer-term evaluation of eGFR slope and kidney outcomes.

CONCLUSIONS

The present findings at 9 months from the mainland China cohort of the VISIONARY trial align with those previously reported in the global cohort IA and support the consistency of the antiproteinuric effect of sibeprenlimab across diverse patient populations, including those from East Asia. Overall, sibeprenlimab was well tolerated in the mainland China cohort, with a safety profile that was generally consistent with the global cohort. Ongoing follow-up will further assess long-term safety and the key secondary efficacy outcome of annualized eGFR slope at 24 months.
