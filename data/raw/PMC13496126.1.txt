Objective

Frequent acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) are associated with poor prognosis, but objective and easily applicable predictors remain limited. This study aimed to investigate the association of Irisin, body mass index (BMI), and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) with frequent acute exacerbations in COPD and to develop a predictive nomogram.

Methods

This single-center retrospective-prospective observational study included 96 patients hospitalized with acute exacerbation of COPD (AECOPD) between March 2023 and March 2025. Frequent exacerbation was defined as ≥2 acute exacerbations or ≥1 hospitalization for AECOPD within one year. Patients were classified into the frequent exacerbation group (n=34) and non-frequent exacerbation group (n=62). Clinical data and serum Irisin levels were collected. Group comparisons, correlation analysis, univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed, and a nomogram was developed. Model performance was evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis, calibration curve, bootstrap internal validation, and decision curve analysis.

Results

Compared with the non-frequent exacerbation group, the frequent exacerbation group had lower Irisin and BMI levels and higher white blood cell count, neutrophil count, and SII. Multivariable logistic regression showed that Irisin (OR=0.981, 95% CI: 0.974–0.989) and BMI (OR=0.980, 95% CI: 0.963–0.998) were independently associated with a lower risk of frequent exacerbations, whereas lnSII (OR=1.117, 95% CI: 1.012–1.234) was associated with a higher risk. The combined model yielded an area under the ROC curve of 0.856 (95% CI: 0.780–0.933), showing better discrimination than Irisin alone. Calibration and decision curve analyses suggested favorable internal performance.

Conclusion

Irisin, BMI, and SII were associated with frequent acute exacerbations in COPD. The combined nomogram may provide a preliminary reference for risk stratification, but external validation is required before clinical application.

Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by persistent and irreversible airflow limitation.1 It is a prominent public health issue worldwide, ranking as the third leading cause of death and representing a common cause of emergency hospital admissions.2 Acute exacerbation of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (AECOPD) refers to a rapid deterioration of respiratory symptoms within a short period of time, which accelerates lung function decline, increases the risk of death, and aggravates the global healthcare burden.3,4 Frequent acute exacerbations are the key indicators of disease progression, poor prognosis, and increased medical burden in COPD patients.5,6 Early identification and intervention in high-risk populations are essential to current clinical management and research of COPD.

The role of metabolic disorders and chronic inflammatory imbalance in the occurrence and development of AECOPD has attracted increasing attention. Irisin is mainly secreted by skeletal muscle during physical activity.7 It has been reported to improve metabolic function, enhanced exercise tolerance, and anti-inflammatory effects, and may participate in the pathological processes of various chronic inflammatory diseases by regulating inflammatory signaling pathways and inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory factors.8–10 With its advantages, Irisin has become a promising biomarker in the COPD field.11 Our previous study showed that the level of Irisin in COPD patients was significantly lower,12 but its association with systemic inflammatory response and the risk of frequent acute exacerbations has not been clarified, which is the main focus of the present study.

In contrast to Irisin, BMI and SII are readily available in clinical settings, are also closely related to acute exacerbations of COPD. BMI, as a simple indicator of nutritional status, is closely associated with the risk of acute exacerbation of COPD:13 low BMI increases the risk of acute exacerbation, whereas higher BMI provides some protection for patients;14 a study in the Korean population showed that patients with frequent acute exacerbations generally have lower BMI, suggesting that low body weight may be useful in identifying high-risk populations.15 SII, as a comprehensive inflammatory marker,16 reflect the balance of systemic immune inflammation and is more stable and practical than single inflammatory indicators. Its elevated SII levels are closely associated with the occurrence, severity and adverse prognosis of AECOPD,17,18 and are positively correlated with the risk of rehospitalization,19 and an effective indicator for assessing the risk of acute exacerbation. Therefore, BMI and SII were selected because they are inexpensive, easily obtainable, and clinically practical indicators reflecting nutritional-metabolic status and systemic inflammation, respectively.

Although the GOLD guidelines recommend previous exacerbation history as an important basis for risk assessment, this approach has several limitations in real-world clinical practice. Complete information on prior exacerbations may be difficult to obtain because of the lack of a unified and interoperable medical record system, especially when patients visit different healthcare institutions. In addition, some patients may have limited understanding of acute exacerbations, and recall bias may affect the accuracy of self-reported exacerbation history. Other established predictors, such as blood eosinophil count and GOLD classification, are clinically important but may not fully reflect nutritional status, skeletal muscle-related metabolic dysfunction, and systemic immune inflammation. Therefore, an objective prediction model incorporating measurable indicators, such as Irisin, BMI, and SII, may provide complementary value for identifying patients at high risk of frequent acute exacerbations of COPD.

Based on the above background and clinical needs, this study aimed to construct a risk prediction model for frequent acute exacerbations of COPD by focusing on Irisin, SII and BMI. These three indicators may reflect the disease-related changes from three dimensions: metabolic anti-inflammation, nutritional metabolism, and immune inflammation. Compared with assessment based solely on previous exacerbation history, this combined model may provide complementary objective information for early identification of patients at high risk of acute exacerbations. Currently, studies on such combined prediction models remain limited. Therefore, this study aimed to clarify the association of Irisin, SII, and BMI with frequent acute exacerbations within one year, develop a preliminary prediction model, and provide a potential reference for clinical risk stratification.

Materials and Methods

General Information

Research Subjects

This was a single-center retrospective-prospective cohort study including 96 patients hospitalized with AECOPD from March 2023 to March 2025. Serum Irisin results for patients hospitalized before October 2024 were derived from a previous ethically approved laboratory project for serum collection and biomarker testing (Approval No. 2023-R505), which did not include research-related follow-up. The current study, including retrospective clinical data extraction, use of previously obtained Irisin results, supplementary one-year follow-up of historical patients, prospective recruitment, and follow-up of new patients, was independently approved in 2024 (Approval No. 2024-R701-N1-X1). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients who met the 2023 GOLD diagnostic criteria for AECOPD; (2) patients who were conscious and voluntarily provided written informed consent.

Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) concomitant respiratory diseases requiring treatment, including bronchial asthma, bronchiectasis, active pulmonary tuberculosis, acute pulmonary embolism, interstitial lung disease, and pneumothorax; (2) severe heart, brain, liver, kidney, hematological, or immune system diseases, or acute exacerbation of these diseases; (3) malignant tumors; (4) mental disorders or poor compliance due to various diseases; and (5) withdrawal from the study.

Research Methods

Collection of Clinical Data

Baseline clinical data were collected after admission, including gender, age, height, weight, smoking status, clinical comorbidities, and length of hospital stay. Smoking status was categorized as smoker and non-smoker; current smokers and former smokers were combined into the smoker group, whereas participants who had never smoked were classified as the non-smoker group. Information on inhaled maintenance therapy, vaccination status, treatment adherence, and pulmonary rehabilitation was obtained from medical records and follow-up interviews. All patients received regular inhaled maintenance therapy, completed recommended vaccinations, reported regular use of prescribed inhaled medications, and none underwent pulmonary rehabilitation during follow-up. Therefore, these variables were not included in the multivariable model because no between-patient variability was observed. Clinical laboratory data were also collected, including pulmonary function indicators [forced expiratory volume in 1 second/forced vital capacity (FEV1/FVC), forced expiratory volume in 1 second percentage of predicted value (FEV1%pred)], oxygenation index (PaO2/FiO2, OI), and blood routine parameters [white blood cell count (WBC), neutrophil count (NE), lymphocyte count (Ly), monocyte count (Mono), hemoglobin (Hgb), platelet count (PLT)], C-reactive protein (CRP), and D-Dimer.

ELISA Assay for Serum Irisin Concentration

Within 24 hours after admission, peripheral blood samples were centrifuged at 3000 rpm for 10 minutes at 4°C to separate serum. Then, serum Irisin level were measured using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kit (Cat. No.: E-EL-H5735) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Follow-Up and Grouping

All enrolled patients were followed up for one year. The occurrence of AECOPD, frequency of exacerbations, and rehospitalization status were recorded through outpatient visits, telephone follow-up, and review of hospital medical records. Patients with ≥2 acute exacerbation episodes or ≥1 hospitalization due to AECOPD within one year were assigned to the frequent exacerbation group, while those with <2 exacerbation episodes and no AECOPD-related hospitalization were assigned to the non-frequent exacerbation group.

Statistical Analysis and Graphing

Statistical analysis was performed using R software (version 4.3.0). Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median interquartile range [M (IQR)] according to the results of normality and homogeneity of variance tests. Between-group comparisons were performed using the independent-samples t-test or Mann–Whitney U-test, as appropriate. Categorical variables were compared using the χ2-test or Fisher’s exact test. Pearson correlation analysis was used for normally distributed variables, whereas Spearman correlation analysis was used for non-normally distributed variables. Univariate and multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to identify factors associated with frequent acute exacerbations, and the prediction model was constructed based on the results of multivariable logistic regression analysis. Model discrimination, calibration, and clinical utility were evaluated using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, calibration curve, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling with 1000 repetitions. A two-sided P value <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Multivariable Logistic Regression Analysis of Frequent Acute Exacerbations in COPD Patients Within 1 Year

Variables were selected for multivariable logistic regression based on clinical relevance, previous literature, univariate analysis, and correlation analysis. To avoid overfitting due to the limited number of outcome events, highly collinear inflammatory variables were not entered simultaneously. Finally, Irisin, BMI, FEV1%pred, and lnSII were included. The results showed that Irisin (OR = 0.982, 95% CI: 0.975–0.990, P < 0.001) and BMI (OR = 0.980, 95% CI: 0.963–0.998, P = 0.030) were both independently associated with a lower risk. This indicated that for every 1-unit increase in Irisin level, the risk of frequent acute exacerbations in COPD patients decreased by approximately 1.8%; for every 1-unit increase in BMI, the risk decreased by approximately 2.0%. lnSII (OR = 1.117, 95% CI: 1.012–1.234, P = 0.028) was statistically significant as an independently associated with a higher risk, suggesting that for every 1-unit increase in lnSII level, the risk of frequent acute exacerbations in COPD patients increased by approximately 11.7%. Establishment of a Combined Prediction Model Using Irisin, BMI and SII

Based on the results of the multivariable logistic regression analysis, a combined prediction model incorporating Irisin, BMI and SII was constructed. The area under the ROC curve (AUC) of Irisin alone was 0.800 (95% CI: 0.706–0.894), with a cut-off value of 19.64 ng/mL, a sensitivity of 0.882, a specificity of 0.661, and a Youden index of 0.543. The AUC of the combined prediction model of Irisin, BMI and SII was 0.856 (95% CI: 0.780–0.933). The sensitivity of the combined prediction model was 0.912, the specificity was 0.645, and the Youden index was 0.557. Discussion

COPD is a highly heterogeneous disease, and acute exacerbations directly affect patient prognosis and contribute substantially to increased medical burden.20 BMI reflects nutritional and metabolic status and is strongly associated with COPD prognosis. SII integrates neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts and reflects systemic immune-inflammation using routine blood test parameters. Irisin was selected as the main biomarker of interest because of its potential role in skeletal muscle metabolism and anti-inflammatory regulation. This study constructed a combined prediction model based on Irisin, BMI, and SII. The model integrates inflammatory, nutritional-metabolic, and skeletal muscle-related metabolic dimensions, providing complementary information for the early identification of patients at high risk of frequent acute exacerbations. The results showed that low Irisin levels, low BMI, and high SII were associated with frequent acute exacerbations within one year in COPD patients. Moreover, the combined model showed better predictive performance than Irisin alone, suggesting that multidimensional assessment may be useful for risk stratification in COPD management.

The role of Irisin in the pathophysiology of COPD has received increasing attention.21,22 Multivariable regression analysis in this study showed that Irisin was independently associated with a lower risk for frequent acute exacerbations. Correlation analysis further showed that Irisin was negatively correlated with SII and positively correlation with Ly, suggesting that low Irisin levels may be accompanied by reduced anti-inflammatory capacity and increased susceptibility to acute exacerbations. This finding is consistent with previous studies. Irisin has been reported to improves exercise tolerance and exert anti-inflammatory effects by regulating inflammatory signaling pathways and inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory factors.12,23 In the present study, Irisin level was significantly lower in the frequent exacerbation group, which may reflect skeletal muscle dysfunction and reduced exercise tolerance. Low Irisin levels may further impair the regulation of inflammatory responses, thereby aggravating airway and systemic inflammation. Compared with traditional inflammatory markers, Irisin may integrate information on metabolic function and immune regulation, providing a new perspective for understanding extrapulmonary systemic involvement in COPD and supporting its potential roles as an exploratory biomarker.

Nutritional and metabolic status is closely related to the risk of acute exacerbation of COPD. In this study, BMI was significantly lower in the frequent exacerbation group than in the non-frequent exacerbation group, and higher BMI was independently associated with a lower risk of frequent acute exacerbations. COPD patients are often in a hypermetabolic state accompanied by increased protein catabolism. Low BMI may indicate insufficient nutritional reserves and reduced respiratory muscle function, which can impair ventilatory capacity and immune function, thereby increasing susceptibility to infection and acute exacerbation.24 Correlation analysis showed that FEV1/FVC was positively correlated with BMI, and negatively correlated with CRP and NE, further suggesting a potential interaction among nutritional status, lung function impairment, and systemic inflammation. Previous studies have also shown that BMI is generally lower in patients with AECOPD, suggesting that nutritional support may be an important component of exacerbation risk management.15 This finding may be partly explained by the obesity paradox in COPD, as lower BMI often reflects poor nutritional reserve and reduced respiratory muscle function, whereas relatively higher BMI may provide greater physiological reserve. However, BMI cannot distinguish fat mass from skeletal muscle mass; therefore, this association should be interpreted cautiously.

Systemic inflammatory response is one of the important mechanisms involved in acute exacerbation of COPD. SII, as a novel composite inflammatory indicator, reflects the systemic immune-inflammatory state of the body.25 In this study, SII was significantly higher in patients with frequent exacerbations and was independent associated with an increased risk of frequent acute exacerbations. Compared with single inflammatory indicators, SII may more comprehensively reflect immune imbalance by integrating neutrophil, lymphocyte, and platelet counts, thereby capturing both innate immune activation and adaptive immune suppression.26 Previous studies have shown that SII is associated with the occurrence, progression, and prognosis of AECOPD. High SII levels often indicate a high inflammatory burden and immune dysregulation, which may increase the risk of disease deterioration and rehospitalization.18,27 This present study further suggests that SII, as a stable, easily obtainable, and low-cost indicator, may help identify immune-inflammatory imbalance in COPD patients.

Based on the above results, this study constructed a prediction model incorporating Irisin, BMI, and SII. The AUC of the combined model was 0.856, indicating good discrimination. The nomogram also showed favorable calibration. DCA showed that the model provided a positive net benefit within the threshold probability range of 0.01 to 0.50, suggesting potential clinical utility for risk stratification. However, given the exploratory nature of this study and the lack of external validation, the model should be regarded as preliminary and requires further validation before clinical application.

However, the clinical application of Irisin remains limited. Unlike BMI and SII, Irisin is not routinely measured in clinical practice and currently requires specific laboratory assays, such as enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Differences in assay kits, sample handling, detection platforms, and clinical cut-off values may affect comparability across studies. Therefore, although Irisin is a promising biomarker, its clinical implementation requires further assay standardization, cost-effectiveness evaluation, and validation in independent populations.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective-prospective observational study with a relatively small sample size and no formal a priori sample size or power calculation, the statistical power and generalizability of the findings may be limited, and selection bias cannot be excluded. Although the final model included three predictors with 34 outcome events, the findings should still be interpreted as exploratory. Second, several important clinical confounders were not included in the present model. Previous exacerbation history could not be reliably quantified because of potential patient recall bias and incomplete interoperability of medical records between lower-level hospitals and tertiary hospitals. Chronic bronchitis phenotype was not consistently documented, and blood eosinophil count was not included in the original pre-specified dataset. These factors may have introduced residual confounding. Third, other potential confounding factors, such as bacterial infection and environmental exposure, were not included, and Irisin levels were not dynamically monitored; therefore, changes in Irisin levels at different stages of the disease could not be evaluated. Finally, although bootstrap internal validation was performed, external validation in an independent cohort was not conducted, and the predictive performance of the model may therefore have been overestimated. Future multicenter prospective studies with larger sample sizes should incorporate established clinical predictors, dynamically assess Irisin levels, and perform external validation to further verify and optimize the model.

In conclusion, Irisin, BMI, and SII were closely associated with the risk of frequent acute exacerbation risk in COPD patients. The combined nomogram incorporating these three indicators showed good internal predictive performance and may provide a potential reference for risk stratification. However, further external validation in independent cohorts is required before clinical application.

Ethics Approval and Consent to Participate

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Second Hospital of Hebei Medical University (Approval No.: 2024-R701-N1-X1). This was a retrospective-prospective observational study. Serum Irisin results for patients enrolled before October 2024 were derived from a previous ethically approved laboratory project involving serum collection and biomarker testing (Approval No.: 2023-R505). For these patients, only routine clinical data from existing medical records were retrospectively collected before the current ethical approval, and no research-related follow-up was performed before approval. Prospective follow-up and subsequent data collection were conducted only after ethical approval was obtained. All participants provided written informed consent.
