Aluminum phosphide poisoning is a highly lethal toxicological emergency associated with an extremely high mortality rate, and no specific antidote is currently available. Aluminum phosphide rapidly releases phosphine gas after coming into contact with gastric acid, which strongly inhibits mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase, leading to severe cellular metabolic disorder, lactic acidosis, refractory shock, and progressive multiple organ dysfunction syndrome. In severe cases, the mortality rate can approach 100%. We report the case of a man in his early 40s who intentionally ingested 25.2вҖ…g of aluminum phosphide, equivalent to approximately 16 times the human lethal dose (based on a lethal dose of 1.5вҖ…g). The patient rapidly developed fulminant multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, including refractory cardiogenic shock, acute respiratory distress syndrome, acute kidney injury, liver dysfunction, hypocalcemia, and secondary ventilator-associated pneumonia caused by Citrobacter koseri infection. He received a series of timely critical interventions, including ultra-early gastric lavage with liquid paraffin, sequential hemoperfusion, continuous venovenous hemofiltration, mechanical ventilation with prone positioning, circulatory support, corticosteroids, and targeted antimicrobial therapy. The patient achieved complete clinical recovery and was discharged 18 days after admission. No organ or neurological sequelae were observed at the 1-month follow-up. This rare and successful rescue demonstrates that even ultra-severe aluminum phosphide poisoning can be reversed with standardized, sequential, and comprehensive critical care support without relying on advanced life-support devices such as extracorporeal membrane oxygenation. This study provides a practical and valuable therapeutic strategy for the management of severe aluminum phosphide poisoning in resource-limited clinical settings.

Introduction

Pesticide poisoning remains a major global public health problem, particularly in agricultural and developing regions. Worldwide, approximately 300,000 individuals die annually from acute pesticide poisoning, with organophosphorus compounds and phosphides being the most common fatal agents. 1 Among these substances, aluminum phosphide (AlP) is widely used as a low-cost grain fumigant and rodenticide owing to its strong insecticidal and deratization effects.1,2 However, its high toxicity, easy accessibility, and lack of restricted management have made it one of the most common agents used for intentional self-poisoning in many countries.2,3 Upon ingestion, AlP reacts immediately with gastric acid to release phosphine gas (PHвӮғ), a potent cytotoxic toxin that inhibits mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase, blocks cellular respiration, and triggers severe lactic acidosis and progressive multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS). 1 The lethal dose in humans is only 1вҖ“1.5вҖ…g, and mortality in severe cases approaches 100%.4,5 Clinical features include refractory cardiogenic shock, acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), acute kidney injury, hepatocellular damage, and life-threatening arrhythmias.6,7 To date, no specific antidote is available, and clinical management fully relies on ultra-early gastrointestinal decontamination, sequential blood purification, targeted hemodynamic support, and multiorgan supportive care. Secondary complications such as ventilator-associated pneumonia (VAP) further worsen the clinical prognosis, especially in resource-limited grassroot medical centers. 8

In recent years, high-quality randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and systematic meta-analyses from high-burden countries such as Egypt and Iran have provided robust evidence-based support for the stratified, standardized management of acute AlP poisoning. Regarding gastrointestinal decontamination, a single-blind RCT performed by Helal et al. demonstrated that gastric lavage using liquid paraffin combined with sodium bicarbonate formed a hydrophobic film over residual AlP particles in the stomach, thereby suppressing sustained phosphine release. 9 This intervention significantly reduced blood lactate levels, stabilized vital signs, decreased the need for vasopressors and mechanical ventilation, and improved 72-hour survival compared with conventional water lavage. In a three-arm RCT, Abdelhamid et al. concurrently measured mitochondrial and apoptotic biomarkers, including cytochrome c oxidase, caspases, and catalase, and confirmed that liquid paraffin exerts superior antioxidant and anti-apoptotic effects over L-carnitine, with both adjunctive strategies significantly reducing in-hospital mortality. 10 A systematic meta-analysis of seven RCTs by De Santi et al., which pooled data from 226 patients, corroborated that oil-based gastric lavage reduced the risk of death from AlP poisoning by 38% and concurrently decreased the need for mechanical ventilation, whereas coconut oil demonstrated only a marginal protective effect with low evidence certainty and inconsistent efficacy. 11 In patients with phosphine-induced refractory cardiogenic shock, a placebo-controlled RCT by Adel et al. demonstrated that insulinвҖ“euglycemia therapy improves myocardial contractility and corrects severe metabolic acidosis, reducing mortality in critically ill patients from 96.3% to 35.2% while substantially decreasing vasopressor requirements and the rate of endotracheal intubation. 12 Furthermore, a retrospective intensive care unit (ICU) cohort study by Lashin et al. proposed a novel prognostic algorithm, validating that two bedside parameters, central venous pressure (CVP) and mean arterial pressure-to-CVP ratio, enable rapid prediction of mortality and mechanical ventilation risk without complex laboratory testing, thereby facilitating early risk stratification in critical care settings. 13 Collectively, these advances provided a theoretical rationale and practical guidance for the comprehensive strategy employed for our patient, which included ultra-early liquid paraffin gastric lavage, sequential hemoperfusion, insulin therapy, and goal-directed hemodynamic monitoring.

Most published case reports only document patients ingesting less than fivefold lethal AlP doses with partial organ recovery, whereas survivors of 16-fold lethal doses combined with concurrent MODS and secondary bacterial VAP are extremely rare. We present the case of a patient with extensive lethal-dose AlP poisoning complicated by multiorgan failure and Citrobacter koseri VAP who was successfully rescued. This case highlights the life-saving value of ultra-early paraffin oil decontamination, sequential dual blood purification, and individualized multiorgan support strategies in resource-limited ICU settings.

Case report

A man in his early 40s (height, 180вҖ…cm; weight, 90вҖ…kg) with no prior chronic medical illnesses presented to the emergency department of the 81st Group Army Hospital (No. 13 Jianguo Road, Zhangjiakou City, Hebei Province) approximately 40вҖ…min after intentional self-ingestion of AlP tablets that were fully dissolved in clean water during an acute emotional distress episode in early 2026. He ingested a total of 15 AlP tablets, each weighing 3вҖ…g with a purity of 56%, resulting in a total ingested dose of 25.2вҖ…g, equivalent to 16вҖ“25 times the reported human lethal dose (1.0вҖ“1.5вҖ…g). Attempts at self-induced vomiting were unsuccessful, and the patient rapidly developed progressive dizziness, oppressive chest tightness, shortness of breath, and an unsteady gait, indicating early systemic toxicity due to phosphine gas absorption.

On admission, the patient was fully alert with a Glasgow Coma Scale score of 15; however, he appeared markedly anxious and hemodynamically unstable. Vital signs were as follows: blood pressure, 95/70 mmHg; heart rate, 112 beats/min; respiratory rate, 26 breaths/min; and peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2), 94% while breathing room air. A 12-lead electrocardiogram demonstrated diffuse ST-segment depression and symmetric T-wave inversion across the precordial and lateral leads, consistent with severe phosphine-induced myocardial ischemia and subendocardial injury (Figure 1). Arterial blood gas analysis revealed severe metabolic acidosis with pH = 7.223, bicarbonate (HCOвӮғвҲ’) = 14.4вҖ…mmol/L, and a critically elevated blood lactate level of 13.15вҖ…mmol/L, indicating profound cellular hypoxia and mitochondrial dysfunction. His serum aluminum level was detectable biochemically, confirming the systemic absorption of AlP. Initial liver function test results were normal; however, renal function was already impaired, consistent with early toxic organ injury (Table 1).

Clinical course and interventions

Upon arrival, ultra-early gastric decontamination was performed immediately using 200вҖ…mL of liquid paraffin to coat residual AlP tablets and halt further phosphine gas production, followed by activated charcoal adsorption and mannitol-induced catharsis to minimize ongoing toxin absorption. The first session of hemoperfusion was promptly initiated to actively eliminate circulating lipophilic phosphine and aluminum ions. Concomitant supportive care included aggressive fluid resuscitation with a total of 3500вҖ…mL of crystalloids (normal saline and Ringer's lactate) over the first 24вҖ…h, intravenous norepinephrine infusion (starting at 0.5вҖ…Вөg/kg/min, titrated up to 1.2вҖ…Вөg/kg/min) for hemodynamic support, proton-pump inhibitors for gastric mucosal protection, antioxidant therapy, prophylactic antimicrobial administration, and targeted electrolyte correction including potassium replacement. To reverse severe phosphine-mediated metabolic acidosis, 1500вҖ…mL of 5% sodium bicarbonate (equivalent to approximately 893 mEq bicarbonate) was continuously infused intravenously over the initial 24-hour resuscitation period. Infusion adjustments were performed according to repeated arterial blood gas results, instead of divided 50 mEq bolus injections. Within a few hours of admission, the patient's clinical status deteriorated acutely, with the development of acute hypoxemic respiratory failure and progressive somnolence. Emergency endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation were initiated to secure the airway and support gas exchange. Despite these interventions, the blood lactate level further increased to 14.75вҖ…mmol/L, accompanied with acute hepatocellular injury and significant leukocytosis. A definitive diagnosis of MODS was established, characterized by acute kidney injury, overt myocardial damage, refractory lactic acidosis, and hypocalcemia. Continuous venovenous hemofiltration was promptly initiated for sustained organ support, metabolic stabilization, and cytokine clearance. Bedside echocardiography was not performed because of limited resources and the patient's unstable condition; however, clinical assessment, continuous ECG monitoring, and serial cardiac enzyme measurements were used to guide cardiovascular management.

Chest computed tomography (CT) performed on day 3 revealed bilateral diffuse ground-glass opacities (Figure 2(a)), consistent with acute lung injury and ARDS secondary to phosphine toxicity. High-dose intravenous methylprednisolone and prone positioning ventilation were administered to reduce pulmonary inflammation and improve oxygenation. A second session of hemoperfusion was conducted to eliminate residual toxins, after which, gradual clinical and biochemical improvements were observed.

The patient was successfully weaned off of mechanical ventilation and extubated on hospital day 4. However, follow-up chest CT demonstrated worsening bilateral pulmonary infiltrates on hospital day 6 (Figure 2(b)), and a subsequent sputum culture identified C. koseri, confirming the diagnosis of VAP. Antimicrobial therapy was promptly adjusted according to the microbiological culture and antimicrobial susceptibility results. A repeat ECG performed on day 5 showed significant improvement, with resolution of the previous STвҖ“T wave changes and restoration of normal sinus rhythm (Figure 3). With targeted antibiotic treatment, continued supportive care, and sequential organ monitoring, the pulmonary infiltrates gradually resolved (Figure 2(c)). Over the entire hospitalization (18 days), the total intravenous fluid volume was approximately 33.8 L, comprising 31.2 L of crystalloids (normal saline, dextrose solutions, Ringer's lactate, amino acidвҖ“glucose electrolyte mixtures, and lipid emulsions) and 2.55 L of colloids (succinylated gelatin and human albumin). Vasopressor support during the first 72вҖ…h consisted of dopamine (total 800вҖ…mg), metaraminol (40вҖ…mg), and norepinephrine (6вҖ…mg), all administered as continuous infusions using a central venous catheter and titrated to maintain a mean arterial pressure above 65 mmHg. No other inotropes (e.g. dobutamine and adrenaline) were used. Antioxidant therapy was administered throughout the course, comprising vitamin C (total dose of 30.5вҖ…g, 2вҖ…g per day) and cytochrome C (total dose of 1260вҖ…mg, 60вҖ…mg per day) for 21 days. The patient achieved full clinical recovery and was discharged from the hospital on day 18 post-admission, with near-normalization of all biochemical markers (Table 1). At the 1-month outpatient follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic, with no evidence of residual organ dysfunction or neurological sequelae.

Discussion

AlP poisoning is a highly lethal toxicological emergency in developing countries with mortality rates ranging from 30% to 100%. The primary mechanism of AlP toxicity is the release of phosphine gas, which strongly inhibits mitochondrial cytochrome c oxidase, leading to disordered cellular energy metabolism, refractory shock, and MODS. 2 Currently, there is no specific antidote for AlP poisoning, and treatment mainly relies on early gastric lavage, hemodynamic support, and comprehensive organ support.14,15

In previous reports, most patients with severe AlP poisoning died within 24вҖ“48вҖ…h because of irreversible shock and respiratory circulatory failure.2,16 Even with intensive care, the survival rate among patients who consumed more than five times the lethal dose remains extremely low.6,17 Our patient ingested approximately 16 times the lethal dose of AlP and had severe MODS; however, he finally achieved complete recovery, which has rarely been reported in the literature. Compared with the cases reported by Gebray et al. and Mantzke et al., this case had important distinguishing characteristics.2,18 First, the toxic dose was higher; second, the patient developed severe MODS in the early stage; and third, the patient was successfully treated without extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) or other advanced equipment. In reports by Bogale et al. and Chacko et al., patients with similar AlP doses mostly died quickly because of intractable shock and malignant arrhythmias.19,20 In the present case, ultra-early gastric lavage with liquid paraffin, sequential hemoperfusion, and continuous renal replacement therapy effectively interrupted the toxic process and reversed progressive organ damage. Moreover, AlP poisoning often leads to delayed acute lung injury and an increased risk of secondary infection, which are important factors affecting prognosis. 21 Our patient developed aggravated ARDS and VAP caused by C. koseri after initial stabilization, which further increased the treatment-related challenges. Similar delayed pulmonary deterioration and infectious complications have been reported in recent studies, indicating that close monitoring and timely anti-infection treatment are essential, even after the early critical phase. 17 In most primary and resource-limited medical settings, advanced equipment such as ECMO and intra-aortic balloon pumps (IABPs) are generally unavailable. This case confirms that standardized basic critical care, including early decontamination, sequential blood purification, and precise organ support, is sufficient to rescue patients with ultra-severe AlP poisoning.

However, this was a single-case report, and its findings cannot be generalized to all patients with ultra-severe AlP poisoning. Bedside echocardiography was unavailable in this case, which prevented the quantitative evaluation of cardiac function and targeted titration of inotropic agents; nevertheless, clinical indicators and electrocardiographic monitoring served as adequate alternative assessment tools. Furthermore, the patient received all treatments in a resource-limited environment without ECMO or IABPs; therefore, the management strategy described herein cannot fully represent the complete treatment modalities accessible in well-resourced advanced medical centers. Despite these limitations, our clinical experience indicates that rigorous and standardized basic critical care interventions can save lives even without high-end life-support equipment.

Taken together, complete recovery following ultra-severe AlP poisoning can be achieved without advanced life-support devices. In regions with relatively insufficient medical resources, patients with severe poisoning can be effectively treated through early gastric decontamination, timely blood purification, individualized circulatory support, and serial monitoring of multiorgan function. This clinical experience provides valuable practical references for the management of severe AlP poisoning in low-resource settings.
