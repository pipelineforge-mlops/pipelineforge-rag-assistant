Introduction

We aimed to develop and validate a clinically interpretable multimodal deep learning framework integrating multi-sequence MRI and clinical variables for predicting overall survival (OS) in locally advanced cervical squamous cell carcinoma (LACSCC).

Methods

This retrospective cohort study included 247 patients with pathologically confirmed LACSCC. Pretreatment MRI sequences, including contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (T1CE), T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), were analyzed. Tumor regions of interest were manually delineated, and slice-level deep features were extracted using a fine-tuned ResNet-50 network. A graph neural network was then constructed to model spatial relationships among MRI slices, aggregate slice-level features into patient-level representations, and integrate imaging features with clinical variables to generate a Deep Learning Prognostic Score (DLPS). Model performance was assessed using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, Cox regression analysis, and nomogram calibration.

Results

In the validation cohort, the model achieved area under the ROC curve values of 0.747, 0.749, and 0.706 for predicting 1-year, 3-year, and 5-year OS, respectively. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed significantly better OS in the high-DLPS group than in the low-DLPS group. Multivariable Cox regression analysis identified DLPS as an independent predictor of OS. The nomogram integrating DLPS and significant clinical variables showed good calibration in both the training and validation cohorts.

Conclusion

We developed and validated a multimodal MRI-based GNN framework for individualized OS prediction in LACSCC. By integrating spatially structured imaging features with clinical variables, the model provided effective prognostic stratification and may support personalized risk assessment and treatment decision-making.

Introduction

Cervical cancer continues to pose a major global health burden and remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality among women worldwide. 1 Despite continuous advances in screening and treatment, a considerable proportion of patients are still diagnosed initially at a locally advanced stage. 2 Compared with early-stage disease, LACC is associated with substantially worse survival outcomes and greater prognostic heterogeneity. A large population-based evaluation of the 2018 FIGO staging system demonstrated a progressive decline in survival with increasing disease stage. 3 Moreover, despite advances in concurrent chemoradiotherapy and image-guided adaptive brachytherapy, nodal and systemic failures remain major barriers to further improving survival in LACC. 4 In the INTERLACE phase III trial, the 5-year overall survival rate was 72% among patients treated with standard chemoradiotherapy alone, further indicating that a substantial proportion of patients remain at risk of recurrence or death after standard treatment. 5 Therefore, individualized pretreatment prognostic stratification in this population is clinically important.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) plays a central role in the clinical management of cervical cancer, particularly in disease staging, treatment planning, and response assessment. In patients with LACC, pretreatment MRI can help evaluate tumor size, local extension, adjacent organ invasion, lymph node status, and intratumoral heterogeneity, which are closely associated with tumor burden, treatment response, and prognosis.6,7 Multiparametric MRI sequences, including contrast-enhanced T1-weighted imaging (T1CE), T2-weighted imaging (T2WI), and diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), provide complementary information regarding tumor morphology, cellularity, and the tumor microenvironment. 8 However, conventional MRI assessment still mainly relies on qualitative interpretation and a limited number of quantitative metrics, which may be inadequate for fully characterizing tumor heterogeneity associated with long-term prognosis.7,9,10 In recent years, radiomics- and deep learning-based approaches have been increasingly applied to extract high-dimensional features from MRI, showing considerable potential for predicting treatment response and survival outcomes. 11 Nevertheless, most existing approaches either treat each MRI slice as an independent sample or aggregate slice-level features using simple strategies, such as average pooling or max pooling.12-15 These approaches often fail to account for the anatomical spatial continuity between adjacent slices and the complex intratumoral topological relationships, leading to the loss of important three-dimensional spatial information and consequently limiting the accuracy of prognostic prediction. 16

A graph neural network (GNN), as a powerful framework for modeling non-Euclidean data, offers a promising approach to overcome these limitations. 17 Unlike conventional convolutional neural networks or simple pooling strategies that treat slices as independent and discard inter-slice spatial context, GNN explicitly capture dependencies among data points through nodes and edges. 18 In medical image analysis, sequential MRI slices from an individual patient can be naturally represented as a graph, where each slice serves as a node and edges connect adjacent slices according to their anatomical order. This formulation enables a GNN to effectively aggregate slice-level local features while preserving spatial continuity, and the graph attention mechanism further allows adaptive weighting of prognostically informative slices during patient-level representation learning. 19 Moreover, multimodal fusion of imaging features with clinicopathological information, such as stage and pathological biomarkers, has been shown to further enhance predictive performance. 20 However, studies applying GNN to integrate the spatial features of multi-sequence MRI with clinical information for prognostic prediction in cervical cancer remain scarce.

Therefore, this study aimed to develop and validate a clinically interpretable, MRI-based multimodal deep learning framework for prognostic prediction in locally advanced cervical squamous cell carcinoma (LACSCC). The framework used GNN to model the spatial topological relationships among MRI slices and integrated multisequence imaging features with clinical variables to predict overall survival (OS).

Methods

Study Cohort

This retrospective study included 247 patients with LACSCC treated at Zhangzhou Hospital, Fujian Province, between June 2020 and May 2025. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) histologically confirmed cervical squamous cell carcinoma; (2) locally advanced disease (stages IB2-IVA) according to the 2018 FIGO staging system; (3) completion of definitive radiotherapy; (4) availability of pretreatment multiparametric MRI data; and (5) complete clinical and survival follow-up data. Patients with a history of pelvic malignancy, prior pelvic radiotherapy, incomplete follow-up data, or poor image quality were excluded. All consecutive patients who met the prespecified inclusion and exclusion criteria between June 2020 and May 2025 were included. The final cohort consisted of 247 patients, including 38 OS events. Patients were randomly divided into a training cohort (n = 172; 26 OS events) and an internal validation cohort (n = 75; 12 OS events) using stratified sampling according to OS status. Because this was a retrospective single-center study, no formal a priori sample size calculation was performed. The limited number of outcome events was considered when specifying the multivariable models and is acknowledged as an important limitation. All patients received definitive radiotherapy consisting of pelvic external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) followed by high-dose-rate brachytherapy (HDR-BT). EBRT was delivered using intensity-modulated radiotherapy (IMRT) or volumetric modulated arc therapy (VMAT) techniques, with the clinical target volume encompassing the primary tumor, uterus, parametria, upper vagina, and regional lymph node drainage areas; elective para-aortic irradiation was performed when indicated. The EBRT dose to the elective nodal regions was 45–50 Gy in 25–28 fractions (5 fractions per week), with a simultaneous integrated boost to gross lymph node metastases (up to 60 Gy) and to the primary cervical tumor (up to 54 Gy). HDR-BT was delivered at a total dose of 28–30 Gy. Concurrent chemotherapy was predominantly cisplatin-based, with nedaplatin, carboplatin, or paclitaxel substituted when cisplatin was contraindicated. Nimotuzumab (200 mg weekly for 6 cycles) was administered concurrently with radiotherapy in eligible patients.

The collected clinical variables included age, FIGO stage, EGFR status, HER2 status, PD-L1 CPS, pretreatment tumor volume, uterine corpus invasion, vaginal invasion, bladder invasion, rectal invasion, lymph node metastasis, concurrent chemotherapy, neoadjuvant chemotherapy, concurrent nimotuzumab, immunotherapy, and OS. These clinicopathological factors were selected based on their established prognostic relevance in cervical cancer and their routine availability in clinical practice. 21 Performance status and comorbidity data were not consistently documented in the electronic medical records and therefore could not be reliably ascertained for this retrospective cohort. Age was dichotomized at 65 years according to common clinical practice. Tumor volume was categorized using 50 cm3 as a predefined clinically interpretable threshold. Immunohistochemical markers were categorized according to the institutional pathological reporting system: EGFR was grouped as low expression and high expression, HER2 as IHC 0/1+ and IHC 2+/3+ (Detailed IHC protocols are provided in the Supplementary Material), and PD-L1 according to CPS categories (evaluated by Combined Positive Score [CPS] via the 22C3 pharmDx assay). Missing biomarker data were recorded as “unknown”.

The reporting of this study conforms to the TRIPOD + AI statement. 22

MRI Acquisition and Regions of Interest (ROI) Delineation

All patients underwent pretreatment pelvic MRI, including T1CE, T2WI, and DWI sequences. MRI examinations were performed on a 1.5T scanner (Optima® MR360, GE Healthcare) using a standard head coil. Detailed acquisition parameters are provided in the Supplementary Material. The tumor regions on axial images were manually delineated by two experienced clinicians who were blinded to the patients’ clinical outcomes. The delineated tumor regions were defined as ROIs. The segmentation results were independently reviewed to ensure consistency and accuracy. An ROI-guided image patch extraction strategy was then applied to focus the analysis on tumor-relevant regions. Specifically, axial images were partitioned into fixed-size patches, and only those overlapping with the tumor ROIs were retained. To minimize background noise and improve the signal-to-noise ratio, patches with less than 10% tumor area were excluded.

Deep Learning-Based MRI Feature Extraction

To capture subtle phenotypic characteristics of tumors across different imaging sequences, a fine-tuned ResNet-50 deep convolutional neural network was used as the feature extractor. For each patient, the three-dimensional MRI data, including DWI, T1CE, and T2WI sequences, were decomposed into a series of two-dimensional axial slices and fed into the network. The network had been optimized for survival prediction during the pretraining stage. During feature extraction, the final classification layer was removed, and the 2048-dimensional feature vectors from the penultimate layer were extracted. Through this process, each patient’s imaging data were transformed into a high-dimensional feature matrix, in which each row represented the deep semantic representation of an individual MRI slice. This approach effectively converted unstructured imaging data into structured tensors for subsequent modeling.

Graph Construction and Clinical Data Preprocessing

For categorical clinical variables, including age, stage, EGFR, and HER2, missing values were imputed using Multivariate Imputation based on the Iterative Imputer. For the imaging data, the slice-level features extracted in the previous stage were modeled as a graph structure. Specifically, each slice-level feature vector was treated as a node, and edges were established according to the spatial adjacency order of the slices. This topological graph structure not only preserved slice-level feature information but also maintained the spatial continuity of the tumor. In addition, to improve model robustness, a small amount of Gaussian noise was added to the node features during training as a data augmentation strategy to simulate real-world perturbations.

Multimodal Graph Attention Network for Survival Prediction

We designed an improved graph-based survival network to process the heterogeneous multimodal data described above. The model consisted of two parallel branches. The MRI branch adopted a GAT as the core backbone and used a multi-head attention mechanism to dynamically compute the association weights among different slice nodes. This mechanism enabled the model to automatically identify and focus on the key slices that were most informative for prognostic assessment, and to aggregate local node features into a global imaging embedding through attentional aggregation. The clinical branch extracted higher-order clinical representations using an MLP. Finally, the imaging embedding and clinical representation were concatenated at the fusion layer and fed into fully connected layers to calculate the DLPS for each patient.

The prognostic value of DLPS was evaluated using Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis. Patients were stratified into the high-DLPS group and low-DLPS group according to the median DLPS. The survival differences between the two groups were compared using the log-rank test. Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves at 1, 3, and 5 years were further constructed to evaluate predictive performance.

Model Training Strategy and Interpretability Analysis

To address the right-censored nature of survival data, the Cox partial likelihood loss (CPLL) was adopted as the loss function to maximize the conditional probability of observed events. Model training was performed using the stochastic gradient descent (SGD) optimizer, with the cosine annealing warm restarts strategy applied to dynamically adjust the learning rate and facilitate model convergence. To reduce the risk of overfitting, the GNN architecture was intentionally kept shallow, consisting of two graph attention layers followed by attentional aggregation and a compact clinical branch. Several regularization strategies were applied during training, including dropout (rate = 0.6), L2 weight decay (1 × 10−4), gradient clipping (maximum norm = 1.0), early stopping based on the internal validation C-index, and mild Gaussian noise augmentation of node features during training. The learning rate was set conservatively at 1 × 10−5, and cosine annealing warm restarts were used for learning-rate scheduling. In addition, to enhance the clinical interpretability of the model, a gradient-based feature attribution method was used to quantify and visualize the contributions of different clinical variables and MRI features to survival risk prediction. Gradient-weighted class activation mapping (Grad-CAM) was further applied to visualize the tumor regions that contributed most to model prediction.

Cox Regression Analysis and Nomogram Construction

Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were used to analyze the associations of DLPS and clinical variables with OS. Variables with a P value < 0.05 in the multivariable Cox regression analysis were included in the nomogram. Based on the results of the multivariable analysis, a nomogram integrating DLPS and significant clinical variables was constructed to estimate individualized probabilities of OS. Calibration curves for the training and validation cohorts were plotted to assess the calibration performance of the model.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages. For comparisons in the baseline table, normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test, whereas non-normally distributed continuous variables were analyzed using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed in Python (version 3.10.0).

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki (2024 revision). The study protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of Zhangzhou Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University (approval number: 2024KYB484; date of approval: 23 December 2024). All patient data were anonymized and de-identified prior to analysis. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, which utilized anonymized data from electronic medical records, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the same ethics committee.

Incremental Value of DLPS

As shown in Table S1, the addition of DLPS improved the prognostic performance of the clinical model. In the core clinical model including FIGO stage and concurrent chemotherapy, the C-index increased from 0.688 to 0.825 in the training cohort and from 0.573 to 0.750 in the internal validation cohort after adding DLPS. The time-dependent AUCs in the internal validation cohort also improved at 1, 3, and 5 years. In the screened clinical model based on variables with P < 0.20 in univariable analysis, the internal validation C-index increased from 0.676 to 0.790 after incorporating DLPS. As shown in Table S2, DLPS remained significantly associated with OS after adjustment for treatment-related variables, with an HR of 2.71 (95% CI, 1.90–3.87; P < 0.001) in the treatment-adjusted model and 2.66 (95% CI, 1.86–3.82; P < 0.001) after further adjustment for FIGO stage. These findings suggest that DLPS provides additional prognostic value beyond conventional clinical and treatment-related variables.

Discussion

Accurate pretreatment prognostic stratification remains a major clinical challenge in patients with LACSCC, given the substantial heterogeneity in survival outcomes despite standard treatment.2,6,23 In this study, we developed and validated a multi-modal graph neural network model that integrates deep features derived from multisequence MRI with clinical variables to predict OS in patients with LACSCC. The model-derived DLPS demonstrated robust discriminative performance in the validation cohort and remained an independent predictor of OS in the multivariable Cox regression analysis. Furthermore, the nomogram incorporating DLPS and key clinical variables improved the feasibility and accuracy of individualized survival assessment. Taken together, these findings suggest that, within current imaging-based prognostic frameworks, explicit modeling of spatial imaging structure combined with clinical variables may provide additional value for prognostic stratification in LACSCC.

Previous studies have extensively investigated the role of MRI-based radiomics in prognostic prediction for cervical cancer. However, approaches based on handcrafted texture, shape, and intensity features are often limited by feature stability and generalizability.12-15 With the increasing application of deep learning in cervical cancer imaging analysis, many studies have employed convolutional neural networks to automatically learn high-level imaging features and have shown promise in predicting treatment response and other clinically relevant outcomes. 24 Nevertheless, most of these studies, including a recent multi-center multimodal model by Wang et al, have targeted progression-free survival or relied on pooling-based aggregation; relatively few have addressed OS using structure-aware approaches.25-27 In this context, the stable performance of our model for OS prediction suggests that the deep features extracted by the network may capture tumor phenotypes associated with long-term prognosis. This prognostic association may be partly explained by the ability of multiparametric MRI to reflect tumor burden, local invasion, nodal status, and intratumoral heterogeneity before treatment. These imaging phenotypes may be related to aggressive tumor biology and impaired treatment response. Therefore, DLPS may capture prognostically relevant imaging information beyond conventional qualitative MRI assessment. Spatial heterogeneity is a prominent imaging characteristic of solid tumors and has been widely recognized as being closely associated with tumor biology, treatment response, and patient outcome. 10 In most existing deep learning studies based on cervical cancer MRI, sequential slices are either analyzed independently or aggregated using simple pooling strategies, which may not fully exploit the heterogeneous information distributed across the three-dimensional tumor space.12-15 Against this background, our study explicitly modeled the spatial adjacency relationships among MRI slices, allowing feature aggregation to preserve inter-slice contextual information and thereby provide a more comprehensive representation of overall tumor structure. 28 This strategy is consistent with the emerging trend toward structure-aware analysis in medical imaging research and offers a novel perspective for incorporating spatial heterogeneity into prognostic modeling for cervical cancer. 29

Multimodal fusion of imaging features and clinical variables has generally been considered an effective approach for improving prognostic performance. 20 In the present study, imaging and clinical data were processed through parallel branches and fused at a high-level representation space, enabling DLPS to encode both imaging phenotypes and clinical risk information. 28 Importantly, DLPS retained its independent prognostic value after adjustment for clinical variables, including stage and treatment strategies, further supporting the utility of multimodal integration.

Model interpretability is particularly important for deep learning-based prognostic models, as the “black-box” nature of these approaches may hinder their clinical acceptance and implementation. In the present study, we used multiple strategies to im-prove interpretability. Grad-CAM was applied to visualize the tumor regions contributing most to model prediction 30 and the resulting heatmaps showed that the model primarily focused on intratumoral regions rather than surrounding normal tissues, suggesting that the network captured lesion-relevant imaging patterns rather than re-lying predominantly on background signals. In addition, feature importance analysis was used to quantify the relative contributions of imaging-derived and clinical features to OS prediction. Furthermore, the nomogram integrating DLPS translated the model output into a clinically intuitive tool, which may facilitate its interpretation and application in individualized prognostic evaluation and treatment decision-making. 31

DLPS was among the most influential contributors to OS prediction, supporting its role as a summary measure of prognostically relevant imaging phenotypes. Clinical stage remained an important predictor of OS, which is consistent with its established role in reflecting tumor extent and overall disease burden.32,33 Concurrent chemo-therapy emerged as a protective factor, consistent with its established role in improving outcomes in patients with locally advanced disease treated with definitive radiotherapy. 33 More importantly, DLPS remained independently associated with OS after adjustment for conventional clinical variables, indicating that it may capture prognostically relevant information beyond that reflected by routine clinical assessment. Collectively, these findings suggest that the proposed model may serve not as a replacement for established clinical indicators, but rather as a complementary tool for improving individualized prognostic stratification. 34

Several clinical and molecular variables incorporated in the present model merit further discussion. EGFR overexpression has been associated with tumor proliferation, radioresistance, and adverse outcomes in cervical carcinoma, and its inclusion is clinically relevant given that the majority of patients in this cohort received concurrent nimotuzumab, an anti-EGFR monoclonal antibody. 35 PD-L1 expression, evaluated by CPS, reflects tumor immune evasion status and has demonstrated predictive significance for immunotherapy benefit in pivotal phase III cervical cancer trials. 36 HER2 overexpression, although relatively infrequent in cervical squamous cell carcinoma (approximately 5–15%), has been linked to aggressive tumor behavior in genomic and clinical cohorts. 35 These three biomarkers were processed through an independent clinical branch and fused with imaging features only at the representation level, consistent with the model’s rationale of integrating imaging-orthogonal biological dimensions. In the final nomogram, HER2 IHC 2+/3+ was the only molecular biomarker retained as an independent adverse prognostic factor (HR: 4.27, 95% CI: 1.54–11.79, P = 0.005), providing suggestive evidence that HER2 overexpression may identify a biologically high-risk subset, although the limited number of HER2-positive cases in this cohort warrants cautious interpretation and external validation.

This study has several limitations. First, the limited number of outcome events (38 deaths, approximately 15%) restricts the number of covariates that can be reliably included in multivariable models and may produce unstable estimates or inflated performance metrics. Second, the single-center retrospective design necessitates external validation in larger, multi-institutional cohorts before clinical translation. Third, the imaging protocol included only conventional MRI sequences; the addition of dynamic contrast-enhanced sequences could further characterize the tumor microenvironment and should be explored in future work. Fourth, while Grad-CAM visualizations provide qualitative localization of model attention, they do not establish causal relationships between imaging features and tumor biology. Fifth, despite sensitivity analyses adjusting for treatment-related variables, residual confounding cannot be fully excluded given the non-randomized retrospective design. Finally, subgroup analyses based on molecular biomarkers were exploratory and underpowered; these findings should be regarded as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. These limitations underscore the exploratory nature of the present framework and the need for prospective validation before clinical application. Future studies based on these findings should prioritize prospective multi-center validation, molecularly enriched cohorts to confirm the prognostic role of HER2, and extension of the graph framework to three-dimensional volumetric representations.

Conclusion

In this retrospective study, a multimodal graph neural network framework integrating multi-sequence MRI deep features and clinical variables was developed for predicting overall survival in patients with locally advanced cervical squamous cell carcinoma. The model-derived deep learning prognostic score demonstrated independent prognostic value and enabled effective stratification of patients into distinct risk groups. The nomogram combining the deep learning prognostic score with clinical stage, HER2 status, and concurrent chemotherapy showed favorable calibration, suggesting potential utility for individualized risk assessment. Given the single-center, retrospective design and the limited number of outcome events, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory. Prospective validation in larger, multi-institutional cohorts is needed to confirm the generalizability and clinical applicability of the proposed framework. Future studies integrating pathomics, genomics, and additional imaging modalities may further refine prognostic accuracy and advance the understanding of the biological determinants underlying the imaging-derived risk signatures.
