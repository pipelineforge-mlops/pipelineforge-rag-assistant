==============================
The combined application of microneedles (MNs) and exosomes represents a significant research direction in the fields of targeted drug delivery and regenerative medicine. Their synergistic and complementary effects not only address key technical challenges associated with exosome delivery—such as low delivery efficiency and limited tissue penetration—but also endow MNs with capabilities for targeted therapy and precise diagnostics, thereby demonstrating substantial advantages in the diagnosis and treatment of multisystem diseases. This review summarizes recent advances in MN-exosome delivery systems across dermatological, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular, and musculoskeletal diseases, as well as tumor diagnosis and therapy, ocular surface disorders, and oral diseases. It focuses on the fabrication strategies, mechanisms of action, and therapeutic efficacy of various MN-based exosome delivery systems. Furthermore, it proposes research perspectives and potential solutions for diseases that respond poorly to conventional diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, providing a reference for the future development and clinical translation of MN-exosome systems.

1 Introduction

Exosomes are a class of extracellular vesicles secreted by living cells through an “endocytosis–fusion–secretion” pathway, with a diameter ranging from 30 to 150 nm. They typically exhibit a cup-shaped or disc-like morphology and possess a lipid bilayer membrane structure [1]. Their surface is enriched with lipids such as cholesterol, sphingomyelin, and ceramide, while their interior contains bioactive molecules, including proteins, messenger RNA (mRNA), and microRNAs (miRNAs), enabling them to play crucial roles in the cellular microenvironment [2]. Exosomes derived from different cell types exhibit distinct biological functions [3]. For instance, exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) have been demonstrated to regulate inflammatory responses, promote collagen deposition, alleviate myocardial injury, and facilitate cardiac repair [[4], [5], [6]]. Plant-derived exosome-like nanoparticles have been shown to possess immunomodulatory, gut microbiota-regulating, anti-inflammatory, and anti-tumor properties [7]. Moreover, as exosomes carry lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids originating from their parent cells and mediate intercellular communication, they can also serve as potential biomarkers for early cancer detection [8].

Transdermal drug delivery systems represent an emerging approach for administering therapeutics through the skin. In this process, drugs penetrate the outer skin layers and are absorbed into systemic circulation via capillaries, thereby exerting therapeutic effects [9]. Conventional transdermal formulations primarily rely on passive diffusion across the skin; however, the tightly packed “brick-and-mortar” structure and highly hydrophobic nature of the stratum corneum (SC) [10] severely limit the penetration of most macromolecular and hydrophilic drugs [11], resulting in low bioavailability and restricted clinical applicability. Microneedles (MNs) provide an effective strategy to overcome these barriers. By creating microscale channels in the skin through minimally invasive physical penetration, MNs bypass the SC without stimulating nerve endings, thereby enabling painless drug administration. In addition, MN-mediated delivery avoids gastrointestinal degradation and first-pass hepatic metabolism, reducing systemic side effects while improving bioavailability. Furthermore, these systems allow for controlled and tunable drug release according to therapeutic requirements, thereby enhancing safety and treatment efficiency [[12], [13], [14]]. Based on their mechanisms of action, MNs can be classified into solid MNs, coated MNs, hollow MNs, dissolving MNs, and hydrogel MNs. Among these, hydrogel MNs exhibit unique advantages. After penetrating the SC, they rapidly absorb interstitial fluid, swell, and form continuous, non-occlusive microchannels that facilitate drug transport. This property also enables the extraction of interstitial fluid for real-time monitoring of biomarkers such as glucose and uric acid [15,16].

MN-exosome delivery systems combine the minimally invasive, painless, and efficient delivery features of MNs with the biocompatibility, biological activity, and targeting potential of exosomes. This combination offers a promising means of overcoming key limitations of conventional delivery approaches, particularly limited tissue penetration, poor local retention, and systemic exposure. Given the rapid development of this field, this review primarily focuses on studies published between January 2023 and June 2026 to reflect the most recent advances and emerging trends. Earlier seminal and foundational studies were also included where necessary to clarify key concepts and mechanisms. Previous reviews have discussed biomaterial-assisted delivery of MSCs and their exosomes and summarized the design and application of integrated MN-exosome systems [[17], [18], [19]]. Building on these studies, the present review organizes the available evidence by disease setting, linking the delivery barriers of each tissue to the corresponding MN design, exosome function, and reported therapeutic outcomes. It also considers therapeutic delivery and diagnostic sampling separately and compares the translational requirements of directly accessible tissues with those of internal-organ applications. 2 Research progress in major application fields

2.1 Applications of MN-exosome delivery systems in skin tissue

Skin disorders provide a clear example of how MNs and exosomes can complement each other. Many of the cells involved in wound repair and cutaneous inflammation are located beneath the SC, whereas exosomes applied directly to the skin surface penetrate poorly and are easily lost. Although repeated intradermal injections can improve tissue access, they cause discomfort and do not always provide uniform distribution. MNs offer a more controlled route into superficial tissues and may also protect exosomes while extending their local residence time [20]. Once delivered, exosomes can regulate several processes that are commonly disrupted in chronic or aging skin, including macrophage polarization, inflammation, angiogenesis, fibroblast activity, and re-epithelialization [21,22]. In this pairing, MNs improve tissue access and local retention, while exosomes provide the biological signals needed for repair. This complementarity supports their use in chronic wounds, photoaging, scarring, and inflammatory skin disorders.

2.1.1 Wound healing

The skin serves as a critical physical barrier between the body and the external environment, protecting tissues and organs from physical, mechanical, and chemical damage while participating in essential physiological functions [23]. However, in daily life, the skin is highly susceptible to injury and rupture. The wound healing process is complex and involves multiple tightly regulated stages, including angiogenesis, granulation tissue formation, re-epithelialization, fibrotic remodeling, and extracellular matrix (ECM) reconstruction [24]. Under certain pathological conditions, such as diabetes and burns, the regenerative capacity of skin wounds is impaired, often resulting in chronic non-healing wounds [25]. In addition, population aging has led to an increasing number of patients with compromised wound healing, thereby driving the demand for novel therapeutic strategies [26]. In the field of diabetic chronic wound healing, Moon et al. [27] developed a dual-compartment dissolving MN system (MSCi@MN). In this system, MSC-derived nanovesicles (NVs) were conjugated with bioactive DNA derived from salmon sperm cells to form NV-DNA complexes, which were loaded into the tip layer of hyaluronic acid (HA)-based MNs. Meanwhile, photothermal-responsive Ti3C2 MXene nanoparticles (MX) were incorporated into the backing layer, forming a multifunctional delivery platform (Fig. 2a). In vitro studies demonstrated that NVs could regulate macrophage polarization toward the M2 reparative phenotype and, in combination with bioactive DNA, synergistically promote angiogenesis while inhibiting apoptosis. Under near-infrared (NIR) irradiation, the dissolution rate of HA MNs was accelerated, enabling controlled co-release of NV-DNA complexes. In addition, the mild photothermal effect generated by MXene nanoparticles enhanced blood perfusion and inhibited bacterial proliferation at the wound site. In vivo experiments further confirmed that, compared with DNA/MX@MN patches lacking the biomimetic exosome NV component, MSCi@MN significantly accelerated wound healing in mice, achieving a 7.37-fold increase in healing rate. In chronic non-healing wounds, Xu et al. [28] compared exosomes derived from young and aged dermal fibroblasts. In the original study, “young fibroblasts” (Y-FBs) were primary dermal fibroblasts isolated from neonatal mice, whereas “aged fibroblasts” (A-FBs) were isolated from naturally aged 18-month-old mice. Thus, the distinction between the two cell populations was based on donor age. Exosomes derived from Y-FBs and A-FBs were termed Y-EXOs and A-EXOs, respectively. Y-EXOs were spray-coated onto the needle tips of a Zn2+-doped hyaluronic acid methacrylate (HAMA)/polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) bilayer MN patch (MNP), thereby constructing Y-EXOs@Zn-HAMA/PVA MNP (Fig. 2b). Upon penetration into the dermis, Y-EXOs, serving as the outer layer, are rapidly released and exert therapeutic effects via their cargo of differentially expressed miRNAs. These miRNAs target aged fibroblasts (A-FBs) and CD4+ T cells, promoting A-FB proliferation and migration to enhance collagen deposition and re-epithelialization, while simultaneously modulating the local immune microenvironment of the wound. In vivo studies demonstrated that, compared with A-EXOs-loaded MN patches loaded with exosomes derived from aged fibroblasts (A-EXOs@Zn-HAMA/PVA MNP), treatment with Y-EXOs@Zn-HAMA/PVA MNP resulted in faster wound closure and more pronounced re-epithelialization in full-thickness skin defect mice.

For ultraviolet (UV) -induced skin injury, He et al. [29] incorporated human umbilical cord MSC-derived exosomes (hUMSC-Exo) and epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG), a major bioactive catechin found in green tea with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties, into a HAMA/PVA matrix to fabricate a composite hUMSC-Exo/EGCG-MN patch (Fig. 2c). In this system, EGCG provides additional reactive oxygen species (ROS)-scavenging activity and alleviates oxidative stress, thereby complementing the regenerative and immunomodulatory effects of hUMSC-Exo. Together, these components regulate interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2) expression, reduce excessive ROS generation and inflammatory cytokine release, and promote collagen I/III deposition and epidermal cell proliferation. In vivo experiments further showed that, compared with the blank MN group, treatment with the hUMSC-Exo/EGCG-MN patch significantly reduced inflammatory cytokine levels, restored epidermal thickness, improved dermal structural integrity, and enhanced cellular proliferation in mice with UV-induced skin injury.

2.1.2 Anti-aging

With the accelerating global aging process, the prevention of skin aging has attracted increasing attention and has become a prominent research focus in recent years [30]. Sun et al. [31] loaded β-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) into human umbilical cord MSC-derived small extracellular vesicles (hucMSC-sEVs) via electroporation, thereby constructing NMN-loaded sEVs (NMN-sEVs) with enhanced stability and therapeutic efficacy. NMN-sEVs promote mitophagy, facilitating the clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria associated with cellular senescence, improving mitochondrial quality and function, and ultimately delaying dermal fibroblast senescence as well as overall skin aging phenotypes. A comparison between MN-mediated delivery and topical administration demonstrated that, relative to topical application, sEVs delivered via nanomicroneedles more effectively penetrate into deeper skin layers. This approach significantly reversed epidermal thinning and dermal collagen fiber disorganization in aged mice. Notably, the percentage of dermal collagen fibers, which was markedly reduced in aged skin, was significantly increased following MN-mediated sEV delivery. Furthermore, MN delivery effectively reversed the expression of skin aging-associated genes, exhibiting markedly superior efficacy compared with topical administration.

2.1.3 Scar treatment

The therapeutic efficacy of exosomes in hypertrophic scar (HS) treatment is often limited by their insufficient penetration depth. Due to the dense and rigid structure of HS tissue, repeated intralesional injections are commonly employed. However, such repeated administrations frequently cause severe pain and lead to uneven exosome distribution. Meng et al. [32] isolated engineered exosomes from adipose-derived MSC transduced with a lentiviral vector encoding miR-141-3p (Lv-miR-141-3p), termed miR-141-3pOE-Exos. These exosomes were subsequently incorporated into HA-based dissolving MN arrays (DMNAs) reinforced with hydroxypropyl-β-cyclodextrin (HP-β-CD) to enhance mechanical strength, yielding miR-141OE-Exos@DMNAs, as termed by the original authors. This system directly targets hypertrophic scar fibroblasts, effectively inhibiting their abnormal proliferation, migration, and myofibroblast transdifferentiation while reducing the deposition of fibrosis-related markers, including type I collagen, fibronectin, and α-smooth muscle actin. In vivo studies demonstrated that, compared with control groups receiving free exosomes or blank MNs, treatment with miR-141OE-Exos@DMNAs significantly reduced scar thickness in a rabbit ear hypertrophic scar model. Quantitative evaluation using the scar elevation index (SEI) further confirmed the therapeutic efficacy; after three treatment cycles, the SEI value in the miR-141OE-Exos@DMNAs group was nearly comparable to that of normal skin and was significantly lower than those of the other control groups.

2.1.4 Treatment of skin diseases

Phototherapy has been widely applied in the treatment of various skin diseases (e.g., psoriasis); however, its clinical efficacy is limited by the reflection, absorption, and scattering of light by skin components such as melanin and keratin [33,34]. Psoriasis is a chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory skin disorder characterized primarily by epidermal hyperkeratosis and parakeratosis [35]. The excessive proliferation of keratinocytes is closely associated with the pathogenesis of psoriasis [36,37]. Zhao et al. [38] designed a two-stage MN system, termed SLE MNs by the original authors, composed of a lower needle tip layer fabricated from polymethyl methacrylate (PMMA) with excellent optical transparency and an upper needle tip layer consisting of PVA loaded with MX. In addition, M2 macrophage-derived exosomes were enriched on the surface of the PVA tips. The PMMA layer serves as an optical waveguide for 808-nm NIR light, overcoming the epidermal melanin absorption barrier and efficiently transmitting light energy to the basal layer, thereby activating MX within the PVA layer to generate a localized photothermal effect (∼58 °C), which inhibits the excessive proliferation of keratinocytes. Meanwhile, the PVA tips dissolve subcutaneously, enabling the sustained release of these exosomes. These exosomes promote macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype and downregulate pro-inflammatory cytokines. In an imiquimod (IMQ)-induced murine psoriasis model, treatment with SLE MNs exhibited the most pronounced therapeutic effects. Specifically, erythema, scaling, and infiltration were markedly reduced, body weight gradually returned to normal, psoriasis-associated epidermal hyperplasia was alleviated, and splenomegaly was significantly improved. 2.2 Applications of MN-exosome delivery systems in cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases

Ischemic injury of the heart and brain presents a different delivery challenge. After systemic administration, extracellular vesicles are rapidly redistributed, with a substantial proportion accumulating in clearance organs. As a result, the amount that reaches poorly perfused lesions can be low and variable [55]. Direct injection increases local exposure but is invasive and may not provide uniform or lasting retention. An implanted MN patch can instead act as a local depot at the injured site. In a myocardial infarction model, exosome-loaded MNs increased vesicle retention in the infarcted myocardium and reduced post-infarction fibrosis [56]. This localized approach is particularly relevant because exosomes can influence inflammation, apoptosis, angiogenesis, neural repair, and tissue remodeling—processes that often occur together after myocardial infarction or ischemia–reperfusion injury.

2.2.1 Myocardial infarction

Myocardial infarction (MI) is one of the most serious cardiovascular diseases threatening global human health, with persistently high incidence and mortality rates in China [57]. Following MI, the heart initiates a series of compensatory mechanisms; however, sustained compensation leads to ventricular remodeling, characterized by cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and apoptosis, interstitial fibrosis, and structural alterations such as left ventricular hypertrophy, ultimately progressing to heart failure [58]. Although various clinical treatments are available, including pharmacotherapy, percutaneous coronary intervention, and surgical approaches, effective strategies to prevent or reverse ventricular remodeling remain limited [59]. In recent years, exosome-based therapy for MI has emerged as a research hotspot. However, impaired blood flow in the infarcted region results in rapid accumulation of carbon dioxide and a sharp decrease in extracellular pH from 7.4 to as low as 5.9, which significantly compromises the therapeutic efficacy of exosomes delivered via conventional routes, such as intravenous or even intracardiac injection.

To address this challenge, Wang et al. [60] developed a novel MN-exosome delivery system incorporating self-propelled microrobots loaded with azide-functionalized human MSC-derived exosomes (N3-exosomes), termed DLC-VEGF/ESMP-exo MNs (Fig. 3a). Upon insertion into the infarcted myocardium, the MNs adhere closely to the damaged tissue and release exosome-loaded microrobots (ESMPs). In the acidic microenvironment, ESMPs react with surrounding H+ to generate CO2, producing propulsion that enables directional migration (fan-shaped diffusion) into deeper tissues while simultaneously neutralizing local acidity and enhancing exosome activity. Meanwhile, vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) released from the MN base layer promotes endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenic sprouting, whereas ESMP-released exosomes stimulate cardiomyocyte proliferation and inhibit myocardial fibrosis. The synergistic effects facilitate vascular network reconstruction and myocardial regeneration. In vivo studies in a rat left anterior descending (LAD) coronary artery ligation model demonstrated that this system significantly improved cardiac function (e.g., ejection fraction and contractility) and histological repair compared with all control groups. In another study, Mei et al. This approach promoted angiogenesis and myocardial regeneration, significantly reduced infarct size, inhibited myocardial fibrosis, decreased cardiomyocyte apoptosis, enhanced cardiomyocyte proliferation, and ultimately improved cardiac function while limiting left ventricular remodeling. In vivo experiments using a rat LAD MI model showed that this strategy increased left ventricular ejection fraction by 15–20%, improved ventricular remodeling, and elevated capillary and arterial density by 2–3-fold. Compared with intravenous administration, MN-mediated delivery resulted in markedly higher myocardial retention and superior therapeutic efficacy of exosomes.

2.2.2 Ischemia–reperfusion injury

Ischemia–reperfusion injury (IRI) refers to the phenomenon in which tissue damage is exacerbated rather than alleviated following the restoration of blood flow after vascular recanalization in conditions such as cerebral infarction or myocardial infarction. In some cases, reperfusion not only fails to improve the pathological condition but further aggravates tissue injury, even leading to irreversible damage [64]. Early growth response 1 (EGR1) is a key transcription factor involved in regulating cellular stress responses and autophagy, playing a critical role in myocardial injury [65]. Following myocardial IRI, EGR1 is rapidly upregulated, promoting the expression of inflammation- and apoptosis-related genes and thereby accelerating disease progression [66].

Huang et al. [61] loaded EGR1-specific small interfering RNA (siRNA) into human umbilical cord MSC-derived exosomes (hUCMSCs-Exo) to construct Exosi-EGR1, which was subsequently incorporated into gelatin methacryloyl (GelMA) MNs, termed MN-Exosi-EGR1 (Fig. 3b). Following subcutaneous or epicardial administration, the system enables sustained local release. Exosi-EGR1 is internalized by cardiomyocytes, where it silences EGR1 expression, suppresses oxidative stress, enhances mitophagy, and facilitates the clearance of damaged mitochondria, ultimately reducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis and fibrosis. In an LAD ligation-induced myocardial IRI mouse model, compared with GelMA MNs loaded with blank exosomes, the MN-Exosi-EGR1 group exhibited significantly reduced myocardial fibrosis, improved cardiac function, and a markedly decreased cardiomyocyte apoptosis rate.

In the context of cerebral IRI, microglia migrate to the ischemic lesion and release pro-inflammatory cytokines and cytotoxic substances, thereby exacerbating brain tissue damage [67]. Therefore, inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory factors from microglia has emerged as a key therapeutic strategy for ischemic cerebrovascular diseases [68]. To address the low yield of exosomes associated with conventional two-dimensional (2D) culture systems, Zhang et al. [62] employed a three-dimensional (3D) culture approach to produce MSC-derived exosomes (3D-MSC-Exo). These exosomes were subsequently incorporated into a GelMA hydrogel-based MN patch, termed MN@3D-Exo (Fig. 3c). 3D-MSC-Exo promoted angiogenesis, neurogenesis, and neuronal survival in the peri-ischemic region, thereby facilitating neurological functional recovery and brain tissue remodeling. Additionally, these exosomes induced microglial polarization toward the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype, suppressed neuroinflammation, and mitigated post-ischemic neuronal damage, with sustained release in the damaged brain region for approximately one week. In vivo experiments using a middle cerebral artery occlusion (MCAO) rat model demonstrated that the MN@3D-Exo group achieved the best neurological outcomes, as evidenced by the lowest Longa neurological deficit scores and the smallest infarct volume.

2.3 Applications of MN-exosome delivery systems in musculoskeletal diseases

Musculoskeletal tissues remain difficult to treat through conventional delivery routes. Articular cartilage, tendon, and the annulus fibrosus are poorly vascularized and structurally dense, which limits the amount of systemically administered therapy that reaches them. Even after intra-articular injection, therapeutic agents may be removed through vascular, lymphatic, or phagocytic pathways before a sustained effect is achieved [69]. MNs can place exosomes directly at a defined site and, through detachable or tissue-anchoring designs, keep them there long enough to allow sustained release [70,71]. These disorders are not driven by inflammation alone; oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disrupted ECM turnover also contribute to disease progression and may be regulated by exosomal cargo. The combination is therefore relevant to arthritis, tendinopathy, and intervertebral disc degeneration, where both prolonged local retention and broader biological regulation are needed.

2.3.1 Arthritis

Knee osteoarthritis (KOA) is a highly prevalent chronic degenerative joint disease characterized by pathological changes such as articular cartilage degeneration, subchondral bone cyst formation, and synovitis [72]. Currently, no effective non-surgical interventions are available to halt disease progression, while surgical treatments, such as joint replacement, are associated with a relatively high risk of complications [73]. Pharmacological therapies are limited by low bioavailability due to first-pass metabolism, as well as potential hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity [74]. Therefore, the development of novel therapeutic strategies and efficient anti-inflammatory agents for KOA is of great importance.

Li et al. [75] developed a composite microneedle patch by incorporating MSC-derived exosomes (MSC-Exos) into MNs composed of methacrylated HA/polydopamine (PDA) nanoparticle, termed PDA@Exo MN (Fig. 4a). This system exerts therapeutic effects by activating the phosphoinositide 3-kinase/protein kinase B/mechanistic target of rapamycin (PI3K–Akt–mTOR) signaling pathway, scavenging ROS, and alleviating oxidative stress. In addition, it promotes macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype, inhibits cartilage degradation, and facilitates subchondral bone regeneration. In vivo studies demonstrated that, compared with all control groups, the PDA@Exo MN-treated KOA rats exhibited the best cartilage integrity, the most significant improvement in bone microarchitecture, and the lowest levels of inflammation and oxidative stress, without observable hepatotoxicity or nephrotoxicity, indicating excellent biosafety.Fig. 4 (a) Schematic illustration of the fabrication of PDA@Exo MN [75]. For rheumatoid arthritis (RA), Wen et al. [77] designed a cryogenic MN patch loaded with milk-derived exosomes (mEXOs) carrying tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α)-targeting siRNA, which effectively alleviated synovial inflammation, cartilage destruction, and microcirculatory dysfunction. In vivo experiments using a rabbit RA model demonstrated that MN administration at the “Zusanli” (ST36) acupoint significantly improved knee swelling, body weight loss, microcirculatory impairment, synovial hyperplasia, and inflammatory infiltration. All evaluated parameters were superior to those of the control groups.

2.3.2 Tendinopathy

Similarly, tendinitis is a common clinical condition caused by excessive mechanical loading, affecting approximately 6% of the general population and up to 30% of runners [78]. It is characterized by degenerative changes in tendon collagen fibers. Conventional treatments are limited, as oral medications exhibit low bioavailability and potential hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity, while surgical interventions are associated with inherent risks [79].

Zhang et al. [76] developed an innovative therapeutic system with both efficient delivery capability and local microenvironment modulation by incorporating grapefruit-derived exosomes (GF-Exos) into GelMA hydrogel-based MNs, termed MN@GF-Exos (Fig. 4b). This system enables controlled and sustained release of exosomes at the injury site, promoting tenocyte proliferation and migration while inhibiting abnormal collagen deposition. In vivo studies demonstrated that mice with tendinitis treated with MN@GF-Exos exhibited significantly improved locomotor function. Moreover, key biomechanical properties—including Young's modulus, ultimate tensile strength, and strain at maximum load—were markedly enhanced compared with control groups, approaching those of healthy tendons. Notably, no ectopic ossification or toxicity in major organs was observed, indicating favorable safety.

2.3.3 Intervertebral disc degeneration

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD) is a leading cause of disability worldwide. Conventional surgical interventions, such as discectomy, fail to restore the native function of the intervertebral disc and may increase the mechanical burden on adjacent discs [80]. The annulus fibrosus (AF), the outer layer of the intervertebral disc, plays a critical role in preventing IVDD by generating substantial circumferential stress [81]. However, trauma or chronic mechanical strain can induce varying degrees of AF rupture, leading to decreased ECM synthesis and increased ECM degradation. This imbalance ultimately results in ECM loss, AF structural failure, and progression of IVDD [82,83].

Hu et al. [71] developed a high-strength, spiral-structured MN patch tailored for intervertebral disc tissue using 3D printing technology. This system incorporates bone marrow MSC-derived exosomes engineered with a miR-378 mimic (EXO@miR-378), termed T-MN@EXO@miR-378. The spiral architecture enhances tissue anchoring and increases the contact area. Upon insertion into the lamellar structure of the AF, the MNs release EXO@miR-378, which restores mitophagy in damaged AF cells, suppresses abnormal ECM degradation, promotes ECM synthesis, and maintains ECM homeostasis. In vivo studies using a rat intervertebral disc injury model demonstrated that disc height increased by 31% at week 8 post-treatment. Additionally, ECM synthesis was significantly enhanced compared with the control group.

2.4 Applications of MN-exosome delivery systems in other fields

The conditions discussed in this section are diverse, but they share a practical problem: the relevant tissue is often difficult to access, and conventional formulations do not remain there for long. MN technology has therefore expanded beyond the skin to the oral mucosa, ocular tissues, and other sites where epithelial barriers and fluid clearance limit local delivery [84]. In cancer diagnosis, MNs can sample interstitial fluid and enrich tumor-derived exosomes for subsequent analysis [85]. On the ocular surface and oral mucosa, they can improve local contact and penetration despite continuous tear or salivary clearance and tissue movement [86,87]. Exosomes play different roles across these applications: in some settings they serve as disease-related biomarkers, whereas in others they provide immunomodulatory or regenerative signals. Here, MNs mainly address problems of access, sampling, and retention, while exosomes provide the diagnostic information or therapeutic activity.

2.4.1 Tumor prevention and early diagnosis

Cancer has become a major global public health challenge. According to the latest statistics from the World Health Organization, the number of cancer-related deaths worldwide exceeded 10 million in 2020 and is projected to reach 16 million by 2040. Early diagnosis remains a critical determinant for improving cancer survival rates [88,89]. For instance, advanced melanoma is frequently associated with metastasis, rendering conventional interventions such as radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and immunotherapy less effective, with a 5-year survival rate of only approximately 35% [90]. In contrast, early detection followed by surgical excision can achieve survival rates as high as 98% [91]. However, even among well-trained clinicians, the sensitivity of visual examination for melanoma diagnosis is only around 70% [92], and reliable tools for early melanoma detection remain insufficient.

Smith et al. [85] developed a star-shaped silicon MN platform, termed ExoPatch, composed of a PVA/sodium alginate hydrogel functionalized with Annexin V (AV) on its surface. This system exploits the specific immunoaffinity interaction between AV and phosphatidylserine (PS), which is highly expressed on the surface of tumor-derived exosomes. Upon insertion into the skin, ExoPatch directly captures melanoma-derived exosomes from interstitial fluid (ISF). These exosomes are subsequently analyzed using a lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) to detect melanoma-associated biomarkers, enabling early diagnosis. Notably, the total protein yield of exosomes isolated from melanoma tissues using ExoPatch was 11.5-fold higher than that obtained from healthy control tissues, while the LFIA signal intensity was increased by 3.5-fold, demonstrating its high sensitivity and effectiveness for melanoma detection.

2.4.2 Ocular diseases

Corneal chemical burns are among the most common ophthalmic emergencies and represent a significant public health challenge [93]. Their pathophysiology involves disruption of the cellular barrier and sustained release of inflammatory mediators, which exacerbate corneal cell apoptosis and necrosis [94]. In addition, inflammation can induce corneal neovascularization and fibrosis, further impairing wound healing [95]. Therefore, timely control of inflammation is of critical importance. Current clinical treatments for corneal chemical burns exhibit notable limitations [96]. For instance, although glucocorticoids are widely used to alleviate inflammation, they are associated with adverse effects such as delayed corneal healing and increased risks of infection and perforation.

Yu et al. [86] conjugated anti-tumor necrosis factor-α antibodies (aT) onto the surface of exosomes derived from human adipose-derived MSCs (ADSC-Exo), thereby constructing engineered aT-Exo with synergistic therapeutic effects. These engineered exosomes were subsequently incorporated into PVA MNs, termed E-MN. In the microenvironment of corneal chemical burns, aT is released in an inflammation-responsive manner, working synergistically with exosomes to attenuate inflammatory cascades, reduce macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, and promote immunomodulation and epithelial regeneration. Moreover, MN-exosome delivery significantly enhances corneal retention time and penetration efficiency of the therapeutic agents. In vivo studies using a murine corneal alkali burn model demonstrated that the E-MN group exhibited superior anti-inflammatory efficacy compared with all control groups. Furthermore, MN delivery prolonged the corneal retention time of aT-Exo by 3–5-fold and increased penetration depth by more than twofold.

2.4.3 Oral diseases

Oral ulcers are common inflammatory ulcerative lesions of the oral mucosa, characterized by a high recurrence rate. Currently, clinical management primarily relies on symptomatic local treatments, including glucocorticoids and wound-healing agents. However, the therapeutic efficacy of these drugs largely depends on their retention time on the oral mucosa, and conventional local administration is associated with limitations such as pain, potential tissue damage, and inconsistent drug dosing.

Ge et al. [87] developed a novel drug delivery system, termed EZ-MN, in which lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-preconditioned bone marrow MSC-derived exosomes (LPS-pre-Exos) were loaded onto the tips of silk fibroin-based MNs, while zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8) was incorporated into the base. Upon insertion into the oral mucosa, the MN tips enable sustained release of LPS-pre-Exos, which promote macrophage polarization toward the anti-inflammatory and reparative M2 phenotype. This results in long-lasting anti-inflammatory effects, enhanced epithelial regeneration, increased collagen deposition, and stimulated angiogenesis. Meanwhile, the ZIF-8 base gradually dissociates in the mildly acidic microenvironment of the ulcer, releasing Zn2+ ions to achieve broad-spectrum antibacterial activity, suppress microbial dysbiosis, and improve the healing microenvironment. In vivo studies using a rat oral ulcer model demonstrated that the ulcer area in the EZ-MN group was reduced to 5.3 ± 2.1% within 5 days, which was significantly superior to that of the control groups, including approximately 35% for blank MNs and approximately 22% for topically applied exosomes. Furthermore, EZ-MN exhibited sustained drug release for over 7 days in vitro and achieved an antibacterial rate exceeding 99%, whereas no significant antibacterial effect was observed in the control groups. 3 Challenges and perspectives

Recent work has made the potential of MN-exosome systems easier to see, but it has also shown how early the field remains. Most published studies are short-term proof-of-concept experiments in rodents. Few have examined whether the response persists after repeated treatment, whether it exceeds that achieved with free exosomes or conventional local injection, or whether the MN component provides enough additional benefit to justify a more complex product. Targeting, sustained release, biocompatibility, and minimal invasiveness are therefore better understood as design-dependent outcomes rather than fixed properties of every MN-exosome system. They vary with the source and preparation of the vesicles, the material and geometry of the MNs, the application site, and the surrounding disease microenvironment. Dose-matched factorial designs—including blank MNs, free exosomes, and exosome-loaded MNs—are needed to separate the effect of needle insertion from that of exosome delivery and to determine whether the combination is genuinely synergistic rather than simply additive.

A related difficulty is that the term “exosome” is often used for preparations that have not been produced or characterized in the same way. Donor cell type and condition, passage number, culture environment, collection procedure, isolation and purification method, and storage conditions can all alter vesicle composition and biological activity. The Minimal Information for Studies of Extracellular Vesicles 2023 (MISEV2023) guidelines provide a useful framework for reporting vesicle identity, purity, quantity, and function, although these recommendations have not yet been applied consistently across MN-exosome studies [101]. Given the diversity of vesicle preparations used in this field, future studies should distinguish rigorously characterized exosomes from broader extracellular vesicles or vesicle-like nanoparticles. Dose reporting also requires greater consistency. Particle number, total protein content, and preparation volume are often used interchangeably, even though they do not represent the same biological dose. Nor does a large particle count necessarily indicate a potent product. Potency testing should reflect the intended mechanism and clinical indication; an exosome preparation designed to promote angiogenesis in a chronic wound, for example, should not be evaluated using exactly the same functional endpoint as an anti-inflammatory preparation developed for arthritis [102,103].

The device itself introduces another layer of variation. MNs must be strong enough to enter the target tissue, yet the matrix must still release exosomes at an appropriate rate without compromising their activity. Changes that improve mechanical strength or prolong local residence may also slow swelling, dissolution, or cargo diffusion. Actual delivery is further influenced by needle geometry, material composition, loading method, application force, tissue thickness, and the use of an applicator [11,13]. For this reason, the nominal amount loaded into a patch is not an adequate measure of exposure. Studies should also report insertion efficiency, the proportion of cargo released, the amount retained in the target tissue, and variation between applications. Active delivery designs require additional scrutiny. In photoresponsive systems, light dose, penetration depth, and local heating need to remain within a safe and reproducible range [34]. Electrically driven or self-propelled systems similarly require evaluation of energy input, reaction products, and tissue compatibility. Drying, sterilization or aseptic processing, packaging, and long-term storage must preserve both MN performance and vesicle activity. Dissolving MNs have shown that extracellular vesicles can remain stable during extended storage, but this finding cannot be assumed to apply to every vesicle source, engineered cargo, or polymer matrix [20]. Scale-up will also depend on reproducible needle geometry, consistent vesicle loading, predictable release, and manufacturing processes that can be automated without introducing unacceptable batch variation [104].

Safety needs to be considered in relation to the disease as well as the material. Longer local retention and stronger biological activity are not necessarily beneficial in every setting. Proangiogenic or immunomodulatory effects that support chronic wound healing, for example, may be undesirable in tumors or diseases involving pathological neovascularization. Long-term local tissue responses, immune effects, off-target biodistribution, repeat-dose safety, and the fate of residual MN materials remain poorly characterized [55,103]. Early clinical studies of extracellular vesicle therapies in general have reported encouraging short-term safety, but differences in product preparation and study design, small patient cohorts, inconsistent adverse-event reporting, and limited follow-up prevent firm conclusions about long-term benefit and risk [105]. Despite rapid preclinical progress, direct clinical evidence for integrated exosome-loaded MN systems remains limited [19]. Small-animal models also do not fully reproduce human tissue thickness, mechanics, immune responses, or the procedural conditions required for internal organs. Human-relevant tissue models and large-animal studies will therefore be particularly important for cardiac, cerebral, ocular, and intervertebral applications.

Clinical development should begin with a clearly defined medical need rather than with the most elaborate device design. Accessible lesions, including chronic wounds and oral ulcers, as well as carefully selected ocular surface conditions, may offer more practical starting points because the device can be placed under direct observation and the local response can be monitored. MN-based sampling of tumor-derived exosomes provides a separate route toward minimally invasive diagnosis, but its detection limit, sensitivity, specificity, reproducibility, and added value over existing biopsy or liquid-biopsy methods need confirmation in patient cohorts. Internal-organ applications may provide substantial biological benefits, but they will also need to demonstrate a meaningful advantage over conventional local injection while accounting for procedural risk. The term “minimally invasive” should be used with similar care: cutaneous and mucosal devices may be applied directly, whereas cardiac and other deep-tissue systems can still require surgical exposure or implantation. Real-world adoption will also depend on device handling, application consistency, patient acceptance, shelf life, and whether the clinical benefit justifies the added manufacturing cost. Engineered vesicles, tissue-anchoring structures, and responsive MNs may improve specificity or release control, but each additional component increases manufacturing and regulatory complexity. Such complexity is worthwhile only when it produces a reproducible advantage over a simpler formulation. The regulatory classification of these systems is also likely to vary with their intended use, composition, and jurisdiction. Manufacturing standards, critical quality attributes, potency assays, and the likely regulatory pathway should therefore be considered together from the beginning of product development [103,106].

4 Conclusion

MN-exosome systems bring together two capabilities that are difficult to achieve with either component alone: access to a defined tissue compartment and local delivery of biologically active, cell-derived cargo. The studies reviewed here suggest that this combination can improve vesicle retention and extend the therapeutic or diagnostic use of exosomes across dermatological, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular, musculoskeletal, ocular, oral, and oncological applications. Its main value does not lie in any single MN material or exosome source, but in the ability to match the delivery profile and biological activity to the needs of a particular disease.

The evidence nevertheless remains predominantly preclinical, and greater technical complexity should not be taken as evidence of greater clinical value. Translation will depend on reproducible vesicle preparations, reliable MN insertion and release, a clearly defined delivered dose, appropriate comparators, and convincing long-term safety. Accessible lesions may provide the most practical starting points, whereas applications involving internal organs will require more extensive procedural and safety validation. With better standardization, disease-specific design, and clinically relevant evaluation, MN-mediated exosome delivery may develop into a useful group of therapeutic and diagnostic products. Its future is therefore more likely to be shaped by carefully selected indications than by a single platform intended for every disease.
